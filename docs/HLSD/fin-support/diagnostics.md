# Fin support: diagnostics

[Fin support overview](../fin-support.md)

This reference describes observation and reporting. Authoritative [crease selection and classification](painted-shape.md#crease-selection-and-source-consumption) and [physical lifecycle rules](generation.md) are documented with their algorithm owners. A drawn source or an accepted intermediate member is not proof that a printable support was emitted.

## Snapshot ownership

Blue chart output belongs to the intent pipeline: retained paths and the optional area-center point are produced independently of diagnostic collection. Exporters read those results and capture their own drawing snapshots; diagnostic objects never supply the point to downstream intent resolution. `PaintedBlueDiagnostic` is created only when chart diagnostics are enabled. Crease chart projections and their source-face identities are algorithm inputs for selection and pairing, available regardless of diagnostic output.

## Stage summaries and counters

Info-level logs use the `Fin support` prefix. Painted Shape keeps stage and member summaries, per-chart SVG summaries, aggregate counters and geometry failure records. Member summaries and crease-root layer records may include successful results; detailed branch and contact-query traces use debug logging. Simple reports the highest painted, selected and emitted Z values plus its contour-interval counts. Painted Shape timing summaries use one line per major boundary, alongside member and crease diagnostics. It reports connected islands, intent classifications, skeleton cleanup, fitted segments, proposals and geometry outcomes. `point_intents` counts explicit standalone blue points; `line_intents` and `branched_intents` count islands with one or multiple strokes respectively. An island can contribute both points and strokes. `raw_intent_paths` counts medial-axis polylines; `logical_intent_paths` counts paths after graph recovery; `joined_intent_paths` counts approximate local-width fragment joins. `intent_junctions` and `paired_intent_junctions` describe the prepared graph topology, and `pruned_intent_branches` counts branches removed by either the first or second pruning pass; cap trimming and compact-component rejection are counted separately. `blue_intent_groups` counts the 15-degree groups, `connector_blue_intent_groups` counts short groups retained to connect width-qualified groups, `discarded_blue_intent_groups` counts groups removed after both width classification and connector recovery, and `point_blue_intent_charts` counts chart areas with no blue groups after path preparation, including compactness rejection and cap exhaustion, or whose longest group is shorter than its mean width. `compact_intent_components` counts complete skeleton components rejected before local pruning; those edges are not counted as pruned side branches. `trimmed_intent_samples` counts removed brush-cap samples, and `coarsened_intent_samples` counts vertices removed at full painted-width scale. `junction_split_intent_strokes` counts blue spans exposed at graph junctions. `blue_intent_roots` counts source roots in ordinary directed blue graphs; `merging_intent_strokes` counts unselected incoming segments registered for tail attachment. `branch_fit_candidates`, `branch_fit_rejects` and `branch_fit_continuations` count complete common-window comparisons, candidates with E greater than one and selected continuations. `branch_fit_ties` counts outgoing paths whose continuation was canceled by incoming scores within EPSILON of their best E. `branch_parent_stops` counts junctions with outgoing paths but no selected continuation; `branch_connection_misses` counts child rays without a usable intersection or contributors whose mean falls outside the finite main segment. `branch_cycle_cancellations` counts source junctions whose endpoint attachments were canceled because they form a dependency cycle; junctions only downstream of a cycle are not counted. `fit_split_intent_strokes` counts ordinary growth rollbacks that confirm a segment and restart at its tail. `line_plane_alignment_rejects` and `line_plane_missing_macro` count edges that could not define an allowed fixed plane. `macro_plane_targets` and `tangent_plane_targets` count the two selected correction directions; `ambiguous_plane_targets` counts displacement ties resolved as points. `vertical_point_targets` counts ordinary green sources forced to points by the 80-degree surface threshold, including XY-degenerate strokes. `line_to_point_fallbacks` identifies point interpretations of sources carrying a line or graph-edge identity; this classification belongs to intent resolution, not candidate feasibility filtering. `resolved_line_candidates` and `resolved_point_candidates` count the preferred alternatives selected before geometry, and no geometry-stage point fallback is performed. `root_intent_fins` counts realized line generation roots. `composite_intent_fins` counts connected graph components emitted as one fin, `composite_intent_segments` counts lower planes absorbed into them, `continuous_intent_envelopes` counts degree-two line junctions included in the shared body-center network, and `composite_intent_merge_failures` counts invalid junction-axis connections and component assembly failures. For Painted Shape, `single_projection_exposed_layers` and `composite_projection_exposed_layers` count successfully materialized taper sections across attempts, including successful terminal absorption sections. These counters do not test whether a section extends outside the body and do not count unique print layers; `composite_projection_retained_layers` counts materialized support sections still wholly present after member-by-member attachment and common layer clearance, including carried sections inside contact bands. `finite_line_candidates` counts line descriptions accepted before path conversion. `intent_direction_used` counts accepted ordinary lines; `without_contour`, `contact_top_clamped`, `geometry_fallback`, `shifted_to_contact_top`, `direction_adjusted` and `max_center_distance_mm` describe ordinary point-site selection only. The `painted-fin-intents` summary reports resulting finite groups, generation roots, added root stems, topology spans, shared nodes and discarded groups. `resolved_node_heights` counts successful downstream queries, `missing_node_heights` counts nodes without a surface hit or available upstream height, and `unresolved_node_heights` counts nodes still waiting on cyclic dependencies. Unresolved incoming-height and cyclic-height diagnostics identify exceptional working-graph failures. `merged_outline_splits` counts additional contours emitted by merged outlining. `merged_outline_failures` counts merged physical regions whose required first loop could not be generated, not optional inner-loop exhaustion; it does not count rejected fins or rolled-back layers. Painted Shape combines every populated rejection reason for one diagnostic stage into one `rejects=` field; each entry retains its count, first failing print Z and reason-specific value. `lower_contacts_accepted` excludes every root or point seed contact, and `suppressed_seed_projection` counts periodic bands omitted because their top reached the seed contact or its downward projection.

## Height, contact and assembly failures

At info level, `painted-fin-height` records failed downstream height queries with node and island IDs, incoming node IDs, fixed XY, source Z, any available upper bound and the failure reason. Successful correction, classification and height resolution are represented by the aggregate counters rather than per-edge or per-node traces. Path validation reports `too-few-vertices` and `ascending-path` at info level, so a trace rejected before any layers are allocated retains its reason even without debug logging. These diagnostics do not change fitting, correction, classification or height resolution.

For an `empty-neck` rejection, value 1 means cap resolution failed, 2 means the center polygon degenerated, 3 means model or bed clipping removed it, and 4 means a carried trapezoid could not connect to its surviving member body.

A failed Painted Shape downward taper emits `painted-contact-projection` with its member and crease identifiers, band-bottom layer, failing layer, slice Z, print Z, wall presence and surviving body-region count. An unavailable actual contact or failed carried section is reported as `unavailable-contact-or-continuation`; a fixed-plane-clipped section that cannot join its body is reported as `contact-boundary`. These failures emit `skip=<reason>` and omit only that layer's taper geometry. Lower layers continue independently after these failures unless the failing layer was already marked terminal by the contact-axis absorption test; successful layers remain available. Entry of the fixed-plane/contact-axis intersection point into the original member's physical body envelope selects the terminal layer, counted by single_projection_absorbed or composite_projection_absorbed. That layer still attempts completion: success contributes one taper layer, while a failed join logs contact-boundary and omits its geometry. Neither outcome resumes below. Skipped layers stay inactive in the component record; neither a member rejection nor a point replacement is triggered by a taper-layer failure. Actual contact-band validation and final body checks remain separate.

The final Painted Shape line stores the first graph-attachment and component-assembly failure in `stage_failure_samples`, with the aggregate counts beside it. Detailed records for every failure remain available at debug level. `composite_intent_merge_failures` includes rejected axis connections and component assembly failures. Invalid axis connections do not join components; remaining paths must reach an already valid root before they can proceed; failure does not create a replacement seed or root. Component assembly failures reject that component before it enters the common clearance pass. `painted-shape-layer-clearance` summarizes that pass. A previously nonempty common layer that is completely removed records `empty-center-after-gap`, its slice/print heights; only that layer is skipped. No post-clearance whole-fin rejection or per-fin overlap statistic is computed.

`single_projection_layers` and `composite_projection_layers` count materialized transition sections across attempts; they are not counts of unique print layers. The corresponding `*_max_layers` value is the largest count in one attempt, and `*_zero_layers` counts attempts with no materialized transition. A completed Painted Shape pass evaluates down to the bed, even if earlier sections were removed by C, so `bed` records completion of the pass rather than proof of nonempty bed geometry.

## Island SVG and skeleton stages

At info log level or higher verbosity, Painted Shape writes one `SVG/fin-support/painted-shape-island-<run>-<island>.svg` per source painted island. Owned drawing snapshots from all its charts and crease stages are rendered together after fin-intent resolution. Each chart has equal-scale panels for raw skeleton, compact-component filtering, first-pass pruning, cap trimming, second-pass pruning, prepared orange paths, coarsening and final dark blue. Panels are numbered in processing order and fit the chart to a compact display scale. Only the surviving geometry and painted outline are drawn at each stage; removed raw edges do not obscure later stages. Cyan identifies the protected axis in the first-pass panel. The stage geometry shows where branches disappear, while the chart classification summary records the final line/point result, its reason, cap-discarded branch count, retained path count and longest blue-group L/W. The island overview adds ordinary green/source geometry in world XY and resolved fin intents in XY/XZ, including roots and point outputs. For charts with incident persistent crease segments, a chart-probe row preserves the snapshot after blue filtering and before surface-Z orientation or green solving. Ordinary charts omit these four crease panels but retain their eight skeleton-stage panels and chart classification summary. The chart number is not an island index; `patch_seed` and `source_chart` identify the projected source area. Persistent crease IDs remain shared across patch views within the run. Four panels show source geometry, direction passes, rejected/unavailable probes, and selected points reconnected in crease order, all in the current surface chart enlarged fourfold for readability. Purple marks convex crease segments, gray marks non-convex segments, blue marks retained blue paths and points, cyan marks the sampled crease window, and orange marks the normalized average direction with its endpoint. Green/red in the middle panels indicate direction results, not final selection. In the fourth panel green marks selected points and their new connections; red marks discarded points. P labels identify retained path/point indices; R labels identify crease/segment indices, and J marks a source junction. A crease chart with an empty blue result still produces a view. Point-only crease comparisons reserve only a status line instead of an empty distance plot. Unreliable normals, empty projection or excessive projection overlap instead emit a `painted-crease-probes` skip reason before returning.

The first crease-probe panel overlays orange prepared paths with the final retained blue paths. The orange snapshot follows first-pass pruning, cap trimming, second-pass pruning and connectivity recovery; it preserves their mapped source samples before coarsening or blue-group filtering. Orange paths are drawn wider underneath blue so coincident portions remain distinguishable, and O labels identify these input paths independently of final P labels. Orange geometry remains visible when the blue result is a point. A black dot labeled POINT marks the standalone blue output at its chart-area centroid. The panel reports POINT or LINE for this chart's blue classification, with `compact-components`, `cap-trim-no-line`, `second-pass-no-line`, `preparation-no-line`, `longest-below-width` or `retained-groups` as the reason. `compact-components` means every extracted skeleton component failed the preliminary compactness classifier. Orange paths are downstream of this classifier, so rejected compact components have no orange path. When groups exist, L and W identify the longest group's chart length and common width threshold. This classification does not guarantee downstream fin validity. `painted-blue-classification` info records and SVG comments carry the same decision, group and path counts. Point results also report whether a point was emitted, its chart coordinates, mapped surface XYZ, source face and represented area; failure to map a valid source face leaves the point un-emitted. Classification records distinguish cap exhaustion from second-pass exhaustion and count fully cap-discarded branches independently of orange paths. This overlay does not include green fitting or change geometry generation.

The first panel of the chart-probe row overlays the raw medial axis in pink, before compact-component filtering and branch pruning. Orange and blue retain their prepared-skeleton and retained-path meanings. The skeleton-stage panels distinguish the raw graph from geometry retained after compact filtering, the first pass, cap trimming and the second pass.

## Crease views and records

The fourth probe SVG panel shows accepted recheck chords in magenta and rejected chords in red, with `RECHECK` identifiers, both endpoint angles, cyan sampling windows and orange average directions. Original direction panels retain their original results. `painted-crease-junction-recheck` debug records and SVG comments identify the source junction, both endpoints, local widths, recomputed probes and acceptance; the info summary includes `junction_rechecks` and `accepted_rechecks`. Selected-point records distinguish `junction-bypassed` and `junction-recheck-selected`.

Crease angle-routing diagnostics record each source chart's macro normal and near-vertical result. `ordinary-side` identifies the selected nonvertical chain; `ordinary-side-no-line` identifies a selected chain with fewer than two samples. Green paths labeled `ORDINARY` show the direct side output instead of a fused crease intent. `full_expansion` identifies crease outputs using the unattenuated factor. `painted-crease-ordinary-side` records the prepared ordinary input and its independent generation identity; the island resolved-intent views retain the selected source chain alongside the generated path. A source-chain drawing does not establish successful physical realization.

A crease-section row in the same island SVG shows the original 3D crease and selected surface chains in XY, XZ and YZ, followed by distance plots for line candidates. The views use the original centered-object surface coordinates and the same display scale. Gray marks the original crease, chart colors identify the selected blue chains, yellow marks common untrimmed painted coverage, pink marks the fused cap-trimmed candidate, green marks accepted line intent paths, and magenta dots mark point intents. `I<pair>.<intent>` labels identify independent outputs; red section labels indicate failed measurements or symmetry checks. `C<chart>:P<path>.<point>` labels link back to the chart-probe row; `M<pair>.<sample>` identifies the same section in all views and its distance plot. The fourth panel of the chart-probe row also overlays yellow untrimmed crease spans and pink single-side cap-trimmed spans. A crease without an eligible opposite-patch pair still produces an overview with a no-pair explanation.

`painted-crease-cap` records retain each side's raw/trimmed arc positions, endpoint widths and the P identifiers that supplied them. `painted-crease-candidate` records distinguish `line-candidate`, `blue-point`, `cap-trim-exhausted`, `no-selected-point`, `ordinary-side` and `ordinary-side-no-line`. Records include paired source charts and spans, crease candidate positions when applicable, the selected ordinary chart for a side output, and the `full_expansion` flag. The chart-probe and crease-section rows retain the relevant details in comments as well as debug logs. `painted-crease-sections` info records include chart/pair/section counts, line/point/unavailable candidate counts, `ordinary_candidates` for pairs routed to the ordinary side (including those without a selected line), unique measurement pairs, accepted/rejected section counts, output line/point intent counts, missing sides, ambiguous sides and the SVG path. `painted-crease-intent` and `painted-crease-intent-vertex` records identify each independent output, its supporting section range and its original-crease 3D coordinates. Section records include acceptance and, for two unique hits, the measured difference and its 100%-of-mean limit. Detailed `painted-crease-section`, `painted-crease-section-hit`, `painted-crease-section-coplanar`, `painted-crease-section-chain` and `painted-crease-section-point` records retain source identities, coordinates and measurements.

After fin-intent resolution, a resolved-crease row in the same island SVG compares each accepted source part with the resolved input in equal-scale XY, XZ and YZ views. Gray is the accepted original crease geometry, green the unchanged input path before gap clipping, orange the added root-stem prefix, and magenta the direct point input. A rejected part still produces a source-only view labeled `NO RESOLVED INTENT`. These views precede per-layer clipping and physical contact/body generation and do not assert that a printable fin succeeded. `painted-crease-resolved` identifies the file and resolved group count; `painted-crease-resolved-vertex` records input and corresponding unchanged source coordinates in logs and SVG comments. XY labels `S#` identify zero-based source vertices; source `S0` corresponds to path vertex 1 after the synthetic stem. `painted-crease-root` records the root 3D normal query, its contributing face weights, horizontal-section edges and exterior bisector, and validity. Vertex weights are corner angles in radians; edge and face weights are one. Query face/vertex IDs belong to the complete-model mesh, not the painted subdivision mesh. Section Z uses slicer float precision. These diagnostics describe root preparation only; there are no per-vertex offset directions or displacement comparison rays. `painted-crease-direct` identifies independent generation ownership and source root Z. `painted-crease-consumption` reports consumed sample counts, remaining ordinary paths, discarded isolated residual samples and whether a chart still supplies an ordinary point. All crease selection, comparison, consumption and input construction run independently of logging level; SVG rendering is enabled at the existing diagnostic logging level. `painted-crease-layer-contact` records identify the source crease and layer when tracing cannot supply its anchor or normal: `no-section-front-normal` records a missing or ambiguous horizontal direction with section-query details. `no-unique-intent-intersection` identifies a crease-source query without a unique point at the layer height. The original 3D source determines availability; no nearby-corner substitute is selected. Resolved wall hits and axes are recorded by `fin-wall-contact` and `crease-root-layer`. Crease paths retain their crease/pair/part identity in member diagnostics; stored member grouping defines the highest-band failure withdrawal range, including any interior junctions in that member. `painted-shape-member` info records attribute trace and geometry acceptance, retained path/wall layer counts and existing rejection summaries to each island/edge and member kind; crease members additionally carry crease/pair/part IDs. Successful geometry can still report rejected periodic bands, and member acceptance does not imply successful component assembly or merged toolpath output.

Crease contact diagnostics distinguish accepted candidates from emitted toolpaths: `emitted_top_z` is the highest print Z with actual support extrusion entities. `crease-root-band` reports source top Z, candidate slice/print heights, configured contact height, and planned/retained half-open layer ranges. `crease-root-layer` records each planned root layer's path availability, wall hit, accepted contact, source point, physical hit, resolved contact axis, front normal and sideways displacement relative to the source bisector.

## Rejection values

`contact-turn` values are angles in degrees. `contact-span` values identify section 0 (middle), 1 (bottom), or 2 (top). `projection-intrusion` is the inner-edge increase in mm, compared with `w/2`; depth and contour values are mm, while `wall-facing` is a dot product. For Simple/Simulated legacy finalization, `topology` uses a bit mask: 1 means multiple or missing physical areas, 2 physical holes, 4 multiple or missing center regions, 8 center holes, and 16 an invalid center contour. `no-direction` records requested D in degrees and uses Z=-1 because no layer was selected. `contact-contour` value -1 means no intersection in the sampled span; source-paint checks use the model contour and actual contact checks use printed-wall coverage. Nonnegative values report displacement in mm. `top-wall` distance zero may mean no intersection rather than a valid zero-distance hit. Painted Shape reports assembly failures before common clearance and output failures per merged layer region; it does not perform a separate post-clearance stack-connectivity rejection. Initial exits also log insufficient layers/raft or an empty footprint; the Simulated planner additionally reports negligible base area or peak risk at most 0.8. GUI logging defaults to info; a higher severity suppresses these diagnostics.

## Simulated and direct-paint diagnostics

Each Simulated directional attempt logs requested D, resolved A when available, and its own rejection summary before the object-wide aggregate. `seed-ray-miss` counts eligible layers with painted cuts but no forward intersection on the fixed A ray; its value is the cut count. `seed-low-risk` counts painted layers excluded by the 0.8 risk threshold. These layer counts explain a small number of actual seed-validation attempts despite a large painted region.

A failed direct-paint lookup receives a second diagnostic-only scan, using the same facing, parallel and endpoint tolerances. `paint-empty` means the requested section has no authorized painted cuts. `paint-facing` means none face the lookup ray. `paint-parallel` means every facing cut is parallel to that ray. `paint-distance` reports the nearest in-segment ray intersection in mm, to compare with support line width. `paint-endpoint` reports the minimum endpoint overrun in mm when intersections lie outside the painted segments. These details apply both to middle-section lookup and full-span validation; the corresponding `contact-span` section index identifies vertical sampling failures. This second scan does not run for successful lookups and does not relax any condition.
