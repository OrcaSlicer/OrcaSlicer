# Fin support: Simulated

[Fin support overview](../fin-support.md)

Simulated is the default style. It plans support against the printed prefix of the model and owns the feedback loop between tentative plans and validated physical results.

## Shape analysis and directional demand

For the Simulated planner, the first object layer supplies base area `A0`, centroid `C0`, and an XY covariance, subtracting holes. Its outer contours form a convex-hull footprint; each hull edge supplies an outward direction and the original centroid margin to that tipping boundary. For every subsequent layer, slice area and first moments update the centroid `C(h)` of only the material printed so far. The geometric risk is `h / sqrt(A0) * (1 + norm(C(h) - C0) / sqrt(A0))`. Risk at or below 0.8 does not start a fin. Above this threshold, each footprint direction receives demand proportional to `(1 - 0.8 / risk) * original_margin / current_margin`; current margin is clamped at half a support line width to keep the heuristic finite when a centroid crosses the footprint. The current section-centroid offset and covariance growth add signed directional demands. Covariance is measured about the section centroid relative to the first-layer covariance, so translating an unchanged section does not masquerade as expansion. Its directional variance uses a support-bead-sized floor, and positive spread growth is compressed logarithmically so a nearly linear first-layer footprint cannot create unbounded demand. Nearby directions merge within 15 degrees to avoid repeated equivalent requests. The footprint, moments, and directions are model geometry; painting does not move the centroid or change these raw demands. The method is a geometric stabilizing heuristic, not a force or adhesion calculation.

## Upward plans and candidate selection

Planning sweeps upward through these printed-prefix states. An active plan has a fixed sheet direction A and fixed sheet plane. Its seed follows the intersection of that plane with the next layer's painted wall; a missing or invalid intersection, an excessive between-layer jump, or an inward retreat that would erase the lower sheet projection closes the plan at its previous valid height. Candidate plans whose tops converge in the same direction stop duplicating future demand, while retaining their separate lower histories. A stopped plan remains in every later layer's support calculation. Planning capacity is proportional to `(seed_top_height / current_height)^2 * thickness / 1.2`, reduced by a nominal contact-neck-distance factor. For every layer and direction u, remaining demand is recomputed from that layer's raw demand minus `capacity * max(0, A dot u)^2` for each existing plan. Support therefore reduces risk without moving the printed material's center of mass. The opposing direction receives no capacity from that plan.

Whenever a layer has remaining demand above 15% of its raw demand, the planner may add another seed on that same layer and immediately recomputes the residual before considering a further seed. It chooses the greatest unmet direction D. For each painted run, A is the direction closest to D that stays within 30 degrees of the run's XY normal; runs more than 60 degrees from D are unavailable. The run supplies a direct A-ray intersection and central/quarter alternatives, all sharing that A. These are geometric sites, not angular bins. Sites are ranked first by alignment with D, then by position: a short curved run favors the D-ray from the printed centroid, while a broad flat face favors the center of its whole sliced contour rather than the center of its painted subset. The selected point must still lie on the painted run. Existing fins facing the same run impose a preferred lateral separation limited by half that run's painted span. The complete printed-layer patch must be painted, unblocked, and have a local averaged normal within 30 degrees of A. The seed's source XY is evaluated at this layer's `slice_z`; `print_z` identifies its printable output height. The final averaged contact normal validates A but does not rotate it. At most four plans begin on one layer, eight plans may remain active, and 32 may be retained in total; a blocked direction does not prevent another direction from being considered.

## Realization and replanning

The upward sweep stores seed and contact feasibility, without rebuilding polygons or toolpaths on every layer. Once all plans close, the existing downward tracing, band validation, contact projection, body, foot, and overlap checks generate final geometry. When a plan's final top fails geometry or collides with another sheet, earlier valid tops from its upward history are checked. A printable earlier top found by an exponentially bracketed geometry check limits that plan on the next upward sweep, so lower contact history can remain while upper residual demand is solved again. If no earlier top is printable, the plan's original seed is excluded. All failed plans in a pass are collected before another upward sweep; one failed fin no longer restarts the entire sweep immediately. A taller printable fin can supersede a nearby lower fin of the same direction when it has a separate accepted contact band no later than each lower band and within one band period, and its bed reach is no shorter. An outline failure excludes its original seed before replanning. The emitted set contains only plans with validated printable geometry. Contact bands still use their own local averaged normals and remain all-or-none. Their height is not searched or adjusted in this version. All selected geometry is completed before any extrusion paths are emitted.

The planner also compares accepted contact bands with planned bands after realization. Reduced realized coverage lowers the planning capacity through `capacity_overrides` and may trigger another upward sweep. `top_limits` retain an earlier printable top; `rejected` excludes failed original seeds. These are planner-owned state, not diagnostic counters or instructions for the generator to replan.

## Data and code boundaries

`FinShapeAnalysis` supplies shape-derived demand. Each `FinPlan` retains the initial seed, current top, active state and earlier candidate tops. `select_simulated_fin_candidate()` creates candidates and `extend_fin_plan()` advances an active plan. The Simulated branch of `generate_fin_support()` owns planning passes, realization calls, placement checks and retries.

`realize_fin_candidate()` returns generated geometry through `TracedFin`; the current planner reads contact counts and placement outcomes from that result. The loop is therefore not confined to computation before physical generation. Its retry policy remains specific to Simulated.
