# Fin support: physical generation

[Fin support overview](../fin-support.md)

Generation consumes the prepared candidate geometry and contact policy. The rules below distinguish Painted Shape finite/point realization from Simple and Simulated projected fins. Common entry points do not imply identical acceptance or finalization rules.

The [intent input](intent-input.md) feeds correction and preparation before this boundary. `generate_candidate_fin_support()` receives an explicit geometry mode from its caller; it does not choose the physical route from the configured producer style. Current Simple and Painted Shape calls retain their respective existing routes.

| Boundary | Input | Output and ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracing | Prepared candidate, model sections, bed and clearance | Member-local layer paths and source anchors |
| Contact/body construction | Traced member, wall coverage and contact policy | Completed member contours, actual contacts and transitions |
| Component assembly | Completed members and resolved shared junctions | Joined contributions preserving generation-member provenance |
| Finalization and output | Per-fin geometry for Simple/Simulated; common layer centers for Painted Shape | Closed support paths and ordinary `SupportLayer` storage |

[`FinSupport.cpp`](../../../src/libslic3r/Support/FinSupport.cpp) implements these boundaries in `trace_fin()`, `generate_fin_geometry()`, `generate_candidate_fin_support()` and `emit_fin_support()`. See [Painted Shape generation units](painted-shape.md#generation-units-and-connectivity) for member activation and failure scope, and [Simulated feedback](simulated.md#realization-and-replanning) for planner-owned retries.

## Painted Shape data ownership

Prepared point seeds and ordered finite paths enter generation through `FinCandidateProposal`. Generation reads that input and owns its working copy in `TracedFin`; the original intent and correction/preparation workspaces are not modified. The candidate and tracing types still serve multiple styles. Ordinary/crease contact and point/path body selection keep their existing independent rules.

| Data | Owner and lifetime | Consumers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layer paths, contact anchors, wall hits, body axes and attachment caps | Member tracing/construction workspace in `TracedFin` | Contact construction and root-reachability resolution |
| Normalized body query geometry | Local to `generate_fin_geometry()` | Exposure and taper-absorption queries; never component output |
| `PaintedFinExtensionWork` | Local to member construction; each layer owns its section, completed contour and section kind together | Band continuation and taper construction |
| `PaintedFinMemberGeometry` | Published only after all member extensions complete; contains completed layer contours and contact/transition sections | Component assembly reads this result |
| `PaintedFinAssemblyMember` | Assembly-owned copy, paired with the source member index | Reachability removes layers from this copy without altering the published member geometry |
| `PaintedFinAssemblyLayer` | Layer assembly workspace | Common layer union, clearance exemptions and projection-retention accounting |
| `PaintedFinOutput` | Completed layer geometry plus separate source contours | Output reads clipped layer centers as geometry and source contours only for seam orientation |

A missing member result means construction has not completed successfully. No partial extension state crosses the publication boundary. `assemble_painted_fin_layer()` accepts completed assembly members and shared joins without access to producer graphs or tracing state; contact and transition sections retain their distinct semantics through the clearance pass.

`TracedFin::centers` holds finalized per-fin geometry only for Simple and Simulated. Painted Shape stores attribution in `PaintedFinOutput::sources`, in retained-member order. Those pre-clearance contours never reconstruct or replace the common clipped output. Diagnostic counters remain in the generation workspace and do not determine geometry publication.

## A1 and contact signals

`TracedFin::body_endpoints` stores A1 coordinates, endpoint origin and `FinContactAction` together. Origin records a model-gap intersection, finite endpoint, inherited intersection or projected endpoint; it is independent of permission to create contact. Generation completes A1 inheritance and assigns the action before cap construction. Cap-only layers retain the existing wall-resolved direction; resolving that direction does not accept a contact or create a clearance exemption.

| Action | Meaning and consumer |
| --- | --- |
| `query` | The contact resolver may attempt a new contact under the band's ordinary/crease rule |
| `continue_only` | No new query; an already active band or taper may use its saved front |
| `stop_band` | End the band's retained range above this layer and enter the existing downward taper; the signal itself does not change taper geometry or query eligibility |

Inherited point endpoints use `continue_only`. A missing finite path uses `stop_band`; a missing finite source uses `continue_only` when there is a downstream line, otherwise `stop_band`. Body and finite-source availability are still checked separately, including the finite-path stop during taper. The resolver checks `continue_only` before its geometry cache; `stop_band` does not itself prohibit the taper's normal wall query. A1 clipping determines the retained centerline independently of this band transition. A failed permitted query retains the existing top-failure/lower-continuation behavior and does not rewrite the action. The existing band workspace owns the saved front, and the existing extension work owns completion state; no separate controller owns a second lifecycle. The fixed taper plane, shared attachment geometry and publication boundary are unchanged.

## Upward Z gap

Painted Shape uses the existing **Top Z distance** setting through `SlicingParameters::gap_support_object`, including its normal layer-height rounding and zero-gap interpretation. The setting is editable for Painted Shape; Simple and Simulated retain their existing behavior. For a fin layer with physical top `print_z`, the upper-model mask unions later object layers whose physical bottom `print_z - height` lies below the fin top plus this gap, using the support height-comparison tolerance. An exactly sufficient gap is retained. The query uses all later object layers, including layers above the fin's seed, and works with variable layer heights; it does not use `slice_z` or a fixed layer count. Zero gap produces no upper-model mask.

The mask unions nominal model sections (`lslices`), then offsets the union by the existing XY model gap (`support_object_xy_distance`) with the same round joins as current-layer clearance. Source sections remain unchanged; no bridge-extrusion envelope is added. The mask is cached per layer and applied to centerlines through the existing start-preserving `clipped_fin_path()`. A finite path receives this cut after its XY-gap cut and before root reachability is resolved. A point segment is cut from its outer tip toward A1 after endpoint inheritance. Inheritance retains the last successful XY-gap intersection before the Z cut, and the inherited segment receives its own layer's Z check. Root depth extensions also receive the same centerline cut before side-chain construction.

A shortened or removed main centerline carries `stop_band`; later contact-policy assignment preserves that signal. A removed prefix leaves no body on that layer and cannot be replaced by a disconnected fragment. The existing band-top failure and finite-component lifetime rules still apply. Contact axes and caps use the retained A1. Inherited endpoints remain ineligible for new contact even when their action is `stop_band`. The signal only ends the contact band and starts the existing downward taper. This centerline operation does not add a final Z mask, change taper clipping, or change the XY contact/transition exemptions.

## Painted Shape geometry and lifecycle contract

The contact rule is selected for each band, separately from its point/line body input. Ordinary/crease contact crossed with point/line body input produces four geometry combinations; all four share contact geometry, side-chain construction and contact-band failure rules. Finite paths additionally retain their group-based clipping, root reachability and component lifetime.

1. **Contact rule:** Ordinary contact selects the nearest point on this model's section at the current layer, with no source-contour, surface-owner or proximity restriction. Its turn is clipped to the inclusive range of -30 to +30 degrees relative to the local body direction; an out-of-range nearest direction is clipped rather than rejected. The ordinary front is the straight tangent approximation at the configured contact length. Failure of the half-sample contact rule rejects the attempt; the front is not shortened to fit. Crease contact resolves Q from the original 3D crease source at `slice_z`, then queries physical wall coverage along the negative horizontal-section exterior bisector; it has no turn limit. Its front is perpendicular to that bisector. Crease lines use this rule for every band and taper; crease points use it only for the highest band and its taper. Lower periodic point-fin bands and their tapers use ordinary contact. Ordinary and crease retain their respective front-direction construction, and actual contacts lie on the physical outer surface of generated wall extrusions.
2. **Body input:** A point fin supplies a ray. A line fin supplies its existing finite fin-intent polyline. Prepared finite groups define start-preserving clipping and member failure scope; ordinary roots use separate stem groups, while a crease stem and its source path remain one group.
3. **Contact geometry:** One shared trapezoid construction connects the contact rule's front segment to the two actual attachment endpoints supplied by the body. A zero-length front produces a triangle. Both endpoints are shared with body assembly rather than reconstructed independently.
4. **Contact-band rules:** Every band is processed from its scheduled top downward. Failure to construct the top layer invalidates that band. Every contact failure below the top invokes continuation, including wall lookup, turn, span, blocker and geometry failures. Continuation adds the last successful actual contact's original axis perpendicular to the shared side-chain cuts described below; continued attachments do not update this limit. Finite bands retain the existing path/source-presence and downstream-member rules: a missing retained path clips the band; a missing source contact clips it only when no downstream line is available, otherwise continuation can retain its scheduled bottom. Taper begins below the retained band bottom. A taper-layer failure emits no taper geometry on that layer and continues below unless the contact-axis intersection has already marked that layer as terminal. Failure of a highest band rejects its entire generating member; surviving members are checked again for root reachability.
5. **Body geometry:** Both inputs produce two continuous side chains. Inner bends use line intersections and outer bends use circular arcs. Root outer ends are flat. Cross-member junctions receive only the missing circular sectors when at least two incident contributions meet; a lone surviving endpoint remains flat. An internal bend already owned by a member receives no duplicate junction sector. Contact ends are clipped by both model gap and the contact's rear cap before the body hands over its actual attachment endpoints.
6. **Body rules:** Each layer clips the complete centerline input afresh. A retained finite fragment must contain its entire generation group's original start and connect to a valid root. An interior junction is not a replacement start. Contact search cannot extend beyond a finite endpoint. Geometry failure cannot convert a line to a point or create a root.
7. **Lifecycle:** A band progresses from contact or continuation to taper, then finishes. A later successful query cannot reopen that band after taper begins. A failed taper layer is skipped without ending the taper unless it is already the terminal absorption layer. For a nonempty, plane-and-bed-clipped taper section, intersect the fixed plane with the selected contact axis at the current layer. When this single intersection point lies inside or on the boundary of the generating member's original physical body envelope, including extrusion width, that layer becomes the final layer of the band's taper. Absorption tests this point, not the side intersections or containment of the entire section, and is checked before contact-specific boundary completion. The terminal layer still attempts normal boundary completion and publishes its taper if successful; only lower layers receive no geometry or additional saved-cap cuts from this taper. A failed terminal-layer join also stops evaluation below. Other bands remain independent. Finite-member rejection and group-start retention follow the prepared grouping. Root reachability uses the existing component-wide termination state: once no root-connected body survives after root activation, that component does not reappear below. There is no additional per-junction segment withdrawal or per-segment permanent-stop policy.

## Printed-wall contact boundary

**Printed-wall contact boundary:** All styles, point/line inputs and ordinary/crease contacts resolve their actual contact with the same wall-coverage query. Ordinary contacts first determine their source axis, then query from A1 towards the source. Bands using crease contact, on either point or line bodies, instead query from their source intersection Q along its inward horizontal normal -n; after finding H, they resolve the contact axis from H toward A1 before building the body and its shared attachment. Both queries intersect the physical coverage of the current layer's generated perimeter extrusions. Each segment is a round-ended capsule with radius half its actual `ExtrusionPath::width`, including widths produced by Arachne. Nested perimeter collections, loops and multipaths are traversed, including thin-wall/gap-fill paths held in those collections; force-no-extrusion paths are excluded. Infill is not a contact target. A per-run, lazy per-layer AABB index limits capsule tests to the query axis. For an origin outside wall coverage, the smallest forward capsule entry is the first boundary of the coverage union, without constructing or Boolean-unioning full-layer coverage polygons. The capsule intersection primitive is shared with infill through `Geometry`.

Painted Shape uses one bidirectional wall-boundary query for ordinary and crease contacts, independently of point/line body input. The contact rule supplies the origin and inward direction; intersections along that axis retain signed distances. An origin outside coverage extends inward to the first covered interval. An origin inside coverage clips back in the outward direction to the outer boundary of the covered interval containing it. Overlapping capsule intervals are treated as their coverage union, so an individual capsule boundary hidden inside another bead is not a contact surface. A zero-distance hit is valid only when the origin is already on the applicable surface. Both cases resolve the same outward-facing boundary using extension or clipping; they do not select whichever of the two directions gives the nearest arbitrary wall. This query changes the physical contact location, not source eligibility, finite-path endpoint limits or the body's prescribed centerline.

The resolved hit H is on the physical outer surface of the generated wall coverage. An origin inside the coverage is not itself an outer-surface hit merely because it is covered. If a valid surface hit H coincides with A1, the existing axis direction is retained rather than normalizing a zero vector. No hit means no actual contact, with no nominal-contour fallback. Source-model anchors remain separate for paint eligibility, surface queries and axis provenance; physical hits do not rewrite source heights, normals or paint measurements. Contact fronts retain the half-support-bead placement along the contact rule's front normal. Ordinary fronts use the straight local tangent approximation; crease fronts are perpendicular to the source intersection's horizontal-section exterior bisector. The front normal is separate from the angle-clipped query axis and the body cap normal. Actual contact bridges are not recut against the nominal model outline, which may extend beyond the printed corner. Body gap clipping, lower-transition clearance and contact continuation retain their separate rules. Coverage is the planar swept-width model of the generated wall paths, not a prediction of molten extrusion deformation.

Source-model or crease anchors remain separate from printed-wall hits. A missing physical hit is a contact-construction failure, not a path-end event. At a band top it invalidates the band; below the top it invokes the same saved-front continuation as every other contact failure. Source presence does not restrict eligibility for continuation.

**Separate front construction:** Ordinary and crease contacts have independent front-solving entry points. The crease entry constructs its perpendicular straight segment and finishes without ordinary span validation. The ordinary entry retains the straight tangent approximation at one physical wall hit, followed by lateral sampling with the half-sample acceptance rule below. The span samples only accept or reject that approximation and do not construct its endpoints. A shared straight-segment coordinate helper does not select either contact policy. Body attachment, contact-band lifecycle and taper clipping consume the selected front afterward. This separation applies by band contact type: a crease point's periodic bands and their tapers use the ordinary entry.

**Physical coverage criterion:** Ordinary Painted Shape contacts retain the existing sampling positions across the lateral span `c + w`, where `c` is configured contact length and `w` is support line width. There are `N = max(2, ceil((c + w) / w)) + 1` sample points, including both endpoints. A point counts as contacting only when its physical-wall query returns a hit whose displacement from the expected sample position is at most `w`. The attempt passes when at least `round(N / 2)` points contact, with .5 rounded upward: 3 points require 2 contacts, 4 require 2, and 5 require 3. Contacting samples need not be consecutive. Missing or displaced samples count as noncontacts and do not individually reject the attempt. The sample locations and straight front endpoints remain unchanged; this count criterion does not claim continuous surface coverage or an exact physical touching length. It applies to every ordinary Painted Shape band and taper, including a crease point's lower periodic bands. Crease fronts do not perform this sampling. Simple and Simulated retain their all-samples requirement. The island-area gate remains a separate intent decision.

## Body paths, directions and source anchors

Body direction A, finite contact axis N and the actual surface normal remain separate quantities. Simple, Simulated and Painted Shape point intents use a fixed sheet plane through the seed and A. Painted Shape finite paths supply the retained local segment and its A1 directly. Ordinary and crease point/line fins use their resolved contact axis N as the cap normal. Their physical cap passes through A1 perpendicular to N and intersects the two symmetric body side lines; no preliminary flat end truncates those line extensions. In printable-center space, the cap line is shifted from A1 by half an extrusion width along its cap normal. The two continuous symmetric side chains, separated locally by `fin thickness - extrusion width`, are clipped by both that cap line and model clearance. The actual surviving endpoints are stored once as shared integer XY coordinates; a clearance-limited endpoint need not lie on the cap line. The open body perimeter and contact outer arc use those endpoints directly; no closed body or half-plane mask is used to locate the attachment. Both point and line inputs obtain their contact axes from the selected ordinary or crease contact rule at the current layer. Their actual contact points come from printed-wall intersection. For each newly resolved contact, body and contact construction consume that layer's stored hit and axis without repeating the same query. Taper resolves its own lower-layer contacts under the contact rule and retains the band's fixed clipping plane. For ordinary contacts, N is the outward, angle-clipped query axis; for crease contacts, N points from H toward A1. A valid coincident hit retains its resolved direction. Zero-gap and ray-generated point handling retain their existing rules. Highest-band failure rejects the complete generating member; remaining members are checked again for an available root under group-start retention. A periodic band whose top fails is omitted. Neither event changes point/line classification. Member-local construction and contact-band validation precede assembly; completed Painted Shape layers do not undergo a separate whole-fin topology or stack-connectivity rejection.

Contour lookup along body direction A locates each layer's source-model anchor for Simple and Simulated; actual contact is subsequently resolved on printed wall coverage. Painted Shape point and line inputs use the shared ordinary or crease contact rule. Simple and Simulated retain their painted-contact rule. Painted Shape points resolve A1, N and the actual local wall normal without requiring further paint; they have no finite line endpoint inherited from a discarded green line. Their lower inner edges follow the round model-clearance boundary while outer endpoints expand with their retained slope. Each layer first resolves the point body's ray against that layer's clearance. Point bodies are then constructed from top to bottom. When a layer has no resolved endpoint, it inherits only the nearest upper layer's successfully resolved model-gap intersection. The saved value updates when the intersection is available, independently of whether that layer produces printable body geometry. The inherited segment is checked against the current layer's upper-model Z-gap mask before entering the common capping pipeline; it does not repeat the current-layer XY center-path cut. Its outer endpoint still uses the current height and expansion slope. The inherited endpoint behaves as a natural body end: cap direction starts from the sheet direction, and the ordinary side-chain, cap, model-clearance and bed-clipping operations run again at the current layer width. Neither an upper cap, contact axis, attachment nor completed boundary is copied. An inherited endpoint cannot authorize a new contact beyond that end, including an ordinary periodic contact on a crease point; existing contact-band continuation and taper retain their own rules. An empty inherited body layer remains absent and does not change the saved intersection. A later layer with a resolved endpoint uses its own endpoint again. With no resolved upper intersection there is no endpoint to inherit; highest-band failure still rejects the member. This per-member state belongs to generation and never changes the published point intent. Painted Shape line intents instead use the prescribed finite groups, per-layer path clipping in this section and final clearance in [component assembly](#member-completion-and-component-assembly). `fin_support_depth` controls the initial outward depth. Periodic contact-band top heights are anchored to the build plate and shared by every fin: `bed_z + fin_support_contact_start_height + n * fin_support_contact_spacing`. `fin_support_contact_height` extends each band downward from that top. The default start height is zero, so the first nominal band is below the printable volume and is naturally omitted. Simple and Simulated require a real painted wall across every layer of a band. A new Painted Shape contact is checked against printed-wall coverage, blockers and its contact rule; any failed attempt below the band top invokes common continuation. Ordinary contacts retain their clipped query axis; crease contacts resolve A1-H. The contact-front direction is supplied separately by the contact rule. The seed has a separate highest contact band ending at its own top layer. Painted Shape rejects the generating member on failure and rechecks the remaining finite graph for root reachability; Simple and Simulated reject the candidate. A periodic band whose top section reaches the seed contact or its downward projection is omitted. The completed body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before the plan is emitted. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

**Appearance near rounded gap corners:** As a point-fin ray approaches tangency to a rounded model-gap corner across successive layers, small contour changes can move the selected exit substantially along the ray. The resulting inner boundary and taper can look stretched or torn even when the intersections and subsequent clipping are valid. Intersection positions are not smoothed between layers; an inherited endpoint also preserves the last resolved intersection rather than a visually averaged position. This appearance alone does not establish that body layers or contacts were lost.

A prescribed line supplies its finite centerline directly. Every layer starts from its full activated finite polyline, subtracts that layer's model offset by XY gap, then clips its retained prefix against the upper-model Z-gap mask; it does not progressively expose segments according to their vertex heights. A retained fragment must contain the whole generation group's original start and connect to a surviving root. An interior junction is not a replacement start; another valid root cannot rescue a fragment cut away from its own group start. Connections are usable only where the actual retained polylines reach their shared junction, checked against constituent segments rather than the head-to-tail chord. Root extensions receive the same start-preserving clip. Reachability is recomputed after member failures using surviving members and roots. Within a connected finite component, once no root-connected body survives after root activation, the shared termination state suppresses all lower layers of that component. Before that termination, layers are independently clipped from the untrimmed group input. Highest-band failure withdraws the whole generating member, not only its prefix up to an interior junction.

For prescribed paths, tracing retains the terminal segment direction and supplies A1 to the ordinary or crease contact resolver before analytical cap construction. The gap boundary locates A1 and determines whether a natural endpoint can contact; its normal does not orient the cap. Each layer stores its complete retained polyline. Body strips use their own segment directions and join at bends with the existing rounded-junction geometry; a bend does not receive a model-facing cap. Member-local contact construction and compound body assembly both retain the bends. Natural endpoints and junctions do not acquire contacts by tracing past their finite limits. A finite endpoint on the clearance contour can supply an actual contact, including at zero gap; an endpoint away from that contour does not authorize a new contact. Band clipping follows retained-path and source-contact presence, with the downstream-member exception described below, rather than an unconditional transition at every finite endpoint. When A1 coincides with the model contour, that actual model contour's outward normal supplies the zero-length contact-axis direction, with hole normals oriented out of the solid. A band whose top has no body is invalid. A missing retained path clips the band. Source-contact loss clips it when there is no downstream line; otherwise continuation may retain the scheduled bottom. Fixed-plane transitions stop when their member has no retained path. Analytical cap construction is shared by ordinary and crease fins. It accepts both signs of the longitudinal-direction/cap-normal dot product; only an absolute denominator at most EPSILON rejects the parallel line intersection. No cap-facing angle rejection is applied. The physical envelope is expanded from the common clipped center geometry only at toolpath generation; it does not impose another per-intent acceptance check. The completed center geometry receives the existing contact-and-transition-exempted model-gap mask afterward; the first path clip does not replace this physical-clearance step.

## Ordinary and crease contact rules

Ordinary Painted Shape contacts use the same nearest-point and angle-clipping rule for point and line bodies. The search covers this model's entire current-layer section rather than an originally selected contour. After clipping the query direction to plus or minus 30 degrees around the local body direction, the ordinary resolver constructs its straight tangent front at the physical wall hit. It retains the configured contact length and requires at least half the sample points to contact, rounding .5 upward, rather than shortening the front. At a band top this invalidates the band; below the top it invokes the confirmed continuation rule. The resolved front and the body/contact axis remain separate values.

Crease contact uses the same source-intersection and wall-query method for a line body and a point body's highest band. The original crease geometry, rather than an XY search radius, determines Q at each queried `slice_z`. The highest point band's taper retains this method below the band; periodic point bands and their tapers use ordinary contact. A source query without a unique result supplies no new actual contact and follows the applicable band or taper failure rule. `support_object_xy_distance` controls body clearance, not a nearest/sharpest-corner search radius for crease contact.

A resolved crease line retains its original 3D source geometry. Its finite body path uses the unchanged vertices with the added outer root extension; the stem and source path form one generation group. Each layer clips that body path by the common model gap and retains its A1. Independently, `FinCreaseIntent::position_at_z()` intersects the original 3D source with the layer's `slice_z` to obtain Q. Shared-vertex hits count once; multiple distinct intersections or no intersection supply no new source contact. The source is not extrapolated, and a missing intersection is not replaced by a nearby convex corner. The synthetic root stem is excluded from this source query. At Q, the model's horizontal section supplies its outward bisector n. A ray from Q along -n finds the printed-wall hit H; A1-H defines the contact axis before body-cap and side-chain construction. Both cap and printable-center model clearance clip the side chains, whose actual endpoints are shared by body, contact and taper. Below the source range, no new actual crease contact is created; any retained geometry follows the common continuation and taper rules.

Direct crease lines and points share contact schedules, printable-center construction and merged output. Whenever a band uses crease contact, its source query is the original-3D-source intersection at `slice_z`, followed by Q/-n wall coverage and A1-H axis resolution. The contact front is perpendicular to n, independently of A1-H, and retains the configured contact width and half-bead placement. The section-normal calculation sums adjacent unit outward edge normals equally; a source point inside a straight section edge uses that edge's normal. Missing or ambiguous source intersections or section normals are contact failures, not permission to choose a nearest or sharpest corner. Crease contacts have no turn limit. After resolving their physical wall hit, they construct the configured straight front perpendicular to the source exterior bisector, with the half-bead offset. This completes crease-front solving: no lateral surface sampling, span-coverage validation or physical touching-length test follows. Their source-normal ray must still hit printed wall coverage. Blockers and subsequent body attachment remain common contact responsibilities.

**Contact-type scope for point and line bodies:** A crease line uses crease contact for its highest band, periodic bands and each band's downward taper. A crease point uses the same crease source query for its highest band and that band's downward taper only. All lower periodic bands of a point fin, including a point selected from a crease, use ordinary contact; their tapers also use ordinary contact. The ordinary +/-30-degree turn clip and half-sample physical-wall coverage checks therefore apply to those periodic bands. Contact type belongs to the band and remains attached to its taper; it is not selected solely from the current layer's position inside or below the highest band's height range. A point fin's ray, expansion and body clipping remain the ordinary point-body construction throughout.

Crease point and line contacts share one source-query pipeline; point classification does not select a different source-search rule. The source geometry needed for crease contact is distinct from the body input. Classifying an input as a point fin changes its body to a ray while retaining a separate original 3D contact source. A point produced by the paired point/cap-exhaustion decision retains the common untrimmed painted crease interval; a point produced by one accepted section retains that complete source section interval. Line inputs retain their accepted source interval. Original vertices and interval endpoints supply the same unique-height-intersection query in all cases. Body classification depends on the body input, not on the number of contact-source vertices. No gap-radius corner search or conversion of a point body into a line body is needed.

Direct crease inputs do not participate in ordinary green fitting, junction attachment or normal correction. All their completed bodies participate in the common final model-gap subtraction; only actual contact and transition regions provide exemptions, never the whole crease body. Contacts use the shared side endpoints after cap and clearance clipping. Member rejection and group-based root reachability retain the existing rules. No failure restores consumed blue geometry.

Every actual Painted Shape contact layer uses its own resolved contact axis and its member-owned attachment. Point and line bodies supply A1 and the local body direction to the resolver selected for that band; a taper keeps its parent band's contact type. Before constructing the body, each contact layer resolves the printed-wall hit using its contact-type query. Crease contacts update their contact axis to A1-H; ordinary contacts retain their source axis. Body caps use the resolved contact axis, and each band and its taper consume the cached hit and shared attachment for their contact type. The physical hit anchors the contact front. Ordinary fronts use the straight tangent approximation at the configured length and the half-sample acceptance rule; they are not defined by taking a perpendicular to the clipped query axis. Crease fronts use the source intersection's horizontal exterior bisector as their normal. Its outer arc replaces the body's shared cap edge. Each contact validates its applicable span, blocker and geometry rules. No contact midpoint is constrained by the highest contact layer's vertical plane. Simple and Simulated retain their existing per-layer painted contacts.

## Contact-band lifecycle and continuation

Painted Shape seed and periodic bands are processed from their scheduled top downward. The top must construct an actual contact; it cannot borrow geometry from an earlier band. Every failed contact attempt below the top invokes continuation, including wall, turn, span, blocker and geometry failures. This failure-routing rule does not settle the continuation attachment method described below. Actual and carried sections retain distinct contact flags. Neither continuation nor taper creates roots or restores rejected members.

Finite band clipping retains the existing code's rules. Scanning down from the scheduled top, a missing retained path ends the band above that layer. A missing source contact does the same when the member has no downstream line. With a downstream line, source-contact loss can continue using retained geometry through the scheduled bottom. Downstream membership comes from resolved directed node order, including an interior junction of another continued line; a junction shared only by incoming lines is insufficient. The test uses source-contact presence, not printed-wall-hit presence. A wall-query miss with a source still present is a contact failure and follows the lower-layer continuation rule. Taper starts below the resulting retained band bottom and stops if the member's retained path disappears.

**Continuation attachment:** A continued layer retains the last successful actual contact front and its original contact-axis perpendicular, separately from the clipped rear attachment. The saved perpendicular is constructed by the common cap resolver through that successful layer's A1 plus the half-bead inset along its resolved contact-to-body axis, before side-chain and model-clearance clipping. Its position and direction remain those of that actual contact; continuation does not relocate it to the current A1. This saved cap line joins the current layer's shared side-chain cuts. The complete current centerline produces both side chains; the current cap, saved contact-axis perpendicular and printable-center model clearance retain their common outer-to-inner prefix. The saved perpendicular only clips a side; it never extends it. The final side endpoints supply both the contact lower base P/Q and the ends of the open body perimeter. All applicable side cuts precede closure. The original body snapshot remains available for termination queries, but must not replace this cut perimeter or contribute old endpoints to the completed contour. The saved two front points connect directly to those endpoints through the common trapezoid/triangle construction. No backward corner rays or auxiliary rear bridge are constructed, and no separate connected/hole-free query-body test decides continuation eligibility. Only a new actual contact that passes validation and body/contact boundary construction replaces the saved front and perpendicular together. This also applies to actual contact candidates within a taper, before fixed-plane clipping. A successful continued layer records its resulting rear attachment for that layer's geometry only; it does not update the saved front or perpendicular. Unequal side-clearance cuts therefore cannot rotate or move the next layer's continuation limit. A failed continuation also leaves the saved state intact and follows the existing lower-band omission or taper-layer skip rule. The same construction serves ordinary and crease contacts, point and finite-path bodies, contact-band continuation and downward taper.

Painted Shape contact bands publish actual and continued sections under the common top-failure/lower-continuation rule. Continued sections use the saved front and the actual endpoints from the additional rear-edge cut. Finite-band clipping follows path/source presence and downstream membership. Highest-band failure rejects the complete generating member; surviving finite members are checked for root reachability, with group-start retention and the component-wide termination state. Ordinary stem and direct crease-path grouping remain as prepared. Simple and Simulated retain their existing whole-band validation.

## Member completion and component assembly

A component with multiple generation roots may have separate cross-sections above their physical junction. Physical layer regions remain an `ExPolygons` collection through bounds, overlap checks and outlining; no secondary region is dropped to fit a single-polygon container.

Each member keeps a component-center record until layer finalization. It retains its source edge, uncropped body-center layers for continuation and exposure queries, completed member contours, contact/transition regions, and per-layer materialization states. Attachment edges belong to contact construction and its layer/contact-type cache; completed extensions do not store them separately. Each resolved layer/contact-type pair owns one printable-center cap, quantized to integer XY and shared by its body and contact producers. Contact lookup and body attachment are cached by layer and contact type. A highest crease-point taper and an ordinary periodic band can therefore resolve different contacts on the same layer without overwriting each other. Each section closes its own body/contact boundary before publication; member assembly unions those completed local contours while retaining contact-only regions for clearance exemptions. Every taper layer forms its local trapezoid, from a new contact or the saved successful front, and clips it against the fixed transition plane. Before splicing the result into a contact-specific body boundary, the single fixed-plane/contact-axis intersection point is tested against that member's original physical body envelope, reconstructed from its body-center layer with the same half-bead outward offset used for body generation. The intersection must exist and be inside or on its boundary to end the taper; trapezoid side intersections and remaining corners do not control termination. The point query excludes other members and other bands' contact/taper geometry. The terminal absorption layer still completes and publishes its body/taper contour, separate taper region and materialized state through the normal path. If that join fails, the layer stays inactive for this extension and assembly uses the original body unless another independent extension supplies a completed contour; evaluation still stops below the terminal layer. Query geometry is not substituted for owned construction boundaries. Contact regions remain separate semantic data for contact flags, clearance exemptions and diagnostics; they are not independent polygons left for the compound pass to attach. A compound is assembled only after all members have completed their local boundary joins.

Geometry uses completion barriers rather than incremental compound merging. Connected finite members resolve their common maximum thickness before constructing boundaries or contacts. Each member constructs two continuous, open symmetric side chains in source-path order. Adjacent inner side lines meet at their intersection; an outer bend uses one circular arc with the shared arc-discretization utility and a 0.005 mm chord tolerance. Segments do not produce overlapping closed strips. The root outer end connects the two sides with a flat edge. Cross-member junctions add only missing circular sectors where at least two incident contributions meet. A lone surviving endpoint stays flat, and an internal bend already owned by a member is not supplemented with a duplicate sector.

The contact cap is perpendicular to the resolved contact axis for ordinary and crease intents alike. A contact cap retains the half-bead inset. A reached tail junction omits that inset; junction assembly supplies the applicable shared sectors rather than an unconditional round end cap. Each complete open side is traversed from the outer end toward the model, including every sampled circular join. The cap-line cut and the model-clearance cut retain their common outer-connected prefix: the first encountered limit owns that side endpoint, and all subsequent segments and arcs are discarded even if they later re-enter the allowed area. Side clearance uses the same model offset by gap plus half the support extrusion width as final layer clipping, rather than the gap-only offset used to locate A1. Only when the finite side has no cap-line intersection may its terminal line extend forward to the cap before clearance clipping; it cannot extend backward across a prior bend. Both clipped side endpoints of the original body are stored once and shared by its end contacts and tapers. A continued contact applies its additional saved-perpendicular limit to these same side chains. Its final P/Q and open body perimeter must come from that one fully cut result; the pre-continuation body snapshot is retained only for queries. No gap-contour arc is inserted between them; the contact front connects directly to these endpoints, and the temporary shared bottom edge disappears during boundary splicing. The open perimeter runs from one attachment through the root end to the other. It is never reconstructed by locating guessed endpoints in an already normalized offset polygon. Exact path reversal has no finite inner-side intersection; no fold-repair policy is provided.

The generating member prepends its own gap-clipped root depth before side-chain construction and contact attachment. This extension retains its flat outer end and half-bead inset. Where the root extension and intent continue through an internal vertex, their side chains form one continuous boundary without an internal transverse cap or a duplicate root strip. Junction contributions follow the incident-member sector rule; a single incident endpoint is not given a standalone round cap. Source intent heights and graph connectivity remain unchanged.

Seed and periodic contacts and their transitions are completed against the member's own body before the compound barrier. Body regions closed across the attachment are normalized, bed-clipped query snapshots for exposure. Final member construction starts from the owned open perimeter instead of those snapshots. The original member body remains an open chain around the root until contact/transition attachment endpoints are known. For end contacts, each accepted section supplies its outer arc with the shared rear chain excluded; these arcs are ordered along the body closing edge, retaining uncovered intervals. Continued contacts use the same closure: finish the current-cap, saved-perpendicular and model-clearance side cuts first, then connect the selected contact outer arc to the resulting P/Q-root perimeter. The pre-cut body is never reintroduced to recover an exterior boundary. P/Q must equal the two ends of the supplied open perimeter; a mismatch fails the attempt instead of restoring old endpoints. Only these complete point sequences are converted to closed output polygons, followed by bed clipping. No temporary closed body polygon, overlap-area test or area-union fallback participates in local body/contact closure. The separately constructed bed foot retains its existing expansion geometry. Each completed member contour enters assembly exactly once. Compound assembly adds only missing cross-member junction sectors not already owned by an internal bend; it does not give each junction endpoint a separate round cap. Root depth is already part of its generating member. It does not regenerate a second whole body network, widen completed members, or unite their pre-contact body copies with their completed contours. Shared junction contributions are collected at exact printed-coordinate endpoints without creating new intent-graph connections or duplicating already owned boundary geometry. The union resolves physical intersections between these distinct completed contributions. Shared node identities retain connectivity between finite groups, including different roots and interior junctions. Clipping and member rejection follow prepared generation groups; interior junction identities provide connectivity without splitting those failure units. Member body snapshots remain available for contact solving; they are never added alongside the completed member contour. Only that completed contour supplies member geometry to output. Transition and actual-contact regions separately provide the model-clearance exemptions. The layer-wide clearance pass follows assembly, and its result directly enters merged outlining. Half-bead expansion records the physical envelope at that output boundary; there is no intervening ownership remapping or per-fin topology/stack rejection. No toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

For a composite contact, the member-local body supplies the owned attachment used by contact construction and supplies continuation geometry and the projection-exposure reference. A sibling branch cannot terminate or shorten that member's transition. Completed member contours already include their root extensions and internal bends; compound assembly unites these completed contributions with the required shared-junction sectors, without adding another root extension. The component's greatest member thickness is resolved before member geometry, so body and contact attachments agree without a later widened-body copy; each member retains its own contact width and attachment. After line components and independent point intents are prepared, every prepared support center on the same print layer is united. Actual contact triangles/trapezoids and transition regions supply exemptions in the cached round `support_object_xy_distance + extrusion width / 2` model-clearance mask. Before subtraction from that mask, the contact/transition union is expanded outward by half the model gap, using round joins with a 0.005 mm arc tolerance and no additional safety offset. This protects nearby body material but does not guarantee complete join protection, as described below. That mask clips the layer union once. The resulting layer centers go directly to toolpath generation, where they are expanded once by half an extrusion width to record the common physical envelope used for output-region grouping, inner walls and bed fill. No per-contributor geometry is reconstructed after clearance. Region count, holes, thickness and cross-layer connectivity do not trigger a second whole-fin rejection pass. Source contours remain available only for diagnostic attribution and seam-direction selection; transition-retention statistics compare against the common clipped layer. Ordinary and crease point/line bodies all participate in this common clearance pass; only their actual contact and transition sections supply clearance exemptions.

Sibling cavity filling groups attached lines by their immutable source island, attachment-host edge and original junction node. Correcting, splitting or detaching a junction does not erase this provenance. Once all members are assembled, each layer unions only the group's completed member contours, which already include their contacts and transitions, excluding the attachment host and other groups. With at least two contributing source children (finite splits of one child count once), bounded holes of that union are filled once; open notches are unchanged. Member sections remain identifiable after compound assembly so the attachment host cannot accidentally close a hole for the attached lines. Fills retain a touching component as their diagnostic source, enter the common layer union, and receive ordinary model-clearance clipping without introducing a contact exemption or restoring graph connections.

Before the final model-gap subtraction, the union of contact and taper regions is expanded outward by half of `support_object_xy_distance`, using round joins with a 0.005 mm arc tolerance. This expanded region is subtracted from the model-clearance mask without an additional safety offset; zero gap gives zero expansion. The exemption protects nearby fin-body material as well as the contact trapezoid, so local model clearance can be smaller than the configured gap. It does not guarantee protection of the complete join, especially at corners. Any small hole left by the final cut can appear as an extrusion dot or a short path between the contact trapezoid and the fin body when outlined.

## Downward taper

Painted Shape downward support clips independent per-layer contact trapezoids against one fixed inclined plane C. X is the mathematical midpoint of the lowest contact segment, before the half-bead offset. Let n be the bottom section's resolved unit XY contact-to-body axis, and t = (-n.y, n.x, 0). This is the ordinary clipped axis or the crease A1-H axis, retained with the saved front when continuing a contact. Starting with the horizontal plane through X, rotate it downward toward A1 by `90 degrees - fin_support_contact_projection_angle` about the horizontal hinge through X parallel to t. For setting x, the retained half-space is `cos(x) * dot(n, P.xy - X.xy) + sin(x) * (P.z - X.z) >= 0`. The hinge and C stay fixed throughout this band. Tapering starts below the retained band bottom. The retained bottom follows the finite-band path/source-presence and downstream-member rules. A downstream line can preserve the scheduled bottom through continuation; without one, the first missing source contact clips the band. A missing retained path clips it in either case. The bottom section's saved front midpoint and resolved contact-to-body axis define C at that bottom layer's `slice_z`; the separate crease-front normal does not rotate C. Carried sections preserve both directions from their last actual contact. Each lower layer with an actual contact forms its local trapezoid from its own A1 and Ncap. Each newly resolved taper contact uses that lower layer's stored printed-wall hit and resolved axis; it does not reuse a higher layer's hit unless it is continuing the saved front after a failed attempt. A failed local contact attempt invokes the shared continuation: the last successful actual contact's original axis perpendicular joins the current side cuts, and its saved front connects directly to their resulting endpoints. The clipped endpoint chord is used only for this layer's attachment, never as the next layer's continuation limit. Taper retains its parent band's contact rule. A crease line's tapers and a crease point's highest-band taper use original-source intersections at each `slice_z`; lower periodic point-band tapers use ordinary contact. No taper selects a nearby or sharper corner to replace a missing source intersection. Neither query searches beyond a finite endpoint. Once taper begins, a successful lower-layer query supplies taper geometry only and cannot reopen the contact band. The resulting geometry is clipped to C at `slice_z`. A layer whose contact, cap or carried trapezoid cannot be constructed contributes no taper geometry; solving continues on the next lower layer without discarding the other layers or the fin. No convergence layer is selected and no endpoint is interpolated between layers. Abrupt changes of the local contact axis receive no special smoothing or cross-layer distortion checks.

Lower-layer evaluation stops at the bed, loss of the retained member path, or entry of the fixed-plane/contact-axis intersection point into the generating member's original physical body envelope. Entirely cut-away sections emit nothing and do not themselves terminate evaluation. For a nonempty plane-and-bed-clipped section, intersect the fixed plane's XY trace with the selected contact axis through its printed-wall point, in its resolved contact-to-body direction. An accepted actual contact supplies the current layer's wall point and axis; continuation uses the wall point and axis saved from the last successful actual contact. This axis is distinct from the front normal, the saved perpendicular, and the rear chord P/Q. Use the same integer plane trace as clipping and solve the two infinite lines; displayed unit-axis segments and the bounded plane-mask edge do not limit this intersection. Parallel or coincident lines have no unique intersection and do not satisfy termination. This one resulting point must be inside or on the boundary of the original member's physical body envelope, excluding other members and other bands. Reconstruct this envelope from the original body-center area by an outward offset of half the current layer's support extrusion width, using the same round joins and 0.005 mm arc tolerance as physical body generation. The inset body-center area itself is not the termination reference. This envelope is used only for the point queries and diagnostics, never substituted for the owned open perimeter or added to output. Do not test the entire taper polygon for containment or require its remaining corners to enter the body. This single-axis-point condition marks the current layer as terminal before boundary completion. The section still proceeds through the common body-attachment step and, if successful, publishes its completed body/contact contour and separate transition region before stopping. All lower layers receive no geometry or saved-cap cuts from this taper. If the terminal-layer join fails, it emits no taper and still stops instead of retrying below. Nonterminal sections follow the normal completion and failure-skip rules. These sections remain identifiable until compound completion and participate in the existing contact-and-transition model-clearance exemption and final layer assembly. A vertical C (setting 0 degrees) retains its fixed XY half-plane at every height; a horizontal C (setting 90 degrees) removes the sections below X. Projection clipping does not reposition the seed, A, Ncap or the fin's outer endpoints. The seed projection remains a temporary mask for suppression of redundant periodic contacts and does not create another contact band.

For Painted Shape, each contact is created directly in printable-center space. Its front edge comes from the contact rule, with a half-support-bead offset along that rule's front normal outside the resolved printed-wall surface. Ordinary contact uses the full configured contact length and rejects insufficient coverage. Only an exactly zero-length front produces one nose vertex; no EPSILON threshold collapses a positive-length front into a triangle. Front endpoints connect to the stored integer side endpoints after both cap-line and model-clearance clipping. The shared cap chain is omitted from each contact section; its remaining outer arc is connected to the open body perimeter using the recorded endpoints. The body is not independently closed along P-Q before this connection. Full-width contacts, zero-length noses and partial taper/bed sections use the same point-based closure. Multiple sections with disjoint attachment intervals are ordered along Q-to-P, retaining uncovered portions of the edge between them. Section holes are preserved as holes of the completed contour. There is no guessed rear overlap or Boolean union as a local attachment method. Membership on the recorded attachment allows only one scaled integer unit for clipping-intersection quantization, not an EPSILON search or snapping to unrelated nearby edges. Carried contacts record their rear attachment after adding the saved actual-contact axis perpendicular to the side cuts. This attachment does not replace the saved perpendicular. The same fully cut side chains supply the open body perimeter for final closure, and its endpoints must match the recorded attachment exactly. Each section identifies one nonzero contiguous rear chain and disjoint rear intervals are ordered along the closing edge. Missing attachments, mismatched perimeter endpoints or ambiguous rear intervals fail the existing contact attempt. There is no full-body/contact boundary reconstruction, restoration of pre-cut vertices, proximity bridge or Boolean-union fallback. Unions of completed bands, members and whole-layer output remain separate operations. A failed highest band rejects the generating member as `top-band`; failed lower-band continuation skips that layer. A failed taper attachment logs `contact-boundary` and skips that layer; a terminal absorption layer still stops evaluation below even when attachment fails. The diagnostic does not introduce a separate member-wide rejection. Semantic contact regions remain separately available for masks and diagnostics. Simple and Simulated retain their existing convex center bridge and short internal overlap. `fin_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. For all fin styles, contact length and `fin_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the object's resolved support line width at its configured layer height. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. Simple and Simulated sample contact length plus one support extrusion width and require direct paint across their source-model samples. Their physical-span samples project from laterally shifted A1 positions and reject missing coverage or displacement greater than one support line width. Painted Shape ordinary contacts retain the configured contact length; failure of the half-sample coverage rule rejects the attempt rather than shortening its front. The island-area formula does not establish an additional physical-contact requirement over `c + w`. Crease contacts retain their own front-direction construction. Any failed Painted Shape attempt below the band top follows the common continuation rule. The rounded physical envelope can be wider than its contact span.

## Finalization and toolpaths

Before a Simple or Simulated fin is emitted, its completed printable polygons are checked for positive-area overlap against existing fins on every shared layer above the bed, regardless of seed height or direction. Simple excludes an overlapping candidate; Simulated repeats its upward planning with that original seed unavailable. Painted Shape instead permits different intent edges and brush islands to meet or cross: their completed center areas are united and jointly gap-clipped before the common physical envelope and closed outlines are generated, removing duplicate internal paths. A merged area may contain several disconnected center contours, and each is emitted instead of invalidating the complete layer. The required first loop uses only those completed center contours. Missing contours or first-loop failure are recorded without reconstructing contours by eroding the physical area or retrying individual fins. Optional inner loops use physical-area erosion; exhaustion stops further inner loops and retains the preceding loops without recording a first-loop failure. Merged first-loop failure stops outlining only that layer region and records the failure count, layer heights, region index, center-region count and line width. This failure does not withdraw the contributing fins or previously emitted layers. Every style unites bed feet before printing. Nearby plan checks remain a cheap preliminary filter, not the final overlap test.

The first outer toolpath is the contour of the complete printable-center region. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its body-facing edge uses the shared side endpoints after contact-axis and printable-center gap clipping, prepared before the layer-wide model-gap pass. Its front segment has the configured `contact_width`, including positive lengths below one extrusion width; lack of coverage is not resolved by reducing that length. On a flat wall, expanding this center region by half a bead width makes the model-facing physical boundary tangent along the same span. Ordinary polygon erosion is never used to recover Painted Shape's first center contour because it would change a short contact span or erase a zero-length nose. Additional loops offset inward from the completed physical area by the configured spacing. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance converted to scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, Simple and Simulated finalize each fin's polygons and center areas and check topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry supplies no directional capacity to the Simulated planner. Once geometry for all selected fins is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. For Simulated, an outline failure discards the batch's temporary paths and restarts upward planning with that plan's original seed excluded; its capacity is therefore never counted without a printable fin. Only a successful Simulated batch is stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and used to generate bed infill. Simple excludes the failing candidate before emission. Painted Shape uses the merged outlining behavior described above. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Above the bed, generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit or its center contour is absent or invalid; already generated loops are retained. Painted Shape emits all valid contours of a merged region, including split inner contours; Simple and Simulated require their single-contour loop. Thicker fins can therefore have hollow interiors. Simple and Simulated reject a fin if its first contour cannot be generated; Painted Shape records merged first-loop failure as described above. Each fin contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.

The bed layer unites all accepted fin footprints before generating any bed contours or infill. Its outer center area combines the merged footprint's half-width inset with the saved contact centers, preserving narrow fronts and point contacts. Overlapping feet therefore share a perimeter instead of retaining intersecting internal walls. Disconnected regions and holes retain separate closed contours; further walls use the configured loop count and support flow spacing. Bed contours start at their vertex with the greatest clearance from the first model layer. Infill is generated once over the merged interior after subtracting space for the emitted walls, using the ordinary support infill generator.

## Eligibility and failure scope

Let `w` be support line width, `t` fin thickness, `c` contact length and `g` model XY clearance. Simple and Simulated require their complete painted contact span. Painted Shape ordinary contacts use the half-sample physical-wall coverage criterion and use the common top-failure/lower-continuation rules; table entries below state style-specific exceptions explicitly. Painted Shape point bodies, like line bodies, rely on shared side-chain geometry and model/bed clipping. They do not use the legacy minimum ray-depth checks, highest-wall distance veto, historical projection-intrusion veto or sampled center/side collision veto.
