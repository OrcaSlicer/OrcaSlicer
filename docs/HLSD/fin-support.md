# Fin support

Fin support has three styles. **Simple** preprocesses painted side cuts into intervals on the actual sliced outer contours. The paint grants permission but never supplies candidate coordinates; intervals on the same model edge are merged. Consecutive painted edges on one island form a contact line while their turn stays within 15 degrees and the straight contact remains within half a support line width of that line. Candidate centers need enough room from the ends of the joined line for the complete contact bead, including its line width. On each highest available layer it ranks sharp convex corners and long straight-edge midpoints derived from the complete sliced model contour after scale-dependent simplification. A remaining interval midpoint is the fallback. Every chosen site subtracts an unavailable range at its own contour location and along its downward wall projection. If a lower section has no new wall intersection, the last wall location snaps to a same-facing contour within one support line width; that snapped location continues to the next section. The configured placement distance limits travel along the connected island contour at each location. Walking stops at a back-facing edge, so nearby edges across a fold or on another island remain available. Candidate selection exhausts the region before constructing fins. Each selected site becomes a standard fin candidate; tracing, periodic contact validation, projection, body geometry, finalization and toolpath generation then use the same pipeline as the other styles. Risk is fixed to one and only the existing surface-tilt falloff scales body expansion. **Simulated** uses the printed-prefix planner described below and remains the default.

**Painted shape** first interprets each connected painted island as a scale-aware intent graph. Island connectivity groups one continuous brush gesture; it does not limit the island to one fin. Painted facets connected by an edge, subdivision vertex or exactly coincident mesh vertex belong to one island. Components separated by less than half the printable feature scale are also joined unless their average normals oppose by more than 120 degrees; a sub-contact-width crack cannot describe two independent contacts, while nearby opposite walls remain separate. Every nondegenerate painted facet contributes its transformed area and first moment, including horizontal and locally upward-facing facets. Persistent creases partition the island into macro patches; each patch is projected into its own surface-aligned chart. Gaps within the island-join distance are closed in that chart with a half-distance radius before `Geometry::MedialAxis::build()` extracts its unextended skeleton. Its minimum-width argument remains zero so a valid narrow stroke is not discarded before its topology is known. The returned `ThickPolyline` segments retain their local full painted widths and original junction endpoints.

The intent extractor rebuilds coarse connectivity from those returned polylines. Every consecutive point pair becomes an edge between exact scaled-integer vertices. Each connected graph obtains a global axis from two weighted farthest-point searches. Before local pruning, a compactness classifier measures the original axis mean full width W using chart-length weighting. It simplifies a separate copy of the axis with per-sample error tolerance equal to half the larger of local full width and physical feature scale; replacement chords must remain inside paint. Adjacent axis-edge widths are averaged at shared axis vertices for this tolerance only. The original width integral remains unchanged. With simplified axis length L, a component is compact when max(0, L - W) + EPSILON is less than max(feature_scale, W), or no axis edge exists. All edges of that component are removed together. This subtraction is a classification allowance for caps, not a geometric trim. Other components continue independently. Surviving axes remain protected during first-pass removal of side branches shorter than the larger of the physical feature scale and their length-weighted mean full painted width. Protection applies to this pruning pass only; it does not exempt a later blue group from its common-width threshold. There is no second-pass branch voting or separate isolated-short-path collapse.

Path preparation retains the existing connectivity recovery: degree-two vertices join automatically, while junction directions are measured over one painted-width/physical-feature scale. A continuation within 30 degrees is paired; one between 30 and 60 degrees is paired only when its opposing-direction score exceeds the next pair by at least 0.1. The last two unpaired incident edges are paired. Open fragments without a shared vertex may join when their endpoint tangents continue within 30 degrees, their gap is at most 1.5 times the larger of the physical feature scale and their mean endpoint width, and the complete connector remains inside the same painted area. Closed prepared paths are excluded from line construction; if no blue line survives, the chart emits a standalone point.

Paths beginning or ending at degree-one vertices have their brush caps trimmed inward by half the original path's chart-length-weighted mean full painted width. Both ends use this same pre-trim measurement, without reducing the requested distances to preserve a fraction of the path. Each end stops at the first junction and never consumes the continuation beyond it; a junction endpoint is not cap-trimmed. Only original samples inside the remaining interval survive, with the existing numerical tolerance at its boundaries. No endpoint is interpolated and no excluded sample is retained to make a line. A terminal span with fewer than two surviving points, counting its junction, is removed up to that junction while the continuation remains. A path with fewer than two surviving points is discarded; losing one path does not classify other surviving paths as points. If no blue line survives in the chart area, its point classification is explicit, including for crease diagnostics.

The remaining blue path is coarsened at one full painted width: endpoints stay fixed while sub-width edges remove the interior vertex with the smallest accumulated chord error, provided the replacement remains inside paint. Junction samples still in that interval and one direction witness about one local painted width away along each incident path cannot be removed. Source order, original widths, measurement sources and surface data survive this preparation.

The coarsened blue edges are grouped in the surface-aligned two-dimensional chart, before surface-Z orientation and green fitting. A group follows exact shared source vertices while each successive turn is at most 15 degrees. Among available continuations, the smallest turn wins; exact ties preserve source-list order. Edges belong to one group only. Unassigned edges seed groups in source-list order, extending first toward the seed's second endpoint and then outward from its first endpoint so an interior seed does not split a smooth run. The angle is local, not relative to a fixed overall direction. Group length is the sum of chart-space edge lengths, with no additional smoothing, chord-length substitution or cumulative-turn restriction. Small successive turns can therefore accumulate into a winding path; this filter does not prove that every retained group is a central axis.

For each chart area, the strictly longest blue group supplies one common threshold W; equal-length groups retain list order. W is that group's length-weighted mean full painted width, integrating the mean of each edge's two endpoint widths over its chart-space length. If the longest group is shorter than W, all blue groups are discarded and the chart emits a standalone point. Otherwise every group of length at least W is width-qualified and survives. Short groups also survive when they lie on an existing connection between distinct width-qualified groups, including chains of short groups. The qualifying set is frozen before connector recovery; rescued short groups never become new qualifying anchors. Connections use exact shared source vertices, including junctions inside groups, rather than XY proximity. A short spur touching two qualifying groups at their single shared junction is not a connector. Neither isolated short components nor dangling branches or cycles attached at only one junction qualify. Existing alternative routes between qualifying groups are retained without selecting a new shortest route. There is no per-group width threshold, feature-scale floor, extra cap subtraction or protected-axis exemption. Removing a group splits its source paths at discarded edges; remaining samples are never connected across the resulting gaps. The threshold does not alter any original width or Q measurement. A nonempty input always retains its longest group unless it takes the point alternative. At the final blue-output boundary, a chart area with no retained blue path emits one standalone blue point at its two-dimensional area centroid, with holes subtracted. This includes compactness rejection, cap exhaustion and group rejection. The point uses the same chart-to-surface mapping as line samples and retains its mapped surface position, source face, patch/chart identity, patch macro normal and represented chart area. It has no fabricated width measurement or zero-length line. Patch aggregation carries these points alongside line strokes, so a point from one patch is not lost when another patch in the island has lines. Candidate preparation consumes each explicit point through the existing point-fin pipeline; it never creates a point merely because an island has no lines, and does not substitute an island centroid or a discarded-path length moment. Invalid charts that never reach blue output do not implicitly become points. Actual contact-site selection and line-candidate-to-point alternatives retain their existing validity rules.

Retained source paths split at source junctions, with the junction included in every adjacent span. Group filtering does not move junctions or create connections at projected intersections. At the end of blue processing, every retained edge is directed from higher original surface Z to lower Z; equal-Z endpoints follow their stable source-list order. Paths split where this direction changes. A node with outgoing edges and no incoming edge is a root, including a local maximum inside a path. Equal-Z directed edges count as incoming. Root identity and junction connectivity are independent, so a root can also be a junction. There is no component-wide highest-point fallback or later reversal from one global root.

The ordinary intent pipeline is painted area → orange medial axis → dark-blue samples and width measurements → original 3D surface → world XY → final green graph → fin checks. `Geometry::MedialAxis` optionally returns one measurement for every `ThickPolyline::width` entry: the actual contour segment or endpoint source, its identity and the closest point Q in scaled chart coordinates. Segment sources use the nearest point on that finite segment; vertex sources use the vertex. The existing side choice is preserved. Whenever shared samples retain the smaller full width, they retain its matching Q and source. No opposite-side averaging or side reselection is used. The chart point P, original width, source identity, mapped surface position and Z, and mapped Q all travel with retained blue samples. P and Q use the same surface mapping and world-XY projection. Fitting uses `h_i = max(norm(Q'_i - P'_i), EPSILON)`, where `EPSILON = 0.0001 mm` is only a numerical lower bound for the allowed half-width. It does not alter the original measurement. There is no perpendicular width projection or elliptical error model. Samples coincident in XY retain their independent surface heights, order and sources.

Ordinary fits, free fits and branch comparisons use the same XY arc-length weights: `w_i = (norm(P'_i - P'_(i-1)) + norm(P'_(i+1) - P'_i)) / 2`, counting only adjacent edges inside the participating range. A fixed-start orthogonal fit passes through its fixed anchor; a free fit passes through the weighted centroid. The anchor is a constraint, not an additional sample. For unit direction d and line origin O, every participating point is checked with `e_i = norm((P'_i - O) - dot(P'_i - O, d) * d)` and `E = max(e_i / h_i)`. Acceptance requires `E <= 1`, including samples of zero weight. A single sample with a fixed anchor initially uses the anchor-to-sample direction. A free first segment waits for a distinct XY position to determine its direction. If the entire group coincides in XY, it remains a degenerate planar segment with all original surface data and heights and reaches the existing downstream macro-plane handling.

Ordinary growth adds the next unaccepted blue sample, refits all participating samples and projects the newly added sample onto the line as the candidate tail. An accepted candidate saves the complete state. A failing candidate restores the previous accepted state and confirms that segment. The next segment is fixed at its predecessor's confirmed tail and retries the rejected sample. Accepted samples do not participate in the next fit, and the generated tail does not become a blue sample. The predecessor's original surface endpoint is retained separately as boundary metadata for the represented surface interval. There is no green minimum-length filter, recursive whole-range splitting, adjacent-fit merging or post-fit segment absorption. Fine-grained skeleton cleanup belongs to orange and dark blue.

Roots start green solving in source-list order. Each junction waits for all incoming blue paths before choosing continuations; arrival timing does not select the main line. Every incoming accepted state is compared with every outgoing blue path. Each pair adds up to two subsequent blue samples, excluding the starting junction. The next junction or path end stops the window and is itself included. All pairs use the smallest available count. Each pair is fitted and checked once after its entire window, using only that incoming state's samples and the selected outgoing window.

Among compliant pairs, repeatedly choose the smallest E, using each incoming and outgoing path at most once, up to two continuations per source junction. Equal scores for distinct eligible choices preserve source-list order. If multiple incoming paths score within EPSILON of the best compliant E for the same outgoing path, that outgoing path loses continuation eligibility; it remains available as a separately solved child and may reconnect geometrically. Each winner carries its full accepted state and samples into further growth. A continuation remains one straight green segment across the junction; adding an interior junction does not split or refit it. Ordinary later growth may still exceed the fit tolerance and confirm that segment under the usual rollback rule.

Unselected incoming paths confirm their accepted states and register their last actual green segments for tail attachment. Unselected outgoing paths grow freely in source order, leaving the main-facing endpoint of their first actual green segment open. Once that first segment is confirmed, subsequent segments use fixed starts. Grandchildren use the same competition and forward-growth rules. If no continuation is selected, the source-list-first incoming segment's confirmed tail supplies the common junction. Paths sharing a source root use its fixed start. Every outgoing first segment of a source root retains that root even if its tail merges elsewhere.

Each source junction records its final main segments, each outgoing child's nearest actual green segment and each unselected upstream tail. Attachment waits until these segments are solved. An internal junction is resolved after its main segments' external endpoint attachments, using committed segment identities rather than whole source paths. A confirmed stopped tail or fixed root start does not depend on the opposite endpoint. Reconnection moves only the registered head or tail, preserving its other endpoint, and may rotate, extend or truncate the whole segment. It neither refits nor checks the residual again. If actual committed segments create a cycle of endpoint dependencies, cancel the endpoint attachments at source junctions in that cycle before applying any of them, preserving the fitted segments. Junctions outside the cycle still resolve normally. A valid crossing between two continued mains may still be represented without moving either endpoint; it waits for any remaining external attachments.

When two continued finite main segments have a unique intersection, that point is their shared internal junction. Both segments remain whole, and all pending children and upstream tails at that source junction attach directly to the crossing without averaging it with their own intersections. Otherwise the main segments remain separate. Each pending segment casts a ray from its opposite endpoint toward the endpoint being attached and chooses the first finite main segment it hits; equal hit distances preserve main source-list order. Parallel, directionless, backward and out-of-range intersections are unusable. Attachments are grouped by the chosen main, and each group retains the arithmetic-mean collapse to one junction on that main. A mean outside the finite main disconnects its contributors; a ray with no usable hit disconnects individually. There is no averaging across separate mains. Connected members share an actual XY node while retaining their original blue junction and root identities. Disconnected members retain their solved geometry and independent ownership. Foldback detection is intentionally absent.

Candidate preparation, straight-line correction and fin-intent resolution share one private working graph. Each island owns a stable collection of mutable nodes and a fixed collection of line records; lines hold node references, and nodes refer to immutable source measurements. `prepare_painted_fin_sources()` creates one working entity per green node, without copying junction state into each line. `correct_painted_fin_lines()` first plans rotations and reconnections against frozen segments, then commits node positions and reference changes. A shared junction remains one entity unless its corrected attachments actually split; a broken endpoint attachment creates an independent free endpoint. Coincident hits originating at the same junction share a node. If distinct source junctions meet the same host position after correction, the existing host placement supplies the shared entity in source-list order, including its complete source identity and height bound. Incoming references reuse that entity rather than retaining its ID with a different source Z. Direction acceptance still participates in contact-host selection and contact-top validation. `make_painted_fin_candidate()` prepares physical attributes without copying the graph. The ordinary straight-line selector orders its endpoint-first node references along the accepted line before passing them to fin-intent resolution. Prepared groups carry ordered node references, allowing a nonbranching polyline with shared junctions; fin-intent resolution preserves this order without chord sorting, reversal or refitting. Only completed fin intents become value snapshots containing every path vertex and its resolved height for the layer-geometry pipeline; no working reference escapes that boundary. The ordinary selector retains line-to-point fallback. Accepted convex-crease outputs enter through a separate direct-path producer. Their source vertices bypass ordinary green fitting, straight-line normal correction and candidate-angle/site selection; they still use the same shared-node fin-intent boundary and ordinary contact realization.

All source charts finish blue generation before ordinary green solving begins. Selected blue chains supplied to a measured crease pair are consumed as a whole, regardless of missing/ambiguous hits, symmetry rejection or later fin failure. Point pairs consume their participating selected chains and any explicit source-chart point. Consumption uses original chart-local sample identity, so shared junction occurrences are removed consistently. Remaining ordinary blue paths split at consumed samples; there is no bridging chord across a consumed point, no restored source after rejection and no new area-center fallback. Residual spans still require at least two samples. Multiple eligible crease pairs remain separate; they are not merged or deduplicated.

Each accepted crease part has independent nodes and generation ownership, separate from ordinary fins and other crease parts. Line inputs retain all accepted original 3D crease vertices, oriented from the higher endpoint toward the lower endpoint; equal endpoint heights retain source order. A subsequent increase larger than EPSILON rejects the whole path before a root stem can be created, without splitting or repairing it. The source high end supplies the root; an isolated point supplies its own position. Crease-line XY and Z enter unchanged, without per-vertex offset. The complete-model owner samples the root 3D normal for expansion slope and computes its horizontal-section exterior bisector for root extension. Required root heights are batched through the regular mesh slicer at source Z (using slicer float precision), not the nearest print layer. The root section uses equal unit outward normals of adjacent contour edges, or the normal of a straight edge. Missing or ambiguous root direction, or an invalid root surface normal, rejects the line without restoring consumed blue points. Interior vertices no longer require normal or section-direction queries. Point inputs retain the existing direction from the normalized sum of the two separately normalized XY face normals, their source face aggregate for slope, and ordinary ray-generated geometry.

A resolved crease line retains its original 3D contact source. Its finite body path uses the same unchanged vertices, preceded by the added outer root vertex. The root stem and crease form one continuous generation group, with the original root retained as an internal junction. This group owns both the highest and periodic contact bands. Each layer projects the complete path to XY and applies the same model-gap path clipping as ordinary finite lines, keeping only the portion connected to the group start. Its surviving tail supplies A1. The contact axis defines the perpendicular cap line with the existing half-bead inset. Each complete symmetric side chain, offset by half of (fin thickness minus extrusion width), is truncated by both that line and the model clearance for printable centers. The clearance is the model offset by gap plus half an extrusion width. The surviving outer-connected side endpoints supply the body, contact trapezoid and downward taper; they need not both lie on the cap line. Independently, the original 3D source supplies the unique crease intersection at slice_z as the contact-axis anchor. From that source point Q, the ray opposite its horizontal exterior bisector finds the first printed-wall coverage hit H. The resolved contact axis is A1-H and is stored before body-cap and side-chain construction. Shared-vertex hits count once; distinct intersections are not arbitrarily selected. The synthetic same-height root stem is excluded from the source intersection query. A missing body cannot generate contact. Below the source end, surviving body geometry may continue through ordinary clipping but no new actual crease contact is created. An unavailable unique intersection within the source height range is diagnosed rather than replaced by a nearest model point.

Direct crease lines and points share ordinary Painted Shape contact schedules, taper, printable-center construction and merged toolpath output. Crease-line contact queries printed-wall coverage from the original source point Q inward along the negative horizontal-section exterior bisector n. The first hit H becomes the contact midpoint, and the axis from H toward the gap-clipped A1 orients the body cap and taper. Its contact segment remains perpendicular to n, independently of the resolved A1-H axis. The bisector reuses the root-section calculation: adjacent unit edge normals contribute equally, while a point inside one edge uses that edge's normal. Per-layer queries use the existing model slice at `slice_z`, without another mesh slice or a projected 3D average. A missing or ambiguous section normal is diagnosed and cannot borrow the query axis as a substitute. Crease-point highest bands use the same Q/-n wall query and A1-H axis resolution at their selected convex corner; their lower periodic bands retain the ordinary point-contact query and orientation. The prescribed contact segment joins directly to the body's two surviving side endpoints to form its trapezoid, retaining the configured contact width and half-bead placement. Crease-line contact-axis turning has no angle limit. Ordinary and crease point/line intents share the contact-axis cap and model-gap side clipping; the crease-source bisector supplies the inward wall query and independently orients the contact front. Crease lines retain their direct contact-span policy without the ordinary bead-span displacement check; the source-normal ray must still hit printed wall coverage. The highest and periodic bands use this same rule, and fixed-inclined-plane downward taper remains enabled; there is no split two-face contact construction. Direct inputs do not participate in ordinary green junction attachment, normal correction or inter-intent composite grouping. The line and its outer extension undergo ordinary model-gap path clipping. All Painted Shape point and line intents, including creases, use the same final model-gap subtraction and contact/transition exemptions. No crease member exempts its complete body. Side clipping and final clearance use the same model-offset geometry, and contacts are constructed only after the shared side endpoints have been resolved. Bed limits, contact validity and cap construction still apply. Output contour validity and loop-fit checks apply to the merged layer, not to separately reconstructed physical candidates. Actual model-contact failure can reject an input; it never restores consumed blue geometry. Direct point inputs are not displaced and retain ordinary point-body clearance processing. Their input position and sheet normal remain fixed; the root anchor is selected separately on the sliced model, then its inward horizontal-normal ray locates the printed-wall contact.

Geometric attachment names describe their role rather than generation ancestry: `attachment_host_edge` identifies the line used for reconnection, `attachment_point` records that attachment, and `source_attachment_junction` preserves the original host/node pair for sibling cavity grouping. None determines a generation root or a junction height.

The ordinary retained green graph passes through normal correction and candidate-angle checks before becoming fin intent. Source surface samples, width measurements and source height bounds remain immutable. A planar junction never passes back through the chart mapping. The resulting finite path and its one-time model queries are described below.

A chart whose surface normals cancel or whose projected triangles overlap by more than 30% cannot supply ordinary blue geometry or a two-sided crease comparison. A persistent crease alone does not create fin intent: it needs usable opposite-patch blue outputs and the crease selection, cap and section rules below. Represented length, painted width, surface position and height bounds derive from source surface data, never from a generated XY segment or junction. The feature scale used by skeleton cleanup is `max(3 * support line width, contact length + support line width)`.

An ordinary chart with no retained blue path supplies its own area-center point. Every ordinary graph edge carries a fitted line description; candidate selection may use a point at its center if the line cannot be accepted before fin-intent conversion. Ordinary fitted edges from one macro patch share that patch's crease-aware, area-weighted macro normal. Direct crease inputs retain their two source face normals separately. Each edge's endpoint chord supplies its own turn within this common projection frame, and its vertical plane is anchored at the final green segment's first XY endpoint. The sheet direction therefore comes from the painted macro region rather than the normal at a later selected contact point. Direction ambiguity is measured entirely in the XY ground-plane projection: the unnormalized endpoint chord is oriented toward the projected macro normal, and its endpoint is compared with the equal-radius endpoint on that macro direction. A separation no greater than one full local painted width is drawing-scale ambiguity, so the edge uses the common macro direction. Correction rotates the finite XY segment about that fixed origin while preserving its projected length; the source Z limits remain separate contact-height semantics. All corrected axes are frozen before junction updates. An affected dependent endpoint follows its fixed A axis toward the host's finite rotated segment, using its head-or-tail attachment orientation. A missed or backward hit breaks that connection without erasing a source root or another connection at the member's other end. Rotation about a shared root retains that exact pivot even for coincident corrected axes. Different hits split a source junction; coincident hits from that same source on the same host share a resolved junction. These updates do not feed back into the correction decisions. Only after junction resolution is complete does the candidate resolver check that the normalized XY direction stays within `fin_support_painted_shape_line_angle` of the macro direction. The candidate resolver tries the line description when its fit and plane are usable and otherwise resolves the point description before creating the proposal. This setting defaults to 40 degrees and supplies the direction-angle threshold for ordinary Painted Shape line selection. Once A is accepted, contact-band generation validates actual contact geometry without another fixed 30-degree angle rejection. Candidate-resolution failures can still select the point alternative before conversion. `fin_support_contact_projection_angle` independently controls downward projection. A segment with a fixed contact top retains its highest source surface sample and explicit start Z as the contact target. Other targets use their length-weighted center and move upward by half the configured contact height.

For a line intent, the fixed vertical intent plane intersects each painted sliced contour directly; the selector does not project the target to the nearest arbitrary painted segment. Point alternatives still use the nearest contour position because a point supplies no plane; accepted point intents use the existing directional construction when no finite path is prescribed. Candidate hosts require a usable local macro side, an exact complete-contact contour span and a downward projection ray. The fixed sheet direction is checked against the actual contact surface but is never rotated by it. When moving a region-center target upward to place the contact top, either a geometric wall end or a continuing unpainted wall clamps it to the highest reachable painted point. Passing blue-group filtering and producing a green segment does not guarantee a line candidate: if the line target fails candidate resolution, its usable point alternative is selected. This downstream fallback is separate from the blue-group common-width decision. Point fallback is confined to candidate selection before fin-intent conversion. Failure or disappearance of a resolved line intent never creates a point replacement or an end extension.

Painted Shape detects facet adjacencies turning by at least 45 degrees. Connected sharp-edge chains shorter than the feature scale are treated as mesh-scale detail; longer chains become normal-propagation barriers and possible intent edges. A facet's macro normal is the area-weighted Gaussian average inside the same geodesic radius without crossing those barriers. A finite crease does not have to split the whole painted island: averaging may pass around its endpoint only within that radius. Adjacent candidate runs whose macro normals differ by more than 45 degrees remain separate. The macro-patch average supplies one slope and validation frame to every ordinary fitted edge from that patch; each actual contact retains its own local contour position and XY wall direction for clearance and contact geometry. Painted Shape contact bands do not apply a separate local direction-angle cutoff to accepted intents. A crease intent uses the two adjacent macro normals for its starting plane, requires only that the selected local face point outward into that plane, and moves its contact center inward from the contour endpoint far enough to contain the complete bead. A target smaller than `(contact length + support line width) * contact height` is ignored.

All candidate selectors define seed XY at the layer's `slice_z`, matching the height of `layer.lslices` and final wall lookup. Simple retains the individual-facet side threshold and local non-upward contact rule. Painted Shape admits any facet with a nonzero XY cut and applies its crease-aware macro-side rule instead. Both clip complete painted cuts to the sliced contour before selecting a site. Painting confined to the highest printed layer can therefore supply a candidate even when a cut at the physical layer top would collapse against the model's top edge.

**Printed-wall contact boundary:** All styles, point/line inputs and ordinary/crease contacts resolve their actual contact with the same wall-coverage query. Ordinary contacts first determine their source axis, then query from A1 towards the source. Crease lines and crease-point highest bands instead query from their source corner Q along its inward horizontal normal -n; after finding H, they resolve the contact axis from H toward A1 before building the body and its shared attachment. Both queries intersect the physical coverage of the current layer's generated perimeter extrusions. Each segment is a round-ended capsule with radius half its actual `ExtrusionPath::width`, including widths produced by Arachne. Nested perimeter collections, loops and multipaths are traversed, including thin-wall/gap-fill paths held in those collections; force-no-extrusion paths are excluded. Infill is not a contact target. A per-run, lazy per-layer AABB index limits capsule tests to the query ray. The smallest capsule entry is the first boundary of the coverage union, without constructing or Boolean-unioning full-layer coverage polygons. The capsule intersection primitive is shared with infill through `Geometry`.

A query origin already inside or on wall coverage is itself the contact: A1 for ordinary queries, Q for crease-source queries. If H coincides with A1, the existing axis direction is retained rather than normalizing a zero vector. No hit means no actual contact, with no nominal-contour fallback. Source-model anchors remain separate for paint eligibility, surface queries and axis provenance; physical hits do not rewrite source heights, normals or paint measurements. Contact fronts retain the half-support-bead placement along their front normal: the resolved axis for ordinary contacts, the source section normal for crease contacts. Actual contact bridges are not recut against the nominal model outline, which may extend beyond the printed corner. Body gap clipping, lower-transition clearance and contact continuation retain their separate rules. Coverage is the planar swept-width model of the generated wall paths, not a prediction of molten extrusion deformation.

Fin support generation requires completed perimeters, including support preview. Perimeter invalidation also invalidates Fin support so changed wall placement or widths cannot leave stale contacts. Other support types do not gain this dependency. Debug-level `fin-wall-contact` records identify the source anchor, A1, actual query origin and inward direction, resolved contact axis, layer, hit status and distance from the query origin; distance zero is valid already-covered contact. Failure snapshots distinguish the source-model anchor from the actual printed-wall point.

**Required contact-authorization invariant:** Simple and Simulated require direct paint at the seed and every lower periodic contact. Painted Shape uses paint to create its intent graph and select each seed, then validates seed and lower bands against printed wall coverage, the blocker mask, the applicable bead-span rule and allowed contact direction without requiring the brush mask to cover the bead. Paint therefore cannot create a Painted Shape seed on unrelated geometry, but it does not clip the contacts after the intent has been accepted. The seed's mathematical height is its highest relevant section, so geometry above that section is ignored.

All styles share candidate realization, contact authorization and final toolpath storage, but Painted Shape constructs its geometry in printable-center space. `select_simple_fin_candidates()` returns a complete candidate batch, while the feedback-driven Simulated planner calls `select_simulated_fin_candidate()` for one candidate at a time. Ordinary Painted Shape fixes its line/point classification during green candidate checks; direct crease inputs use the classification from their two-sided comparison. A point outcome creates only point-fin intent and retains its source island and edge for diagnostics; discarded line endpoints, attachment links and junctions do not participate in subsequent line assembly. A point neither owns downstream lines nor attaches back to a surviving line. Model clearance and contact failures after conversion do not change an intent's type.

Each accepted ordered, nonbranching path remains one finite generation group, whether it has one segment or many. It retains all vertices and shared interior junction references. A bend alone does not connect groups, create a generation root or start a contact schedule. Adjacent node pairs describe topology and height dependencies only; they do not own separate activation heights, contact schedules or clipped bodies. Roots are determined in this line-only graph before fin-intent conversion: a node with outgoing spans and no incoming span is a generation root. Removing a point-classified edge can expose a new root here; layer clipping or geometry rejection cannot create one later. Geometric connectivity and composite membership remain separate from generation ownership. Each root receives one additional finite stem, which alone builds the highest contact band and downward transition. The original outgoing lines become ordinary groups with periodic contacts. Simple and Simulated use `validate_fin_contact()` on source-model anchors to require a complete painted bead. All styles use `validate_wall_fin_contact()` on resolved printed-wall contacts, retaining their applicable span, blocker and contact-geometry checks.

For ordinary green input, fin-intent node Z is the highest model-surface position at that XY not above the node's unique source Z. Direct crease nodes already carry their exact source-surface Z and do not query or reconstruct it from XY. Source measurements remain unchanged; resolved Z belongs to the shared working node. A root without a usable surface height is rejected. Each junction is queried once, and all incident groups read its one result. A junction without a model hit keeps its XY and takes the highest resolved start Z among its incoming directed spans. Dependent missing heights wait for incoming starts to finish resolving; a failed start with no height cannot supply a value. If none supplies a height, the junction is reported as unresolved and incident groups cannot be emitted. Cyclic height dependencies are reported and left unresolved; groups requiring those nodes are omitted without selecting heights by processing order or aborting unrelated resolved groups. Only a free non-root endpoint without a height is clipped once along its final adjacency span against the full model's XY projection, independently of height and without crossing an existing junction; the resulting model intersection supplies its position and Z. This operation does not run again during per-layer clipping. Directions and activation layers are computed after all shared node updates. Input paths must be nonascending in resolved Z from head to tail; equal heights are allowed. Before layer tracing, an increase larger than EPSILON rejects the whole group and emits a debug-level ascending-path diagnostic with its island, source edge, vertex indices and heights. This check does not reorder, reverse, split or repair the path. A valid complete group activates at its head height. Interior vertices and junctions retain their own source and resolved heights, but do not delay any part of the group until a separate activation layer.

At a root's activation layer, the model contour at `slice_z` is offset by `gap + 3 * extrusion line width`. For ordinary input, a new outer root is placed on that boundary along the bisector of the largest angular gap between the original outgoing directions, with source-list order breaking equal gaps. One ordinary outgoing line uses the same formula, giving its opposite direction. Direct crease-line input uses the horizontal-section exterior bisector at its source root instead, and retains the unchanged original root as an interior junction of the same generation group. For ordinary input, the original root stays at its original XY as a junction, and a new finite stem runs from the outer root to that junction. Original lines and interior junctions do not move or rotate. Both stem endpoints share the original resolved root Z and source identity; the outside-model point is not sampled for a new height or normal. The stem length does not count toward top-layer depth. Its contact width and thickness use the existing region lookup at the original junction; outgoing contact widths are not added together. While gap covers the original junction, only the stem can survive there. Original outgoing groups appear only on layers where their start survives gap clipping and connects back to a surviving root.

Each stem owns one outer extension from its new root. Its initial length is top-layer depth; expansion continues with descending `slice_z` until the intent terminates or reaches the bed. It does not freeze at a group endpoint's height or at a ray-tracing stop. The stem retains the greatest existing expansion coefficient among the original outgoing lines, preserving the common-extension maximum, and uses the same outward direction. Finite groups form a composite through their actual shared endpoint or interior junctions; several generation roots can belong to one component.

An ordinary prescribed group no longer searches a fixed A ray to decide its body. Every layer starts from its full activated finite polyline and subtracts that layer's model offset by gap; it does not progressively expose segments according to their vertex heights. A retained fragment must both contain the whole group's original start and connect to a surviving generation root. An interior junction is not a replacement start; even a connection to another valid root cannot rescue a fragment separated from its own group start by a gap cut. Connections are usable only where the actual retained polylines still reach their shared junction; membership is checked against their constituent segments, never the head-to-tail chord. Root extensions receive the same start-preserving path clip. Once an activated intent has no surviving root-connected body on a layer, it terminates there and never reappears below it. Before termination, layers are independently clipped from untrimmed intent rather than inheriting the preceding layer's shortened endpoints.

Ray-generated Painted Shape point fins resolve their model anchor and direction during tracing. Ordinary contacts use the angle-clamped contact axis; crease-point root contacts use their selected corner-to-A1 axis. Ordinary finite paths keep their clipped-endpoint model lookup, while crease paths keep their original 3D source intersections. Geometry preparation intersects each valid axis with printed wall coverage once. Contact bands and downward taper consume that same physical hit and unchanged axis; they do not repeat a nearest-contour search. A layer without a valid source axis or physical hit supplies no replacement contact.

A crease-point root band searches each layer's complete model outer contours at `slice_z`, within an XY radius equal to `support_object_xy_distance` around the original input position. It selects the convex vertex with the greatest positive exterior turn (smallest interior angle); equal turns retain contour/list order. Selection uses actual contour vertices without the Simple selector's smoothing, minimum feature angle or edge-length score. Gap is a search radius here, not an offset of point intent. A missing corner in any root-band layer rejects the point fin with `painted-crease-point-root reject=no-convex-corner`; there is no ordinary-root fallback. The selected corner and ordinary point-body A1 define the contact axis and direct trapezoid, without the ordinary 30-degree clamp or bead-span contour projection. Below the highest band, including lower periodic contacts and their taper sections, tracing uses the ordinary point-fin contact resolver. Point-body clearance, thickness, blockers and final geometry validation remain in force. No original crease path is needed for point contacts. Successful root selections are recorded at debug level with the selected point, turn, layer and search radius.

For ordinary prescribed paths, tracing retains the terminal segment direction, resolves the nearest source-model anchor from A1, and stores that anchor and its axis before analytical cap construction. The gap boundary locates A1 and determines whether a natural endpoint can contact; its normal does not orient the cap. Each layer stores its complete retained polyline. Body strips use their own segment directions and join at bends with the existing rounded-junction geometry; a bend does not receive a model-facing cap. Member-local contact absorption and compound body assembly both retain the bends. Natural endpoints and junctions do not acquire contacts by tracing past their finite limits. A finite endpoint lying on the clearance contour still supplies its contact, including at zero gap; an endpoint away from that contour does not authorize a new contact. When A1 coincides with the model contour, that actual model contour's outward normal supplies the zero-length contact-axis direction, with hole normals oriented out of the solid. Contact bands crossing a retained path's stop are trimmed at that stop while preserving their scheduled top; a band whose top has no body is omitted. Fixed-plane transitions stop when the member has no retained path. Analytical cap construction is shared by ordinary and crease fins. It accepts both signs of the longitudinal-direction/cap-normal dot product; only an absolute denominator at most EPSILON rejects the parallel line intersection. No cap-facing angle rejection is applied. The physical envelope is expanded from the common clipped center geometry only at toolpath generation; it does not impose another per-intent acceptance check. The completed center geometry receives the existing contact-and-transition-exempted model-gap mask afterward; the first path clip does not replace this physical-clearance step.

A component with multiple generation roots may have separate cross-sections above their physical junction. Each center and expanded region must remain valid and hole-free, and each expanded region must pass the existing thickness erosion check. After clearance clipping, every non-bed region must have positive-area overlap with material on the immediately lower layer. These adjacent-layer overlaps must connect the entire component into one physical body; a shared source junction alone is insufficient. Unsupported regions and disconnected stacks reject the complete component. Single-root components retain the one-region-per-layer requirement. Physical layer regions remain an `ExPolygons` collection through bounds, overlap checks and outlining; no secondary region is dropped to fit a single-polygon container.

Each member keeps a component-center record until layer finalization. It retains its source edge, uncropped body-center layers for absorption queries, completed member contours, contact/transition regions, and the owned attachment edge for each materialized layer. Each printable-center cap is quantized once to integer XY and shared by the body and contact producers. Every lower layer forms its own contact trapezoid and clips it against the fixed transition plane. Contact regions remain separate semantic data for contact flags, clearance exemptions and diagnostics; they are not independent polygons left for the compound pass to attach. A compound is assembled only after all members have completed their local boundary joins.

For a composite contact, the component-local body supplies the owned attachment used by contact construction and controls projection absorption and exposure. A sibling branch cannot terminate or shorten that member's transition. Completed member contours are united only with shared-junction additions and root extensions. The component's greatest member thickness is resolved before member geometry, so body and contact attachments agree without a later widened-body copy; each member retains its own contact width and attachment. After line components and independent point intents are prepared, every prepared support center on the same print layer is united. Actual contact triangles/trapezoids and transition regions supply exemptions in the cached round `support_object_xy_distance + extrusion width / 2` model-clearance mask. That mask clips the layer union once. The resulting layer centers go directly to toolpath generation, where they are expanded once by half an extrusion width to record the common physical envelope used for output-region grouping, inner walls and bed fill. No per-contributor geometry is reconstructed after clearance. Region count, holes, thickness and cross-layer connectivity do not trigger a second whole-fin rejection pass. Source contours remain available only for diagnostic attribution and seam-direction selection; transition-retention statistics compare against the common clipped layer. Ordinary and crease point/line bodies all participate in this common clearance pass; only their actual contact and transition sections supply clearance exemptions.

Fin support is an FFF support type for resisting side-to-side motion of a tall, narrow object. It requires support painting to create every seed. As printed height rises, each active Simulated plan reduces remaining directional demand and may extend its seed along a painted wall. Simple and Simulated also require paint at every model contact. Painted Shape treats its painted region as a point, line or branch instruction; accepted point intents may follow the actual model wall to the bed, while line intents use their finite paths and do not trace beyond their endpoints. Every contact layer uses the printed-wall physical boundary and the style's authorization policy; ordinary contacts additionally check samples spanning the complete contact bead. Each layer is a polygon: body thickness is controlled by `fin_support_thickness`, and the neck width along the model surface by `fin_support_contact_width`. Simple and Simulated retain their body and lower-taper clearance; their actual contact bridges are bed-clipped without a second nominal-model cut. Painted Shape first completes the body, first-layer foot, downward transitions and actual contacts, then clips the complete support with the contact-and-transition-exempted model-clearance mask. Its first-layer foot surrounds the expanded bottom fin path, including its line width and end caps, remains inside the bed, and is filled by the ordinary support infill generator.

Body direction A, finite contact axis N and the actual surface normal remain separate quantities. Simple, Simulated and Painted Shape point intents use a fixed sheet plane through the seed and A. Painted Shape finite paths supply the retained local segment and its A1 directly. Ordinary finite paths and ray-generated point fins use their resolved contact axis N as the cap normal. Their physical cap passes through A1 perpendicular to N and intersects the two symmetric body side lines; no preliminary flat end truncates those line extensions. Crease paths retain their own terminal segment's flat cap. In printable-center space, the cap line is shifted from A1 by half an extrusion width along its cap normal. Its intersections with the two continuous symmetric side chains, separated locally by `fin thickness - extrusion width`, are stored as shared integer XY endpoints. The open body perimeter and contact outer arc use those endpoints directly; no closed body or half-plane mask is used to locate the attachment. Ordinary finite axes use the nearest model contour anchor already resolved from A1 during tracing; crease finite axes use the original 3D intent intersection at `slice_z`. Their actual contact points come from printed-wall intersection. Contact bands and taper consume the stored physical hit and unchanged axis without another search. The outward axis points from the source anchor toward A1; it remains valid when the physical hit is A1 itself. Zero-gap and ray-generated point handling retain their existing rules. Required seed-band failure rejects the candidate without changing its point/line classification; an unauthorized periodic band is omitted. Body collision, band validity and connected center-region topology remain validity checks.

Painted facets are sampled at `slice_z` and just inside the physical top and bottom of each layer's vertical extent. The physical limits come from `print_z - object_print_z_min` and layer height; they are independent of `slice_z`, which Z anti-aliasing can move away from the layer center. Every style places seed XY on the `slice_z` section. A Simple or Simulated seed is the terminal painted contact at that mathematical height, so its highest layer checks the middle and bottom sections and ignores the physical top section above the seed. Their periodic bands remain painted across middle, bottom and top. Simple and Simulated sum only sampled normals with `z <= 0`; patches containing only upward-facing samples are rejected. Painted Shape uses the painted samples to select its seed and macro normal, then uses actual contour samples for contact validation. Each contact resolves its outward XY direction independently, so local contour geometry and blockers remain exact even when macro smoothing ignores a tessellated Z ripple.

The first object layer supplies base area `A0`, centroid `C0`, and an XY covariance, subtracting holes. Its outer contours form a convex-hull footprint; each hull edge supplies an outward direction and the original centroid margin to that tipping boundary. For every subsequent layer, slice area and first moments update the centroid `C(h)` of only the material printed so far. The geometric risk is `h / sqrt(A0) * (1 + norm(C(h) - C0) / sqrt(A0))`. Risk at or below 0.8 does not start a fin. Above this threshold, each footprint direction receives demand proportional to `(1 - 0.8 / risk) * original_margin / current_margin`; current margin is clamped at half a support line width to keep the heuristic finite when a centroid crosses the footprint. The current section-centroid offset and covariance growth add signed directional demands. Covariance is measured about the section centroid relative to the first-layer covariance, so translating an unchanged section does not masquerade as expansion. Its directional variance uses a support-bead-sized floor, and positive spread growth is compressed logarithmically so a nearly linear first-layer footprint cannot create unbounded demand. Nearby directions merge within 15 degrees to avoid repeated equivalent requests. The footprint, moments, and directions are model geometry; painting does not move the centroid or change these raw demands. The method is a geometric stabilizing heuristic, not a force or adhesion calculation.

Planning sweeps upward through these printed-prefix states. An active plan has a fixed sheet direction A and fixed sheet plane. Its seed follows the intersection of that plane with the next layer's painted wall; a missing or invalid intersection, an excessive between-layer jump, or an inward retreat that would erase the lower sheet projection closes the plan at its previous valid height. Candidate plans whose tops converge in the same direction stop duplicating future demand, while retaining their separate lower histories. A stopped plan remains in every later layer's support calculation. Planning capacity is proportional to `(seed_top_height / current_height)^2 * thickness / 1.2`, reduced by a nominal contact-neck-distance factor. For every layer and direction u, remaining demand is recomputed from that layer's raw demand minus `capacity * max(0, A dot u)^2` for each existing plan. Support therefore reduces risk without moving the printed material's center of mass. The opposing direction receives no capacity from that plan.

Whenever a layer has remaining demand above 15% of its raw demand, the planner may add another seed on that same layer and immediately recomputes the residual before considering a further seed. It chooses the greatest unmet direction D. For each painted run, A is the direction closest to D that stays within 30 degrees of the run's XY normal; runs more than 60 degrees from D are unavailable. The run supplies a direct A-ray intersection and central/quarter alternatives, all sharing that A. These are geometric sites, not angular bins. Sites are ranked first by alignment with D, then by position: a short curved run favors the D-ray from the printed centroid, while a broad flat face favors the center of its whole sliced contour rather than the center of its painted subset. The selected point must still lie on the painted run. Existing fins facing the same run impose a preferred lateral separation limited by half that run's painted span. The complete printed-layer patch must be painted, unblocked, and have a local averaged normal within 30 degrees of A. The seed's XYZ comes from that site's XY and this layer's physical top Z. The final averaged contact normal validates A but does not rotate it. At most four plans begin on one layer, eight plans may remain active, and 32 may be retained in total; a blocked direction does not prevent another direction from being considered.

The upward sweep stores seed and contact feasibility, without rebuilding polygons or toolpaths on every layer. Once all plans close, the existing downward tracing, band validation, contact projection, body, foot, and overlap checks generate final geometry. When a plan's final top fails geometry or collides with another sheet, earlier valid tops from its upward history are checked. A printable earlier top found by an exponentially bracketed geometry check limits that plan on the next upward sweep, so lower contact history can remain while upper residual demand is solved again. If no earlier top is printable, the plan's original seed is excluded. All failed plans in a pass are collected before another upward sweep; one failed fin no longer restarts the entire sweep immediately. A taller printable fin can supersede a nearby lower fin of the same direction when it has a separate accepted contact band no later than each lower band and within one band period, and its bed reach is no shorter. An outline failure excludes its original seed before replanning. The emitted set contains only plans with validated printable geometry. Contact bands still use their own local averaged normals and remain all-or-none. Their height is not searched or adjusted in this version. All selected geometry is completed before any extrusion paths are emitted.

`fin_support_max_spacing` is displayed as Fin placement distance. Simple uses it as the maximum contour travel distance when subtracting an unavailable range at the selected seed contour location or a lower wall projection location. Each subtraction stays on one island contour at its own layer. Line width and fin thickness do not determine candidate spacing. Simulated uses it as preferred same-facing spacing and to scale the capacity penalty for long contact necks. The calculation uses the whole PrintObject; Simulated does not track connected-component ownership.

`fin_support_expansion_factor` is the dimensionless multiplier `k`, defaulting to 0.05. The seed normal selected by the candidate's surface-normal policy supplies an outward tilt along A: `beta = atan(-normal.z / norm(normal.xy) * max(0, B dot A))`. Lean perpendicular to the sheet does not attenuate its extra reach. Its expansion weight is `w = (1 - clamp(beta / 30 degrees, 0, 1))^2`: vertical surfaces have full weight, 15 degrees outward along A has 25% weight, and outward tilt of 30 degrees or more along A gives a vertical fin outer edge. This 30-degree threshold is a fixed initial heuristic. Each selected seed retains `tan(theta) = k * R * w` independently of remaining directional demand. The weight is calculated once around the seed and does not change between layers. At height `z`, its outer endpoint is `seed + (D_top + (H - z) * k * R * w) * A`. For ray-generated fins, top depth is measured along A from the seed and wall clearance changes only the inner edge. Painted Shape root stems receive the outer extension described above. The first-layer endpoint uses the actual first-layer height. Zero expansion factor gives a vertical outer edge. Model and bed collision checks still determine whether the sheet can be generated. Contact projection angle controls only contact necks, independently of this body expansion.

Contour lookup along body direction A locates each layer's source-model anchor for Simple, Simulated and Painted Shape point intents; actual contact is subsequently resolved on printed wall coverage. Simple and Simulated retain their painted-contact rule. Painted Shape points resolve A1, N and the actual local wall normal without requiring further paint; they have no finite line endpoint inherited from a discarded green line. Their lower inner edges follow the round model-clearance boundary while outer endpoints expand with their retained slope. Painted Shape line intents instead use the prescribed finite groups and two clearance operations described above. `fin_support_depth` controls the initial outward depth. Periodic contact-band top heights are anchored to the build plate and shared by every fin: `bed_z + fin_support_contact_start_height + n * fin_support_contact_spacing`. `fin_support_contact_height` extends each band downward from that top. The default start height is zero, so the first nominal band is below the printable volume and is naturally omitted. Simple and Simulated require a real painted wall across every layer of a band. Painted Shape requires a printed-wall hit, no blocker, its applicable bead-span check and a valid contact axis. The physical hit never rotates that axis to a wall-path normal. The seed has a separate highest contact band ending at its own top layer and must remain geometrically valid. A periodic band whose top section reaches the seed contact or its downward projection is omitted. The completed body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before the plan is emitted. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

Before a Simple or Simulated fin is emitted, its completed printable polygons are checked for positive-area overlap against existing fins on every shared layer above the bed, regardless of seed height or direction. Overlapping candidates are excluded and the upward planner repeats with that original seed unavailable. Painted Shape instead permits different intent edges and brush islands to meet or cross: their completed center areas are united and jointly gap-clipped before the common physical envelope and closed outlines are generated, removing duplicate internal paths. A merged area may contain several disconnected center contours, and each is emitted instead of invalidating the complete layer. The required first loop uses only those completed center contours. Missing contours or first-loop failure are recorded without reconstructing contours by eroding the physical area or retrying individual fins. Optional inner loops use physical-area erosion; exhaustion stops further inner loops and retains the preceding loops without recording a first-loop failure. Merged first-loop failure stops outlining only that layer region and records the failure count, layer heights, region index, center-region count and line width. A geometry-failure SVG shows the merged physical envelope, saved first-loop centers and thickness probe; its source identity is one representative contributing fin, not the ownership of the whole merged region. This failure does not withdraw the contributing fins or previously emitted layers. Every style unites bed feet before printing. Nearby plan checks remain a cheap preliminary filter, not the final overlap test.

The generated paths and their occupied areas are stored in ordinary `SupportLayer` records, so preview and G-code export use the existing support path pipeline. The type does not generate supports under overhangs and does not use the normal support interface or infill patterns.

Geometry uses completion barriers rather than incremental compound merging. Connected finite members resolve their common maximum thickness before constructing boundaries or contacts. Each member constructs two continuous, open symmetric side chains in source-path order. Adjacent inner side lines meet at their intersection; an outer bend uses one circular arc with the shared arc-discretization utility and a 0.005 mm chord tolerance. No segment produces a closed strip or endpoint disk. The root outer end connects the two sides with a flat edge.

The contact cap is perpendicular to the resolved contact axis for ordinary and crease intents alike. Printable-center placement retains the half-bead inset, except at a reached tail junction. The cap-line cut and the model-clearance cut retain their common outer-connected prefix: the first encountered limit owns that side endpoint, and all subsequent segments and arcs are discarded even if they later re-enter the allowed area. Side clearance uses the same model offset by gap plus half the support extrusion width as final layer clipping, rather than the gap-only offset used to locate A1. Only when the finite side has no cap-line intersection may its terminal line extend forward to the cap before clearance clipping; it cannot extend backward across a prior bend. Exact path reversal has no finite inner-side intersection; no fold-repair policy is provided.

The generating member prepends its own gap-clipped root depth before side-chain construction and contact attachment. This extension retains its flat outer end and half-bead inset. Its join to the original intent is an internal vertex of the same continuous boundary, with no transverse cap or independently united root strip. Source intent heights and graph connectivity remain unchanged.

Compound assembly adds only cross-member junction sectors not already owned by a member's internal bend; root depth is already part of its generating member. Shared junction contributions are collected at exact printed-coordinate endpoints without creating new intent-graph connections; when a member already owns an internal bend there, no duplicate sector is emitted. The union resolves physical intersections between these distinct completed contributions. Shared node identities retain connectivity between finite groups, including different roots and interior junctions, without splitting a group into independent generators. Member body snapshots remain available for contact solving and diagnostics; they are never added alongside the completed member contour. Only that completed contour supplies member geometry to output. Transition and actual-contact regions separately provide the model-clearance exemptions. The layer-wide clearance pass follows assembly, and its result directly enters merged outlining. Half-bead expansion records the physical envelope at that output boundary; there is no intervening ownership remapping or per-fin topology/stack rejection. No toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

Every actual Painted Shape contact layer uses its own resolved contact axis and its member-owned attachment. Body caps use the resolved contact axis, and bands and taper consume the same stored hit and shared attachment. The physical hit is the mathematical contact midpoint. Ordinary contact segments are perpendicular to the local query axis; crease contacts use the separately retained horizontal source normal described above. Its outer arc replaces the body's shared cap edge. Each contact validates its applicable span, blocker and geometry rules. Candidate contact-top selection compares source model heights with `slice_z`. `start_z` remains the maximum carried source-sample height; the existing center-anchor alternative and contact-layer selection still apply. Source minimum Z describes the sampled range only and does not set a stopping layer. Neither height is reconstructed by projecting solved green geometry back onto the model. Finite Painted Shape seed and periodic bands are evaluated from their scheduled top downward. Each band must begin with a valid actual contact; a band with no top contact cannot borrow geometry from an earlier band. After a valid contact, losing the model-cut endpoint while retaining the member body carries the last valid contact trapezoid through the remaining band only when the finite line ends at a junction with a downstream line. Downstream membership comes from the resolved directed node order, including a junction inside another continued line; a terminal junction shared only by incoming lines is insufficient. Without a downstream line, the band stops at its last actual contact before the first loss and immediately enters downward tapering. The saved front endpoints and original analytical cap stay fixed. If the saved trapezoid no longer joins the current member body, each saved cap endpoint casts a parallel ray backwards along the local segment direction frozen with that actual contact to the first surviving member-body boundary. A later layer clipping onto another segment does not rotate those rays. An endpoint already inside or on the body stays in place. Both endpoints must reach the body; their pairing is fixed and no side is rotated towards a new cap. Printable-bed clipping and a connected, hole-free completed boundary with the surviving member body are required. The carried section records its actual rear attachment and uses the same boundary-splicing operation as an actual contact; existing positive-area overlap is resolved as region geometry. These layers are materialized support geometry, not actual contact flags or contact sections. An actual contact returning within the band must pass the normal checks and supplies a new saved trapezoid. Blocker, contact-span, axis and geometry failures are not converted into continuations. A lost path or root connection cannot be restored by this mechanism.

Sibling cavity filling groups attached lines by their immutable source island, attachment-host edge and original junction node. Correcting, splitting or detaching a junction does not erase this provenance. Once all members are assembled, each layer unions only the group's completed member contours, which already include their contacts and transitions, excluding the attachment host and other groups. With at least two contributing source children (finite splits of one child count once), bounded holes of that union are filled once; open notches are unchanged. Member sections remain identifiable after compound assembly so the attachment host cannot accidentally close a hole for the attached lines. Fills retain a touching component as their diagnostic source, enter the common layer union, and receive ordinary model-clearance clipping without introducing a contact exemption or restoring graph connections.

**Required model-surface query contract:** The model surface is the actual boundary of the whole object's Boolean result: the union of its positive model parts with negative volumes subtracted. Queries exclude internal faces hidden by overlapping parts and include surfaces created by subtraction, with normals pointing out of the remaining solid. Surface ownership and adjacency refer to this resulting boundary, not to an individual source part or the painted subset. Layer-dependent model-surface and normal queries use the layer's `slice_z`, in a consistent model coordinate frame. Crease-line root preparation samples the unchanged root XYZ; its stored Z is not replaced by a layer height. `print_z` belongs to printable output placement and must not substitute for a model-surface sampling height. Macro-normal estimation and other explicitly defined aggregate calculations retain their own methods. A point strictly inside a face uses that face's outward unit normal. A point on an edge uses the normalized sum of the incident faces' unit normals. A point at a vertex uses the normalized sum of incident unit face normals weighted by each face's corner angle at that vertex. Surface ownership retains all incident faces, including unpainted faces; it does not select one arbitrary face at an edge or vertex. Surface membership uses Orca's existing `EPSILON` tolerance without snapping the query point to a nearby surface; no matching surface invalidates the contact. A non-finite or near-zero sum, or failed normalization, invalidates the contact. When Boolean construction fails, the query uses the un-Booleaned positive model-part meshes: negative volumes are not subtracted and overlap interiors remain present. A successfully computed empty result stays empty. This contract governs operations that require 3D surface queries; source-axis preparation uses sliced contours, while actual contact and transition fronts use printed-wall hits. Crease-line preparation queries the root normal and horizontal section through the same complete-model owner already used for ordinary node-height resolution; it does not construct another per-contact Boolean mesh.

Painted Shape downward support clips independent per-layer contact trapezoids against one fixed inclined plane C. X is the mathematical midpoint of the lowest contact segment, before the half-bead offset. Let n be the unit XY axis from X toward that layer's A1, and t = (-n.y, n.x, 0). Starting with the horizontal plane through X, rotate it downward toward A1 by `90 degrees - fin_support_contact_projection_angle` about the horizontal hinge through X parallel to t. For setting x, the retained half-space is `cos(x) * dot(n, P.xy - X.xy) + sin(x) * (P.z - X.z) >= 0`. The hinge and C stay fixed throughout this band. Tapering starts below the retained band bottom. With a downstream line, the band can retain its scheduled bottom through carried trapezoids; without one, the first lost endpoint moves the bottom to the last actual contact. The bottom section's saved contact midpoint and query axis define C at that bottom layer's `slice_z`; the separate crease-front normal does not rotate C. Carried sections preserve both directions from their last actual contact. Each lower layer with an actual contact forms its local trapezoid from its own A1 and Ncap. Its contact point is the same stored printed-wall hit as the contact band, with the resolved axis retained for points, ordinary finite paths and crease finite paths alike. A finite member without an actual contact at that layer uses the band's last valid trapezoid and the same rear-attachment operation, without searching past its finite endpoint for another contact. The resulting geometry is clipped to C at `slice_z`. A layer whose contact, cap or carried trapezoid cannot be constructed contributes no taper geometry; solving continues on the next lower layer without discarding the other layers or the fin. No convergence layer is selected and no endpoint is interpolated between layers. Abrupt changes of the local contact axis receive no special smoothing or cross-layer distortion checks.

The lower layers are evaluated down to the bed. Entirely cut-away sections emit nothing; sections wholly contained in the member body are marked absorbed. Other retained sections remain identifiable until compound completion and participate in the existing contact-and-transition model-clearance exemption and final layer assembly. A vertical C (setting 0 degrees) retains its fixed XY half-plane at every height; a horizontal C (setting 90 degrees) removes the sections below X. Projection clipping does not reposition the seed, A, Ncap or the fin's outer endpoints. The seed projection remains a temporary mask for suppression of redundant periodic contacts and does not create another contact band.
For Painted Shape, each contact is created directly in printable-center space. Its front edge is the configured contact length, centered half a support extrusion width along the contact-front normal outside the resolved printed-wall hit and perpendicular to that normal; zero contact length produces one nose vertex. Front endpoints connect to the stored integer side endpoints after both cap-line and model-clearance clipping. The shared cap chain is removed from the body and contact contours, and their remaining arcs form one closed polygon before any compound union. There is no guessed rear overlap and no edge-only Boolean union as an attachment method. Taper or bed clipping may shorten the shared chain: the retained endpoints are inserted into that same owned body edge. Membership on the recorded attachment allows only one scaled integer unit for clipping-intersection quantization, not an EPSILON search or snapping to unrelated nearby edges. Carried contacts record their actual rear attachment after extending to the current body. Where a carried section or another transition already has positive-area overlap, region union resolves that real overlap; an unattached edge-only section fails with `contact-boundary` and a geometry snapshot instead of a second attachment route. Semantic contact regions remain separately available for masks and diagnostics. Simple and Simulated retain their existing convex center bridge and short internal overlap. `fin_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. Contact length and `fin_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the resolved outer-wall line width in the seed's print region. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. Ordinary contact authorization samples contact length plus one support extrusion width to include both bead ends. Physical-span samples cast parallel copies of the contact axis from laterally shifted A1 positions; missing wall coverage or displacement greater than one support line width rejects the span. Simple and Simulated additionally require direct paint across their source-model samples, not across the displaced physical hits. Direct crease contacts retain their span-check exemption. The rounded physical envelope can be wider than its contact span.

The first outer toolpath is the contour of the complete printable-center region. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its body-facing edge uses the shared side endpoints after contact-axis and printable-center gap clipping, prepared before the layer-wide model-gap pass. Its front segment has length exactly `contact_width`, including positive values below one extrusion width. On a flat wall, expanding this center region by half a bead width makes the model-facing physical boundary tangent along the same span. Ordinary polygon erosion is never used to recover Painted Shape's first center contour because it would change a short contact span or erase a zero-length nose. Additional loops offset inward from the completed physical area by the configured spacing. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance in scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, Simple and Simulated finalize each fin's polygons and center areas and check topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry supplies no directional capacity to the Simulated planner. Once geometry for all selected fins is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. For Simulated, an outline failure discards the batch's temporary paths and restarts upward planning with that plan's original seed excluded; its capacity is therefore never counted without a printable fin. Only a successful Simulated batch is stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and used to generate bed infill. Simple excludes the failing candidate before emission. Painted Shape uses the merged outlining behavior described above. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Above the bed, generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit or its center contour is absent or invalid; already generated loops are retained. Painted Shape emits all valid contours of a merged region, including split inner contours; Simple and Simulated require their single-contour loop. Thicker fins can therefore have hollow interiors. Simple and Simulated reject a fin if its first contour cannot be generated; Painted Shape records merged first-loop failure as described above. Each fin contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.

The bed layer unites all accepted fin footprints before generating any bed contours or infill. Its outer center area combines the merged footprint's half-width inset with the saved contact centers, preserving narrow fronts and point contacts. Overlapping feet therefore share a perimeter instead of retaining intersecting internal walls. Disconnected regions and holes retain separate closed contours; further walls use the configured loop count and support flow spacing. Bed contours start at their vertex with the greatest clearance from the first model layer. Infill is generated once over the merged interior after subtracting space for the emitted walls, using the ordinary support infill generator.


## Contact eligibility and diagnostics

Let `w` be support line width, `t` fin thickness, `c` contact length and `g` model XY clearance. A large painted plane is not sufficient by itself: the fixed sheet plane must intersect the actual wall at each contact height, and the complete bead span must pass the candidate's authorization and geometry validation.

A contact band is published only after all its sections are accepted. Simple, Simulated and ordinary Painted Shape point contacts retain their existing contact validation. Crease-point root bands use corner-derived axes intersecting printed wall coverage; their lower periodic bands use ordinary point-contact validation. Finite Painted Shape bands distinguish actual contacts from valid carried support sections. A missing model-cut endpoint with a downstream line can carry the band when directional continuation succeeds; without a downstream line it ends the band and starts the existing taper. Other validation failures still reject the band, and a continuation that cannot join the surviving body rejects the member. There is no search for a shorter contact height to rescue an invalid band. Path termination still clips the band to the surviving path, and downward tapering never restores discarded intent.

Each blue point is checked separately against its existing previous and next segments. Among incident-to-patch persistent convex crease segments, the probe selects the nearest perpendicular foot inside a finite projected segment; exterior feet are not clamped, exact ties retain source order, and lateral distance is recorded without rejection. The crease chain retains the existing greater-than-45-degree adjacency and component feature-scale filter. Probing and direct output have no additional crease-intent length/area thresholds. From the foot, the blue segment selects the traversal direction along the crease. The probe walks half that point's measured full painted width in chart-space arc length and normalizes the sum of segment directions weighted by traversed length, including a partial last segment. A crease end truncates the window; non-convex and patch boundaries also stop it. Zero-width, empty/cancelling windows and unavailable traversal directions are reported rather than assigned a direction. Only the angle between the blue segment and this average is compared with 15 degrees; consecutive blue segments are not compared with one another. Each measurement is written to SVG comments and debug-level `painted-crease-probe` records. Info-level `painted-crease-probes` records contain counts, projection degeneracies and the SVG path/open result. `both_sides_in_patch` counts probes on creases whose adjacent faces still share one patch; this is not a successful two-sided match. This single-chart selection does not extend crease cuts or decide two-sided symmetry. Its SVG is a source snapshot before consumption and remains available independently of downstream green or fin success.

Single-patch crease selection uses original source vertices to collect every incident blue segment at a junction, including branches stored in separate blue paths. Incident directions are ranked by their angle to the sampled crease direction, not by lateral distance or the angle between two blue segments. The smallest and second-smallest angles are the only eligible junction branches; equal angles retain source-list order and unavailable angles sort last. Both must pass the existing inclusive 15-degree check for the junction point to survive. Other incident branches do not contribute to that junction's selection or replace a failed member of the pair. Ordinary points initially survive when at least one incident direction passes at that point. Rejecting a junction does not recursively remove other samples. Original branch connections are not carried into the selected-point chain. These decisions select auxiliary source points for the two-sided comparison. Ordinary blue paths remain unchanged until the comparison consumes participating samples.

A rejected junction receives one local recheck using the same two originally ranked branches. If their distinct outer endpoints are both ordinary points, a temporary chord bypasses the junction. At each endpoint, the existing probe uses that endpoint's original half full width to recompute the crease's length-weighted average direction toward the other endpoint. Both chord angles must independently pass the inclusive 15-degree check and refer to the junction's same source crease; angles are not averaged. Success retains both outer points, even if their original junction-facing directions failed, while the junction remains excluded. Failure leaves the original selection unchanged and does not try a third branch. Originally accepted junctions remain unchanged. Adjacent junctions are not recursively bypassed. Rechecks use frozen source geometry and ranking, without changing widths, surface positions, or the ordinary blue graph.

The fourth probe SVG panel shows accepted recheck chords in magenta and rejected chords in red, with `RECHECK` identifiers, both endpoint angles, cyan sampling windows and orange average directions. Each source chart obtains contiguous painted crease spans from its projected painted area and incident convex source-crease edges, independently of blue-point selection. Boundary crossings use the existing polygon clipper, and interval containment includes the painted boundary with the existing numerical tolerance, so a crease coincident with the polygon boundary is retained regardless of open-path clipping orientation. Disconnected painted spans remain separate. Its original local full painted width is the inward trim length along the 2D crease path. The two ends have independent widths, without path-wide averaging or a half-width factor. The resulting segment fractions map each trimmed endpoint back onto the original 3D crease. This crease trimming is independent of the ordinary blue skeleton's own cap trimming and does not shorten the auxiliary blue chain.

Source snapshots are collected across patches by persistent crease ID. A pair requires overlapping untrimmed painted crease spans from two distinct patches whose adjacent source faces lie on opposite sides of that crease. Face-slot ordering may change along the crease; chart identity and color remain stable. A patch containing both adjacent faces is marked `SIDES NOT SEPARATED`, and that segment supplies no two-sided pairing. Multiple eligible chart/span pairs are reported separately without selecting a semantic winner. For a line pair, the two trimmed starts are averaged in source 3D crease arc length, as are the two trimmed ends. This is neither a coordinate midpoint off the crease nor an intersection of the trimmed spans. The fused range retains every original crease bend.

The blue-stage `blue_point_fallback` decision is carried explicitly with each chart. If either paired side has this point classification, the result is a point candidate at the path-length midpoint of their common untrimmed painted crease range; selected blue points, cap widths and section comparisons are not required. Each fused line range is split at original crease vertices, with one midpoint section per nondegenerate interval perpendicular to that source segment's 3D direction. Sampling follows source mesh tessellation, not print layers or a new fixed spacing. Each section intersects both finite selected-point chains in 3D. Distinct intersections are retained; a shared-vertex hit within `EPSILON` is counted once. A segment lying within `EPSILON` of the section plane is reported as coplanar, rather than choosing a point along it. A unique intersection supplies its Euclidean distance from the crease section origin. Only two unique intersections supply the absolute difference between side distances. Missing, multiple and coplanar cases are marked explicitly, without nearest-hit selection, interpolation or fallback; distance curves break at unavailable measurements and uncovered intervals. All discrete hits and coplanar segments are recorded in SVG comments and debug logs, and ambiguous geometry is shown in red. A section is accepted only when both sides have unique intersections and `abs(d0 - d1) <= (d0 + d1) / 2`. The comparison has no absolute-distance floor and does not divide by the mean; two zero distances satisfy the inequality. Missing, multiple or coplanar measurements fail the section. Consecutive accepted sections retain their complete original crease intervals and intervening source bends as one independent line intent. Failed sections and uncovered intervals split intents, without reconnecting across them. A single isolated accepted section produces one point intent at its midpoint instead of retaining its interval. Two-sided comparison and intent construction are separate from SVG rendering.

Section Z uses slicer float precision. These diagnostics describe root preparation only; there are no per-vertex offset directions or displacement comparison rays. `painted-crease-direct` identifies independent generation ownership and source root Z. `painted-crease-consumption` reports consumed sample counts, remaining ordinary paths, discarded isolated residual samples and whether a chart still supplies an ordinary point. All crease selection, comparison, consumption and input construction run independently of logging level; SVG rendering is enabled at the existing diagnostic logging level. `painted-crease-layer-contact` records identify the source crease and layer when tracing cannot supply its anchor or normal: `no-section-front-normal` records a missing or ambiguous horizontal direction with section-query details, and `no-unique-intent-intersection` identifies a layer without a usable point. Resolved wall hits and axes are recorded by `fin-wall-contact` and `crease-root-layer`. A crease path including its stem is one member (`kind=crease-path`), retaining its crease/pair/part identity. `painted-shape-member` info records attribute trace and geometry acceptance, retained path/wall layer counts and existing rejection summaries to each island/edge and member kind; crease members additionally carry crease/pair/part IDs. Successful geometry can still report rejected periodic bands, and member acceptance does not imply successful component assembly or merged toolpath output.


Geometry failures use the common `export_fin_geometry_failure` snapshot writer. It reuses the SVG renderer and receives actual intermediate polygons, paths and points; it does not rerun solving, repair geometry or change rejection decisions. Snapshots are written on failure independently of the normal diagnostic verbosity, to `SVG/fin-support/geometry-failure-<timestamp>-<sequence>.svg`; `geometry-failure-dump` records the stage, reason, object, island/edge, crease identity when present, layer, slice Z, print Z, file path and open result. Timestamp plus an atomic sequence separates repeated and concurrent failures. An unknown layer is explicitly recorded as -1 instead of choosing a guessed slice. Member snapshots include completed contours and the recorded attachment edges so an unclosed contact can be traced before compound assembly.

Each snapshot contains an overlay and equal-scale individual panels with component labels and region/hole counts. Available data includes the model section, full input path projection, gap-clipped path, A1, source-model axis anchor, actual printed-wall contact point, separate unit contact-axis and contact-front-normal rays, complete member axes including root depth, the owned open member boundary, shared junction additions, each source member's contact or materialized extension with its band range, gap masks, clearance exemptions, centers before/after the final cut, physical envelope and the first-loop thickness probe. Empty supplied sets remain visible as empty panels; unavailable stages are omitted. SVG comments retain original integer XY coordinates for every contour, hole, path and point, plus the input XYZ path in millimeters and geometry parameters. Panel translations affect only display coordinates. Independent source/extension panels preserve ownership even when the combined result is disconnected.

Automatic entry points cover empty Painted Shape member bodies (once per source), fatal contact-geometry construction, failed owned-boundary attachment (`contact-boundary`), missing highest contact, incomplete geometry extensions, component merge/assembly failures, empty common clearance output, and failed merged or standalone outline generation. Simple/Simulated also retain snapshots for their per-fin topology and thickness rejection. Partial snapshots reflect only data available at the failing stage. The final clearance pass also records `contact-rear-gap-cut` once per source when it removes body material within half a support bead of an actual contact's owned rear edge. This snapshot compares member body/contact geometry, the nearby removed body and the final mask/output; it is diagnostic evidence, not a rejection or an automatic clearance exemption. Expected candidate filtering, skipped periodic contact bands and skipped taper layers do not each produce a file. Legacy finalization snapshots precede whole-fin rejection. Painted Shape output snapshots describe the failing merged layer region and do not imply whole-fin rejection.

Crease contact diagnostics distinguish accepted candidates from emitted toolpaths: `emitted_top_z` is the highest print Z with actual support extrusion entities. `crease-root-band` reports source top Z, candidate slice/print heights, configured contact height, and planned/retained half-open layer ranges. `crease-root-layer` records each planned root layer's path availability, wall hit, accepted contact, source point, physical hit, resolved contact axis, front normal and sideways displacement relative to the source bisector. At the existing SVG diagnostic level, `crease-root-probe` snapshots show each planned root layer after member assembly, and `crease-taper-probe` snapshots show nonempty taper sections before/after plane and bed clipping, their attachment and current query body. Taper probes identify current-layer versus carried trapezoids and record the fixed plane's origin, axis and band-bottom height. They reuse the geometry snapshot writer and its filenames even for valid geometry; these probes do not reject or repair sections.

`contact-turn` values are angles in degrees. `contact-span` values identify section 0 (middle), 1 (bottom), or 2 (top). `projection-intrusion` is the inner-edge increase in mm, compared with `w/2`; depth and contour values are mm, while `wall-facing` is a dot product. For Simple/Simulated legacy finalization, `topology` uses a bit mask: 1 means multiple or missing physical areas, 2 physical holes, 4 multiple or missing center regions, 8 center holes, and 16 an invalid center contour. `no-direction` records requested D in degrees and uses Z=-1 because no layer was selected. `contact-contour` value -1 means no intersection in the sampled span; source-paint checks use the model contour and actual contact checks use printed-wall coverage. Nonnegative values report displacement in mm. `top-wall` distance zero may mean no intersection rather than a valid zero-distance hit. Painted Shape reports assembly failures before common clearance and output failures per merged layer region; it does not perform a separate post-clearance stack-connectivity rejection. Initial exits also log insufficient layers/raft, empty footprint, negligible base area or peak risk at most 0.8. GUI logging defaults to info; a higher severity suppresses these diagnostics.


Each directional attempt logs requested D, resolved A when available, and its own rejection summary before the object-wide aggregate. `seed-ray-miss` counts eligible layers with painted cuts but no forward intersection on the fixed A ray; its value is the cut count. `seed-low-risk` counts painted layers excluded by the 0.8 risk threshold. These layer counts explain a small number of actual seed-validation attempts despite a large painted region.

A failed direct-paint lookup receives a second diagnostic-only scan, using the same facing, parallel and endpoint tolerances. `paint-empty` means the requested section has no authorized painted cuts. `paint-facing` means none face the lookup ray. `paint-parallel` means every facing cut is parallel to that ray. `paint-distance` reports the nearest in-segment ray intersection in mm, to compare with support line width. `paint-endpoint` reports the minimum endpoint overrun in mm when intersections lie outside the painted segments. These details apply both to middle-section lookup and full-span validation; the corresponding `contact-span` section index identifies vertical sampling failures. This second scan does not run for successful lookups and does not relax any condition.
