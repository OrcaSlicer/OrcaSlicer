# Fin support

Fin support has three styles. **Simple** preprocesses painted side cuts into intervals on the actual sliced outer contours. The paint grants permission but never supplies candidate coordinates; intervals on the same model edge are merged. Consecutive painted edges on one island form a contact line while their turn stays within 15 degrees and the straight contact remains within half a support line width of that line. Candidate centers need enough room from the ends of the joined line for the complete contact bead, including its line width. On each highest available layer it ranks sharp convex corners and long straight-edge midpoints derived from the complete sliced model contour after scale-dependent simplification. A remaining interval midpoint is the fallback. Every chosen site subtracts an unavailable range at its own contour location and along its downward wall projection. If a lower section has no new wall intersection, the last wall location snaps to a same-facing contour within one support line width; that snapped location continues to the next section. The configured placement distance limits travel along the connected island contour at each location. Walking stops at a back-facing edge, so nearby edges across a fold or on another island remain available. Candidate selection exhausts the region before constructing fins. Each selected site becomes a standard fin candidate; tracing, periodic contact validation, projection, body geometry, finalization and toolpath generation then use the same pipeline as the other styles. Risk is fixed to one and only the existing surface-tilt falloff scales body expansion. **Simulated** uses the printed-prefix planner described below and remains the default.

**Painted shape** first interprets each connected painted island as a scale-aware intent graph. Island connectivity groups one continuous brush gesture; it does not limit the island to one fin. Painted facets connected by an edge, subdivision vertex or exactly coincident mesh vertex belong to one island. Components separated by less than half the printable feature scale are also joined unless their average normals oppose by more than 120 degrees; a sub-contact-width crack cannot describe two independent contacts, while nearby opposite walls remain separate. Every nondegenerate painted facet contributes its transformed area and first moment, including horizontal and locally upward-facing facets. Persistent creases partition the island into macro patches; each patch is projected into its own surface-aligned chart. Gaps within the island-join distance are closed in that chart with a half-distance radius before `Geometry::MedialAxis::build()` extracts its unextended skeleton. Its minimum-width argument remains zero so a valid narrow stroke is not discarded before its topology is known. The returned `ThickPolyline` segments retain their local full painted widths and original junction endpoints.

The intent extractor rebuilds coarse connectivity from those returned polylines. Every consecutive point pair becomes an edge between exact scaled-integer vertices. Each connected graph obtains a global axis from two weighted farthest-point searches. This route remains protected during first-pass removal of side branches shorter than the larger of the physical feature scale and their length-weighted mean full painted width. Protection applies to this pruning pass only; it does not exempt a later blue group from its common-width threshold. There is no preliminary compactness classifier or second-pass branch voting.

Path preparation retains the existing connectivity recovery: degree-two vertices join automatically, while junction directions are measured over one painted-width/physical-feature scale. A continuation within 30 degrees is paired; one between 30 and 60 degrees is paired only when its opposing-direction score exceeds the next pair by at least 0.1. The last two unpaired incident edges are paired. Open fragments without a shared vertex may join when their endpoint tangents continue within 30 degrees, their gap is at most 1.5 times the larger of the physical feature scale and their mean endpoint width, and the complete connector remains inside the same painted area. Closed prepared paths are excluded from line construction and leave the existing point alternative available.

Paths beginning or ending at degree-one vertices have their brush caps trimmed inward by half the path's length-weighted painted width, selecting a remaining source-sample interval. A junction at a path endpoint is not cap-trimmed. The remaining blue path is coarsened at one full painted width: endpoints stay fixed while sub-width edges remove the interior vertex with the smallest accumulated chord error, provided the replacement remains inside paint. Junction samples still in that interval and one direction witness about one local painted width away along each incident path cannot be removed. Source order, original widths, measurement sources and surface data survive this preparation.

The coarsened blue edges are grouped in the surface-aligned two-dimensional chart, before surface-Z orientation and green fitting. A group follows exact shared source vertices while each successive turn is at most 15 degrees. Among available continuations, the smallest turn wins; exact ties preserve source-list order. Edges belong to one group only. Unassigned edges seed groups in source-list order, extending first toward the seed's second endpoint and then outward from its first endpoint so an interior seed does not split a smooth run. The angle is local, not relative to a fixed overall direction. Group length is the sum of chart-space edge lengths, with no additional smoothing, chord-length substitution or cumulative-turn restriction. Small successive turns can therefore accumulate into a winding path; this filter does not prove that every retained group is a central axis.

For each chart area, the strictly longest blue group supplies one common threshold W; equal-length groups retain list order. W is that group's length-weighted mean full painted width, integrating the mean of each edge's two endpoint widths over its chart-space length. If the longest group is shorter than W, all blue groups are discarded for the existing point alternative. Otherwise every group of length at least W is width-qualified and survives. Short groups also survive when they lie on an existing connection between distinct width-qualified groups, including chains of short groups. The qualifying set is frozen before connector recovery; rescued short groups never become new qualifying anchors. Connections use exact shared source vertices, including junctions inside groups, rather than XY proximity. A short spur touching two qualifying groups at their single shared junction is not a connector. Neither isolated short components nor dangling branches or cycles attached at only one junction qualify. Existing alternative routes between qualifying groups are retained without selecting a new shortest route. There is no per-group width threshold, feature-scale floor, extra cap subtraction or protected-axis exemption. Removing a group splits its source paths at discarded edges; remaining samples are never connected across the resulting gaps. The threshold does not alter any original width or Q measurement. A nonempty input always retains its longest group unless it takes the point alternative. When no line remains in the intent, the existing point location uses the accumulated surface-path length moment when available, otherwise the painted-region center.

Retained source paths split at source junctions, with the junction included in every adjacent span. Group filtering does not move junctions or create connections at projected intersections. At the end of blue processing, every retained edge is directed from higher original surface Z to lower Z; equal-Z endpoints follow their stable source-list order. Paths split where this direction changes. A node with outgoing edges and no incoming edge is a root, including a local maximum inside a path. Equal-Z directed edges count as incoming. Root identity and junction connectivity are independent, so a root can also be a junction. There is no component-wide highest-point fallback or later reversal from one global root.

The ordinary intent pipeline is painted area → orange medial axis → dark-blue samples and width measurements → original 3D surface → world XY → final green graph → fin checks. `Geometry::MedialAxis` optionally returns one measurement for every `ThickPolyline::width` entry: the actual contour segment or endpoint source, its identity and the closest point Q in scaled chart coordinates. Segment sources use the nearest point on that finite segment; vertex sources use the vertex. The existing side choice is preserved. Whenever shared samples retain the smaller full width, they retain its matching Q and source. No opposite-side averaging or side reselection is used. The chart point P, original width, source identity, mapped surface position and Z, and mapped Q all travel with retained blue samples. P and Q use the same surface mapping and world-XY projection. Fitting uses `h_i = max(norm(Q'_i - P'_i), EPSILON)`, where `EPSILON = 0.0001 mm` is only a numerical lower bound for the allowed half-width. It does not alter the original measurement. There is no perpendicular width projection or elliptical error model. Samples coincident in XY retain their independent surface heights, order and sources.

Ordinary fits, free fits and branch comparisons use the same XY arc-length weights: `w_i = (norm(P'_i - P'_(i-1)) + norm(P'_(i+1) - P'_i)) / 2`, counting only adjacent edges inside the participating range. A fixed-start orthogonal fit passes through its fixed anchor; a free fit passes through the weighted centroid. The anchor is a constraint, not an additional sample. For unit direction d and line origin O, every participating point is checked with `e_i = norm((P'_i - O) - dot(P'_i - O, d) * d)` and `E = max(e_i / h_i)`. Acceptance requires `E <= 1`, including samples of zero weight. A single sample with a fixed anchor initially uses the anchor-to-sample direction. A free first segment waits for a distinct XY position to determine its direction. If the entire group coincides in XY, it remains a degenerate planar segment with all original surface data and heights and reaches the existing downstream macro-plane handling.

Ordinary growth adds the next unaccepted blue sample, refits all participating samples and projects the newly added sample onto the line as the candidate tail. An accepted candidate saves the complete state. A failing candidate restores the previous accepted state and confirms that segment. The next segment is fixed at its predecessor's confirmed tail and retries the rejected sample. Accepted samples do not participate in the next fit, and the generated tail does not become a blue sample. The predecessor's original surface endpoint is retained separately as boundary metadata for the represented surface interval. There is no green minimum-length filter, recursive whole-range splitting, adjacent-fit merging or post-fit segment absorption. Fine-grained skeleton cleanup belongs to orange and dark blue.

Roots start green solving in source-list order. Each junction waits for all incoming blue paths before choosing continuations; arrival timing does not select the main line. Every incoming accepted state is compared with every outgoing blue path. Each pair adds up to two subsequent blue samples, excluding the starting junction. The next junction or path end stops the window and is itself included. All pairs use the smallest available count. Each pair is fitted and checked once after its entire window, using only that incoming state's samples and the selected outgoing window.

Among compliant pairs, repeatedly choose the smallest E, using each incoming and outgoing path at most once, up to two continuations per source junction. Equal scores for distinct eligible choices preserve source-list order. If multiple incoming paths score within EPSILON of the best compliant E for the same outgoing path, that outgoing path loses continuation eligibility; it remains available as a separately solved child and may reconnect geometrically. Each winner carries its full accepted state and samples into further growth. A continuation remains one straight green segment across the junction; adding an interior junction does not split or refit it. Ordinary later growth may still exceed the fit tolerance and confirm that segment under the usual rollback rule.

Unselected incoming paths confirm their accepted states and register their last actual green segments for tail attachment. Unselected outgoing paths grow freely in source order, leaving the main-facing endpoint of their first actual green segment open. Once that first segment is confirmed, subsequent segments use fixed starts. Grandchildren use the same competition and forward-growth rules. If no continuation is selected, the source-list-first incoming segment's confirmed tail supplies the common junction. Paths sharing a source root use its fixed start. Every outgoing first segment of a source root retains that root even if its tail merges elsewhere.

Each source junction records its final main segments, each outgoing child's nearest actual green segment and each unselected upstream tail. Attachment waits until these segments are solved. An internal junction is resolved after its main segments' external endpoint attachments, using committed segment identities rather than whole source paths. A confirmed stopped tail or fixed root start does not depend on the opposite endpoint. Reconnection moves only the registered head or tail, preserving its other endpoint, and may rotate, extend or truncate the whole segment. It neither refits nor checks the residual again. If actual committed segments create a cycle of endpoint dependencies, cancel the endpoint attachments at source junctions in that cycle before applying any of them, preserving the fitted segments. Junctions outside the cycle still resolve normally. A valid crossing between two continued mains may still be represented without moving either endpoint; it waits for any remaining external attachments.

When two continued finite main segments have a unique intersection, that point is their shared internal junction. Both segments remain whole, and all pending children and upstream tails at that source junction attach directly to the crossing without averaging it with their own intersections. Otherwise the main segments remain separate. Each pending segment casts a ray from its opposite endpoint toward the endpoint being attached and chooses the first finite main segment it hits; equal hit distances preserve main source-list order. Parallel, directionless, backward and out-of-range intersections are unusable. Attachments are grouped by the chosen main, and each group retains the arithmetic-mean collapse to one junction on that main. A mean outside the finite main disconnects its contributors; a ray with no usable hit disconnects individually. There is no averaging across separate mains. Connected members share an actual XY node while retaining their original blue junction and root identities. Disconnected members retain their solved geometry and independent ownership. Foldback detection is intentionally absent.

The retained green graph is passed directly to fin checks. Planar endpoints, directions, actual junction nodes, per-junction main hosts and source-root identity are stored separately from original surface samples, source height extrema and the optional XY extension limit. Patch assembly remaps indices while preserving the solved graph; it neither infers new connections from endpoint proximity nor absorbs nearby segments. Completed green geometry and junctions never pass through `to_surface`, and no 3D interpolation repairs a planar connection. A planar junction is not a model contact. True contacts still come from subsequent model-surface or slice-contour queries. Near-vertical macro-plane handling and the candidate-stage normal correction remain downstream; a small projected half-width does not trigger an early alternative.

A chart whose surface normals cancel or whose projected triangles overlap by more than 30% is unreliable for ordinary medial-axis strokes, but may still retain explicit persistent-crease edges. A persistent painted crease becomes an explicit graph edge when its length is at least twice the physical feature scale and its two-sided scale area is at least 20% of the island area. Represented length, average painted width, surface center and contact-height bounds derive from source surface data, not from a generated XY segment or junction. No retained edge gives point intent, one gives line intent, and multiple edges give branched intent. The feature scale used by skeleton cleanup is `max(3 * support line width, contact length + support line width)`.

A compact intent uses the contracted center, falling back to the painted area centroid. Every graph edge carries its fitted line description and a point alternative at its own center. Ordinary fitted edges from one macro patch share that patch's crease-aware, area-weighted macro normal; only a persistent crease keeps its crease-specific normal. Each edge's endpoint chord supplies its own turn within this common projection frame, and its vertical plane is anchored at the final green segment's first XY endpoint. The sheet direction therefore comes from the painted macro region rather than the normal at a later selected contact point. Direction ambiguity is measured entirely in the XY ground-plane projection: the unnormalized endpoint chord is oriented toward the projected macro normal, and its endpoint is compared with the equal-radius endpoint on that macro direction. A separation no greater than one full local painted width is drawing-scale ambiguity, so the edge uses the common macro direction. Correction rotates the finite XY segment about that fixed origin while preserving its projected length; the source Z limits remain separate contact-height semantics. All corrected axes are frozen before junction updates. An affected dependent endpoint follows its fixed A axis toward the host's finite rotated segment, using its head-or-tail attachment orientation. A missed or backward hit breaks that connection without erasing a source root or another connection at the member's other end. Rotation about a shared root retains that exact pivot even for coincident corrected axes. Different hits split a source junction; coincident hits on the same parent share a resolved junction. These updates do not feed back into the correction decisions. Only after junction resolution is complete does the candidate resolver check that the normalized XY direction stays within `fin_support_painted_shape_line_angle` of the macro direction. The candidate resolver tries the line description when its fit and plane are usable and retains the point alternative with the proposal. This setting defaults to 40 degrees and supplies the direction-angle threshold for ordinary Painted Shape line selection. Once A is accepted, contact-band generation validates actual contact geometry without another fixed 30-degree angle rejection. Geometric failures can still select the point alternative. `fin_support_contact_projection_angle` independently controls downward projection. A segment with a fixed contact top retains its highest source surface sample and explicit start Z as the contact target. Other targets use their length-weighted center and move upward by half the configured contact height.

For a line intent, the fixed vertical intent plane intersects each painted sliced contour directly; the selector does not project the target to the nearest arbitrary painted segment. Point alternatives still use the nearest contour position because a point supplies no plane. Candidate hosts require a usable local macro side, an exact complete-contact contour span and a downward projection ray. The fixed sheet direction is checked against the actual contact surface but is never rotated by it. When moving a region-center target upward to place the contact top, either a geometric wall end or a continuing unpainted wall clamps it to the highest reachable painted point. Passing blue-group filtering and producing a green segment does not guarantee a line candidate: if the line target fails candidate resolution, its usable point alternative is selected. This downstream fallback is separate from the blue-group common-width decision. A line that fails complete geometry is replaced by its already resolved point alternative; the skeleton extractor never emits a fallback candidate itself.

Painted Shape detects facet adjacencies turning by at least 45 degrees. Connected sharp-edge chains shorter than the feature scale are treated as mesh-scale detail; longer chains become normal-propagation barriers and possible intent edges. A facet's macro normal is the area-weighted Gaussian average inside the same geodesic radius without crossing those barriers. A finite crease does not have to split the whole painted island: averaging may pass around its endpoint only within that radius. Adjacent candidate runs whose macro normals differ by more than 45 degrees remain separate. The macro-patch average supplies one slope and validation frame to every ordinary fitted edge from that patch; each actual contact retains its own local contour position and XY wall direction for clearance and contact geometry. Painted Shape contact bands do not apply a separate local direction-angle cutoff to accepted intents. A crease intent uses the two adjacent macro normals for its starting plane, requires only that the selected local face point outward into that plane, and moves its contact center inward from the contour endpoint far enough to contain the complete bead. A target smaller than `(contact length + support line width) * contact height` is ignored.

All candidate selectors define seed XY at the layer's `slice_z`, matching the height of `layer.lslices` and final wall lookup. Simple retains the individual-facet side threshold and local non-upward contact rule. Painted Shape admits any facet with a nonzero XY cut and applies its crease-aware macro-side rule instead. Both clip complete painted cuts to the sliced contour before selecting a site. Painting confined to the highest printed layer can therefore supply a candidate even when a cut at the physical layer top would collapse against the model's top edge.

**Required contact-authorization invariant:** Simple and Simulated require direct paint at the seed and every lower periodic contact. Painted Shape uses paint to create its intent graph and select each seed, then validates seed and lower bands against the actual model contour, blocker mask, complete bead span and allowed contact direction without requiring the brush mask to cover the bead. Paint therefore cannot create a Painted Shape seed on unrelated geometry, but it does not clip the contacts after the intent has been accepted. The seed's mathematical height is its highest relevant section, so geometry above that section is ignored.

All styles share candidate realization, contact authorization and final toolpath storage, but Painted Shape constructs its geometry in printable-center space. `select_simple_fin_candidates()` returns a complete candidate batch, while the feedback-driven Simulated planner calls `select_simulated_fin_candidate()` for one candidate at a time; both produce `FinCandidate`. `select_painted_shape_fin_candidates()` instead returns `FinCandidateProposal`: one preferred result plus an optional point alternative from the same graph edge. Every Painted Shape result, including a point fallback, retains its source island, edge, attachment host and actual-node identities. Line results additionally carry their independent source-root identity and signed outgoing A direction. An optional world-XY point on A limits model-facing extension for either a line or a point candidate. A line supplies its final tail, including a tail merged into a junction. Normal correction rotates that tail with its line and replaces it with the corrected junction when reconnection succeeds. A failed reconnection leaves the rotated free tail as the limit. Head and interior junctions do not stop that member. Point intents and point alternatives do not infer a limit from a discarded line or its source minimum Z; without an explicit limit they continue following the model toward the bed. Parent edges resolve before their children. A non-root line with a valid line host enters geometry without a local seed band. Every source-root outgoing member builds its own highest contact band and downward transition along its own A, including when it has a tail attachment to another line. A point result retains its own seed contact and is absorbed into the same composite fin when an immediately adjacent line survives. A surviving parent line is preferred; when graph rooting placed the point above its line children, the highest direct child line becomes the attachment root instead. Lines that lose their line host require an independently valid local seed; this does not rewrite their blue source-root identity. Simple and Simulated contacts use `validate_fin_contact()` and require the complete sampled bead to remain painted. Painted Shape contacts use `validate_model_fin_contact()` and require the complete bead only on the actual outer contour. Both reject blockers and invalid contact directions. A failed layer cancels its complete contact band.

Painted Shape fixes the line/point outcome while retaining the original graph relationship. Directly adjacent surviving line edges belong to one component. A point edge attaches to a surviving line parent, or reverses its relation onto its highest direct line child when no line parent remains; otherwise it stays independent. This point-only attachment choice preserves an adjacent surviving line without joining distinct line components through a point. Composite membership follows validated shared junction connectivity independently of generation ownership; several roots can belong to one component. Each source root owns one common outer extension. Its participating line members keep their individual highest contact bands and transitions. Every surviving line is prepared once as an identifiable member: it traces its own body axis, resolves contact axes and model-facing center caps, searches each required transition downward, materializes the solved transition upward, and then sets its member-complete state. A non-root dependent line omits its local seed band. Preparation does not apply model clearance, expand to physical bead width or generate toolpaths. After every member is complete, connected members contribute their own analytical A1-to-junction body spans. Their provisional standalone tips do not create additional root extensions. For a source root, the final actual A directions are oriented away from the root and sorted around world XY. The largest circular angular gap supplies its outward bisector; exact gap ties follow the original member list. A single direction uses its opposite. The shared extension starts at the actual root junction and is added only after directions and connections are fixed. It is body geometry, not another model contact, and has a round inner junction and flat outer end. Each member retains its own per-layer expansion and freezes when its model-facing boundary reaches its own XY limit. Coincident contributions to the common extension are united by their actual per-layer reach; no shared stopping height is introduced. Resolved junctions join the fixed member axes, including turns, branches, incoming merges and finite hits inside a main intent segment. Original 3D stroke endpoints do not control compound geometry. Each axis retains its source edge, A, A1 and analytical cap normal. The body-center network uses a half-width of `(greatest body thickness - extrusion width) / 2`, round junctions and a flat root outer end. Every member's completed downward transition and actual contact section are united with that layer's shared body center. Attached point results remain prepared center geometry and join before finalization. Contact widths remain local, and every source-root outgoing line keeps its own seed band. Every attachment verifies that the resolved junction lies on both member axes; it never substitutes a chord between their model-facing cap centers.

A component with multiple generation roots may have separate cross-sections above their physical junction. Each center and expanded region must remain valid and hole-free, and each expanded region must pass the existing thickness erosion check. After clearance clipping, every non-bed region must have positive-area overlap with material on the immediately lower layer. These adjacent-layer overlaps must connect the entire component into one physical body; a shared source junction alone is insufficient. Unsupported regions and disconnected stacks reject the complete component. Single-root components retain the one-region-per-layer requirement. Physical layer regions remain an `ExPolygons` collection through bounds, overlap checks and outlining; no secondary region is dropped to fit a single-polygon container.

Each member keeps a component-center record until layer finalization. The record identifies its source edge and retains its uncropped body center, accepted contact bands, completed contact or plane-clipped transition center regions and completion state. Every lower layer forms its own contact trapezoid and clips it against the band's fixed plane before the completed transition is published. Painted contact polygons retain their analytical contact-line-to-cap shape and are clipped only to the cached printable-bed center at this stage; they are not pre-clipped by a model offset. A compound is assembled only after every member has completed this work.

For a composite contact, the component-local uncropped body supplies the cap used while solving the contact and controls projection absorption and exposure. A sibling branch cannot terminate or shorten that member's plane-clipped transition while the member is incomplete. After all components and point attachments are assembled, every prepared support center on the same print layer is united. That layer's actual contact triangles or trapezoids and plane-clipped transition sections are removed from one cached round `support_object_xy_distance + extrusion width / 2` model-clearance mask. The resulting mask clips the whole layer union in one boolean operation. Ownership is mapped back to the contributing components only after this unique model-gap subtraction; no component performs another model-gap clip. Each resulting center region is expanded by half an extrusion width once.

Fin support is an FFF support type for resisting side-to-side motion of a tall, narrow object. It requires support painting to create every seed. As printed height rises, each active Simulated plan reduces remaining directional demand and may extend its seed along a painted wall. Simple and Simulated also require paint at every model contact. Painted Shape treats its painted region as a point, line or branch instruction; accepted point intents may follow the actual model wall to the bed, while line intents stop wall following at their fitted low endpoint. Every contact layer checks samples spanning the configured contact width against the actual outer contour and the style's authorization policy. Each layer is a polygon: body thickness is controlled by `fin_support_thickness`, and the neck width along the model surface by `fin_support_contact_width`. Simple and Simulated retain their existing body and contact clipping. Painted Shape first completes the body, first-layer foot, downward transitions and actual contacts, then clips the complete support with the contact-and-transition-exempted model-clearance mask. Its first-layer foot surrounds the expanded bottom fin path, including its line width and end caps, remains inside the bed, and is filled by the ordinary support infill generator.

Body direction A, finite contact axis N and the actual surface normal are separate quantities. The fixed sheet plane passes through the seed and A; the seed is its pivot, not the outer tip. At each attached height an A-ray first confirms the actual sliced outer contour. Intersecting that ray with the same layer's round-joined `support_object_xy_distance` offset produces the physical clearance endpoint A1 and its boundary normal. The printable-center cap is the corresponding half-plane shifted from A1 by half an extrusion width along that boundary normal. The body centerline is widened by `(fin thickness - extrusion width) / 2` before this half-plane is applied, so an oblique cap cannot leave the missing corner produced by widening a segment which begins at A1. The seed trace still resolves the nearest point M0 on the same original outer contour and clamps its finite N0 axis toward A at the 30-degree limit when necessary. A miss invalidates the contact instead of inventing a tangential or unbounded connection. The seed contact of a line proposal then falls back to its point proposal; a failed lower periodic contact invalidates only that band. Body collision, band validity and connected center-region topology determine geometric feasibility.

Painted facets are sampled at `slice_z` and just inside the physical top and bottom of each layer's vertical extent. The physical limits come from `print_z - object_print_z_min` and layer height; they are independent of `slice_z`, which Z anti-aliasing can move away from the layer center. Every style places seed XY on the `slice_z` section. A Simple or Simulated seed is the terminal painted contact at that mathematical height, so its highest layer checks the middle and bottom sections and ignores the physical top section above the seed. Their periodic bands remain painted across middle, bottom and top. Simple and Simulated sum only sampled normals with `z <= 0`; patches containing only upward-facing samples are rejected. Painted Shape uses the painted samples to select its seed and macro normal, then uses actual contour samples for contact validation. Each contact resolves its outward XY direction independently, so local contour geometry and blockers remain exact even when macro smoothing ignores a tessellated Z ripple.

The first object layer supplies base area `A0`, centroid `C0`, and an XY covariance, subtracting holes. Its outer contours form a convex-hull footprint; each hull edge supplies an outward direction and the original centroid margin to that tipping boundary. For every subsequent layer, slice area and first moments update the centroid `C(h)` of only the material printed so far. The geometric risk is `h / sqrt(A0) * (1 + norm(C(h) - C0) / sqrt(A0))`. Risk at or below 0.8 does not start a fin. Above this threshold, each footprint direction receives demand proportional to `(1 - 0.8 / risk) * original_margin / current_margin`; current margin is clamped at half a support line width to keep the heuristic finite when a centroid crosses the footprint. The current section-centroid offset and covariance growth add signed directional demands. Covariance is measured about the section centroid relative to the first-layer covariance, so translating an unchanged section does not masquerade as expansion. Its directional variance uses a support-bead-sized floor, and positive spread growth is compressed logarithmically so a nearly linear first-layer footprint cannot create unbounded demand. Nearby directions merge within 15 degrees to avoid repeated equivalent requests. The footprint, moments, and directions are model geometry; painting does not move the centroid or change these raw demands. The method is a geometric stabilizing heuristic, not a force or adhesion calculation.

Planning sweeps upward through these printed-prefix states. An active plan has a fixed sheet direction A and fixed sheet plane. Its seed follows the intersection of that plane with the next layer's painted wall; a missing or invalid intersection, an excessive between-layer jump, or an inward retreat that would erase the lower sheet projection closes the plan at its previous valid height. Candidate plans whose tops converge in the same direction stop duplicating future demand, while retaining their separate lower histories. A stopped plan remains in every later layer's support calculation. Planning capacity is proportional to `(seed_top_height / current_height)^2 * thickness / 1.2`, reduced by a nominal contact-neck-distance factor. For every layer and direction u, remaining demand is recomputed from that layer's raw demand minus `capacity * max(0, A dot u)^2` for each existing plan. Support therefore reduces risk without moving the printed material's center of mass. The opposing direction receives no capacity from that plan.

Whenever a layer has remaining demand above 15% of its raw demand, the planner may add another seed on that same layer and immediately recomputes the residual before considering a further seed. It chooses the greatest unmet direction D. For each painted run, A is the direction closest to D that stays within 30 degrees of the run's XY normal; runs more than 60 degrees from D are unavailable. The run supplies a direct A-ray intersection and central/quarter alternatives, all sharing that A. These are geometric sites, not angular bins. Sites are ranked first by alignment with D, then by position: a short curved run favors the D-ray from the printed centroid, while a broad flat face favors the center of its whole sliced contour rather than the center of its painted subset. The selected point must still lie on the painted run. Existing fins facing the same run impose a preferred lateral separation limited by half that run's painted span. The complete printed-layer patch must be painted, unblocked, and have a local averaged normal within 30 degrees of A. The seed's XYZ comes from that site's XY and this layer's physical top Z. The final averaged contact normal validates A but does not rotate it. At most four plans begin on one layer, eight plans may remain active, and 32 may be retained in total; a blocked direction does not prevent another direction from being considered.

The upward sweep stores seed and contact feasibility, without rebuilding polygons or toolpaths on every layer. Once all plans close, the existing downward tracing, band validation, contact projection, body, foot, and overlap checks generate final geometry. When a plan's final top fails geometry or collides with another sheet, earlier valid tops from its upward history are checked. A printable earlier top found by an exponentially bracketed geometry check limits that plan on the next upward sweep, so lower contact history can remain while upper residual demand is solved again. If no earlier top is printable, the plan's original seed is excluded. All failed plans in a pass are collected before another upward sweep; one failed fin no longer restarts the entire sweep immediately. A taller printable fin can supersede a nearby lower fin of the same direction when it has a separate accepted contact band no later than each lower band and within one band period, and its bed reach is no shorter. An outline failure excludes its original seed before replanning. The emitted set contains only plans with validated printable geometry. Contact bands still use their own local averaged normals and remain all-or-none. Their height is not searched or adjusted in this version. All selected geometry is completed before any extrusion paths are emitted.

`fin_support_max_spacing` is displayed as Fin placement distance. Simple uses it as the maximum contour travel distance when subtracting an unavailable range at the selected seed contour location or a lower wall projection location. Each subtraction stays on one island contour at its own layer. Line width and fin thickness do not determine candidate spacing. Simulated uses it as preferred same-facing spacing and to scale the capacity penalty for long contact necks. The calculation uses the whole PrintObject; Simulated does not track connected-component ownership.

`fin_support_expansion_factor` is the dimensionless multiplier `k`, defaulting to 0.05. The seed normal selected by the candidate's surface-normal policy supplies an outward tilt along A: `beta = atan(-normal.z / norm(normal.xy) * max(0, B dot A))`. Lean perpendicular to the sheet does not attenuate its extra reach. Its expansion weight is `w = (1 - clamp(beta / 30 degrees, 0, 1))^2`: vertical surfaces have full weight, 15 degrees outward along A has 25% weight, and outward tilt of 30 degrees or more along A gives a vertical fin outer edge. This 30-degree threshold is a fixed initial heuristic. Each selected seed retains `tan(theta) = k * R * w` independently of remaining directional demand. The weight is calculated once around the seed and does not change between layers. At height `z`, its outer endpoint is `seed + (D_top + (H - z) * k * R * w) * A`. Top depth is measured along A from the seed during member-local tracing; wall clearance changes only the inner edge. Painted Shape assembly replaces provisional root tips with the common root extension described above after the actual member directions are fixed. The first-layer endpoint uses the actual first-layer height. Zero expansion factor gives a vertical outer edge. Model and bed collision checks still determine whether the sheet can be generated. Contact projection angle controls only contact necks, independently of this body expansion.

Contour lookup along body direction A locates each layer's wall in the fixed sheet plane. Simple and Simulated follow these intersections to the bed with their existing painted-contact rule. Painted Shape point intents and point fallbacks instead resolve A1, N and the actual local wall normal without requiring further paint. Painted Shape queries the outermost exiting intersection of A with the round model-gap offset independently of model contact. The gap intersection supplies A1 while it lies outward of the optional XY limit. Once that intersection reaches or passes the limit toward the model, or there is no gap intersection, a limited candidate clamps A1 to the exact limit and ends wall following and expansion on that layer. The endpoint cap is perpendicular to A. The clamped inner endpoint and that layer's outer endpoint are copied vertically below it; the clamped layer and all lower layers cannot create model contacts. Without a limit, source minimum Z does not stop tracing. A gap boundary outward of the limit takes precedence; no limit grants a clearance exemption. The detached body remains subject to model and bed collision checks. While attached, lower inner edges follow the round model-clearance boundary and lower endpoints extend outward by the expansion slope. `fin_support_depth` controls the top-layer depth. Periodic contact-band top heights are anchored to the build plate and shared by every fin: `bed_z + fin_support_contact_start_height + n * fin_support_contact_spacing`. `fin_support_contact_height` extends each band downward from that top. The default start height is zero, so the first nominal band is below the printable volume and is naturally omitted. Simple and Simulated require a real painted wall across every layer of a band. Painted Shape requires a real model wall, no blocker, the complete contour span and a valid finite contact axis. Painted Shape contacts retain their actual XY wall normal while reusing the seed region's common macro slope. The seed has a separate highest contact band ending at its own top layer and must remain geometrically valid. A periodic band whose top section reaches the seed contact or its downward projection is omitted. The completed body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before the plan is emitted. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

Before a Simple or Simulated fin is emitted, its completed printable polygons are checked for positive-area overlap against existing fins on every shared layer above the bed, regardless of seed height or direction. Overlapping candidates are excluded and the upward planner repeats with that original seed unavailable. Painted Shape instead permits different intent edges from one brush island to meet or cross: their per-layer polygons and center areas are united before closed outlines are generated, removing duplicate internal paths. A merged area may contain several disconnected center contours, and each is emitted instead of invalidating the complete layer. If the merged center contours still cannot produce a printable loop, outlining retries the contributing fins individually and records that fallback; an unrecoverable layer is reported explicitly. Every style unites bed feet before printing. Nearby plan checks remain a cheap preliminary filter, not the final overlap test.

The generated paths and their occupied areas are stored in ordinary `SupportLayer` records, so preview and G-code export use the existing support path pipeline. The type does not generate supports under overhangs and does not use the normal support interface or infill patterns.

Geometry uses completion barriers rather than incremental compound merging. For Painted Shape, each member first builds its analytically capped, not-yet-model-clipped body-center layers. Contact work builds the required seed band, records its downward reach, and processes periodic bands from highest to lowest. Each projection has explicit materializing, complete and invalid states. Affected layers are classified as contact, materialized or absorbed; sections removed entirely by the clipping plane remain inactive. Finalization remains suspended until every required layer is materialized or already contained by that member's body. The compound waits for every member-complete flag, constructs its shared centerline envelope and attaches prepared point centers. Shared node identities connect the exact source members on their fixed A axes, including two roots or a member attached at both ends. Each member retains its own A and model-facing analytical cap. Connectivity does not depend on coincident polygon vertices or on a single parent reference. Endpoint role is separate from cap normal: only junctions receive round joins, while the root outer end remains flat. A reached tail junction also receives an inner round join instead of a model-facing half-bead cap inset; the final model-gap subtraction still clips it. An interior junction does not truncate its host line. Each axial strip is intersected with its own analytical cap half-plane; a child junction still behind that cap contributes no reversed body polygon. A later layer-wide pass adds every completed downward transition and actual contact section from every component. Their union is removed from the model-clearance mask because both the contact trapezoid and its clipped transition necessarily cross that band on the way to the A1 cap. The remaining mask performs the layer's single model-gap subtraction; the result is then mapped back to its owners, expanded outward by half an extrusion width and validated for topology. Adding a child never finalizes and then regenerates an earlier physical fin, and no toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

Every Painted Shape contact layer uses its own contact axis: the shortest XY line from that layer's A1 to the actual sliced model boundary. The model endpoint is the mathematical contact midpoint, and the contact segment is perpendicular to that local axis. It connects to the same layer's analytical Ncap edge. Each contact layer validates its span, blockers, finite axis and allowed turn from the fin plane. No contact midpoint is constrained by the highest contact layer's vertical plane. Simple and Simulated retain their existing per-layer painted contacts.

Candidate contact-top selection compares source model heights with `slice_z`. `start_z` remains the maximum carried source-sample height; the existing center-anchor alternative and contact-layer selection still apply. Source minimum Z describes the sampled range only and does not set a stopping layer. Neither height is reconstructed by projecting solved green geometry back onto the model. Seed and periodic contact bands also select surface samples by `slice_z`; a physical layer-bottom height is converted to the model frame before defining the periodic start height. Fin expansion and fixed-plane clipping use these model sampling heights. Printable support layers retain `print_z`; diagnostic rejection heights identify the corresponding print layer, while X, A1 and geometric drop diagnostics use model sampling heights.

For an XY-limited Painted Shape fin, tracing records the first clamped layer. The layer immediately above it is the inclusive lower limit of model contact. Seed and periodic contact bands are clipped to that observed limit before contour lookup; bands entirely at or below the clamped layer are omitted. A crossing band starts its downward transition at the clipped lowest contact layer, using that layer's X and contact segment. Body axes, cap normals and endpoints still propagate vertically below the stop, but that retained body state does not authorize new model contact there. Lower transition layers still query their own model nearest points to construct temporary trapezoids; only the part retained by the fixed clipping plane contributes support, without creating a new contact band.

Sibling cavity filling groups children by their immutable source island, parent edge and original junction node. Correcting, splitting or detaching a junction does not erase this provenance. Once all members are assembled, each layer unions only the group's own body, contact and transition sections, excluding the parent and other groups. With at least two contributing children, bounded holes of that union are filled once; open notches are unchanged. Member sections remain identifiable after compound assembly so the parent cannot accidentally close a hole for its children. Fills retain component ownership for finalization, do not restore graph connections, and receive the ordinary model-clearance clipping without introducing a contact exemption.

**Required model-surface query contract:** The model surface is the actual boundary of the whole object's Boolean result: the union of its positive model parts with negative volumes subtracted. Queries exclude internal faces hidden by overlapping parts and include surfaces created by subtraction, with normals pointing out of the remaining solid. Surface ownership and adjacency refer to this resulting boundary, not to an individual source part or the painted subset. All Fin Support styles locate model-surface points and sample their normals at the layer's `slice_z`, in a consistent model coordinate frame. `print_z` belongs to printable output placement and must not substitute for a model-surface sampling height. Macro-normal estimation and other explicitly defined aggregate calculations retain their own methods. A point strictly inside a face uses that face's outward unit normal. A point on an edge uses the normalized sum of the incident faces' unit normals. A point at a vertex uses the normalized sum of incident unit face normals weighted by each face's corner angle at that vertex. Surface ownership retains all incident faces, including unpainted faces; it does not select one arbitrary face at an edge or vertex. Surface membership uses Orca's existing `EPSILON` tolerance without snapping the query point to a nearby surface; no matching surface invalidates the contact. A non-finite or near-zero sum, or failed normalization, invalidates the contact. When Boolean construction fails, the query uses the un-Booleaned positive model-part meshes: negative volumes are not subtracted and overlap interiors remain present. A successfully computed empty result stays empty. This contract governs operations that require 3D surface queries; Painted Shape contact and transition geometry uses sliced contours and does not construct a separate Boolean mesh or sample 3D contact normals.

Painted Shape downward support clips independent per-layer contact trapezoids against one fixed inclined plane C. X is the mathematical midpoint of the lowest contact segment, before the half-bead offset. Let n be the unit XY axis from X toward that layer's A1, and t = (-n.y, n.x, 0). Starting with the horizontal plane through X, rotate it downward toward A1 by `90 degrees - fin_support_contact_projection_angle` about the horizontal hinge through X parallel to t. For setting x, the retained half-space is `cos(x) * dot(n, P.xy - X.xy) + sin(x) * (P.z - X.z) >= 0`. The hinge and C stay fixed throughout this band; each lower layer independently finds its own closest model point from its own A1, forms its local contact trapezoid against its own Ncap, and clips that trapezoid to the retained half-space at `slice_z`. No convergence layer is selected and no endpoint is interpolated between layers. Abrupt changes of the local contact axis receive no special smoothing or cross-layer distortion checks.

The lower layers are evaluated down to the bed. Entirely cut-away sections emit nothing; sections wholly contained in the member body are marked absorbed. Other retained sections remain identifiable until compound completion and participate in the existing contact-and-transition model-clearance exemption and final layer assembly. A vertical C (setting 0 degrees) retains its fixed XY half-plane at every height; a horizontal C (setting 90 degrees) removes the sections below X. Projection clipping does not reposition the seed, A, Ncap or the fin's outer endpoints. The seed projection remains a temporary mask for suppression of redundant periodic contacts and does not create another contact band.
For Painted Shape, each contact is created directly in printable-center space. Its model-facing edge is the configured contact length, centered half an extrusion width outside the actual contour and perpendicular to that layer's local contact axis. Zero contact length produces one nose vertex. The analytical A1 half-plane supplies the member's cap endpoints before final model clipping; the nose or front endpoints connect directly to those exact points, producing one triangle or trapezoid without a separately offset contact solid or a guessed rear overlap. The shared cap edge is then removed from both contours and replaced by the body's outer arc and the contact's front arc. The resulting printable center is one polygon by construction rather than two edge-touching polygons left to a boolean union. Simple and Simulated retain their existing convex center bridge and short internal overlap. `fin_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. Contact length and `fin_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the resolved outer-wall line width in the seed's print region. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. Contact authorization samples contact length plus one extrusion width to include both bead ends, so the bead does not escape a wall edge. Direct paint must cover all of those samples. The rounded physical envelope can be wider than its contact span.

The first outer toolpath is the contour of the complete printable-center region. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its body-facing edge uses the analytical center-cap endpoints prepared before the layer-wide model-gap pass. Its front segment has length exactly `contact_width`, including positive values below one extrusion width. On a flat wall, expanding this center region by half a bead width makes the model-facing physical boundary tangent along the same span. Ordinary polygon erosion is never used to recover Painted Shape's first center contour because it would change a short contact span or erase a zero-length nose. Additional loops offset inward from the completed physical area by the configured spacing. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance in scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, each fin's polygons and center areas are finalized and checked for topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry supplies no directional capacity. Once geometry for all selected fins is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. An outline failure discards the batch's temporary paths and restarts upward planning with that plan's original seed excluded; its capacity is therefore never counted without a printable fin. Only a successful batch is stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and used to generate bed infill. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Above the bed, generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit, or its center contour splits or collapses; already generated loops are retained. Thicker fins can therefore have hollow interiors. A fin is rejected if its first contour cannot be generated. Each fin contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.

The bed layer unites all accepted fin footprints before generating any bed contours or infill. Its outer center area combines the merged footprint's half-width inset with the saved contact centers, preserving narrow fronts and point contacts. Overlapping feet therefore share a perimeter instead of retaining intersecting internal walls. Disconnected regions and holes retain separate closed contours; further walls use the configured loop count and support flow spacing. Bed contours start at their vertex with the greatest clearance from the first model layer. Infill is generated once over the merged interior after subtracting space for the emitted walls, using the ordinary support infill generator.


## Contact eligibility and diagnostics

Let `w` be support line width, `t` fin thickness, `c` contact length and `g` model XY clearance. A large painted plane is not sufficient by itself: the fixed sheet plane must intersect the actual wall at each contact height, and the complete bead span must pass the candidate's authorization and geometry validation.

| Stage | Condition | Consequence on failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Painted mesh | Only enforced facets of model-part volumes participate. Simple and Simulated require individual XY normal magnitude at least 0.5. Painted Shape includes every facet in island moments and requires only nonzero XY magnitude to supply a cut | Facet supplies no painted cut |
| Painted macro surface | Painted Shape retains persistent 45-degree crease chains as barriers, averages within one contact-scale geodesic radius, and requires macro XY magnitude at least `sin(15 degrees)` | Host site is deprioritized or candidate rejected |
| Painted cut | Facet must intersect the section height in two distinct points; its outward XY normal must leave the model at a 0.6 mm probe | Cut discarded |
| Direction | Forward intersection with painted XY geometry; A within 30 degrees of D | Demand blocked, other demands continue |
| Seed | Layer risk above 0.8; actual outer wall within `w`; unblocked point; an owning print region at `wall - 0.25*w*normal` | Candidate skipped |
| Seed reuse | Nearby aligned accepted fin or an exact cached failed seed | Candidate skipped |
| Contact wall | Actual outer-wall intersection must exist in the fixed sheet plane | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact authorization | Simple and Simulated require paint across the complete bead. Painted Shape requires only that its seed came from the intent island; its contact bands use actual model contour spans | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact blocker | Contact point must not lie in a support-blocker slice | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact span | Sample the complete `c + w` bead span; each sample must project to an outer contour within `w` | Entire contact band skipped |
| Direct-paint vertical extent | Every span sample of a directly painted contact must find paint at layer middle, bottom and top; the seed's highest layer omits the top sample above its mathematical seed height. Segment intersection fraction tolerance is `1e-7`, ray distance at most `w` | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact normal | Simple and Simulated require a non-upward local average. Painted Shape retains the local XY contour normal and applies its crease-aware macro slope, including a locally upward slope | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact axis | From the round-offset endpoint A1, the nearest same-contour axis N0 is used within 30 degrees of A; otherwise Nc is clamped to 30 degrees and must hit that contour within `gap / cos(30 degrees) + support line width` | Seed line falls back to its point proposal; lower contact band is skipped |
| Contact neck | Subtract model interior expanded by `w/2`, clip to bed contracted by `w/2`; neck must remain nonempty | Whole candidate rejected |
| Highest band | Highest contact layer must belong to an accepted band | Whole candidate rejected |
| Body top | Top depth minus gap at least `2*w`; highest wall within `w` of seed | Whole candidate rejected |
| Body projection | Local wall must face A; lower inner edge may not intrude into prior vertical projection by more than `w/2` | Whole candidate rejected |
| Body clearance | Remaining body depth at least `2*w`; sampled center and both thickness edges outside model and inside bed | Whole candidate rejected |
| Completed geometry | One connected polygon and one valid center contour per layer, without holes, with room for the first loop | Whole candidate rejected |
| Fin overlap | Simple and Simulated require no positive-area overlap above the bed. Painted Shape unions intersecting intent fins before outlining | Candidate rejected, or merged for Painted Shape |
| Outline | Every non-bed layer must produce its first closed outline | Exact seed excluded and batch selection retried |

The all-or-none contact-band rule stops on its first failed layer. It does not mean all other layers failed. Lower contact projection absorption, lateral clipping and bed termination affect only the lower neck geometry, not whether the original band was accepted. Contact-height adjustment is not implemented.

Info-level logs use the `Fin support` prefix. Painted Shape keeps phase summaries, per-chart SVG summaries, aggregate counters and geometry failure records. Successful per-branch, per-junction and per-contact operations do not emit individual records. Simple reports the highest painted, selected and emitted Z values plus its contour-interval counts. Painted Shape keeps one line per major timing boundary and uses long aggregate lines instead of per-contact or per-fin records. It reports connected islands, intent classifications, skeleton cleanup, fitted segments, proposals and geometry outcomes. `raw_intent_paths` counts medial-axis polylines; `logical_intent_paths` counts paths after graph recovery; `joined_intent_paths` counts approximate local-width fragment joins. `intent_junctions` and `paired_intent_junctions` describe the prepared graph topology, and `pruned_intent_branches` counts first-pass discarded short leaves. `blue_intent_groups` counts the 15-degree groups, `connector_blue_intent_groups` counts short groups retained to connect width-qualified groups, `discarded_blue_intent_groups` counts groups removed after both width classification and connector recovery, and `point_blue_intent_charts` counts chart areas whose longest group is shorter than its mean width. `trimmed_intent_samples` counts removed brush-cap samples, and `coarsened_intent_samples` counts vertices removed at full painted-width scale. `junction_split_intent_strokes` counts blue spans exposed at graph junctions. `blue_intent_roots` counts source roots in ordinary directed blue graphs; `merging_intent_strokes` counts unselected incoming segments registered for tail attachment. `branch_fit_candidates`, `branch_fit_rejects` and `branch_fit_continuations` count complete common-window comparisons, candidates with E greater than one and selected continuations. `branch_fit_ties` counts outgoing paths whose continuation was canceled by incoming scores within EPSILON of their best E. `branch_parent_stops` counts junctions with outgoing paths but no selected continuation; `branch_connection_misses` counts child rays without a usable intersection or contributors whose mean falls outside the finite main segment. `branch_cycle_cancellations` counts source junctions whose endpoint attachments were canceled because they form a dependency cycle; junctions only downstream of a cycle are not counted. `fit_split_intent_strokes` counts ordinary growth rollbacks that confirm a segment and restart at its tail. `line_plane_alignment_rejects` and `line_plane_missing_macro` count edges that could not define an allowed fixed plane. `line_to_point_fallbacks` counts point selections in candidate resolution when the source carried a line or graph-edge identity. `resolved_line_candidates` and `resolved_point_candidates` count the preferred alternatives selected before geometry, while `line_geometry_fallbacks` counts line proposals replaced by their point alternative after geometry. `root_intent_fins` counts distinct realized source roots plus independently promoted seeds; `promoted_intent_roots` counts children which became roots after their parent or connector failed, and `attached_point_fallbacks` counts point results absorbed by an adjacent surviving line. `composite_intent_fins` counts connected graph components emitted as one fin, `composite_intent_segments` counts lower planes absorbed into them, `continuous_intent_envelopes` counts degree-two line junctions included in the shared body-center network, and `composite_intent_merge_failures` counts invalid junction-axis connections and component assembly failures. `composite_projection_exposed_layers` counts generated compound projection layers extending outside the shared body center; `composite_projection_retained_layers` counts those same semantic extensions still wholly present after member-by-member attachment and compound finalization. `axis_limited_candidates` counts candidates carrying an explicit XY limit on A. `wall_detached_fins` and `axis_limit_detached_fins` count composite or independent fins that continue vertically after reaching their limit. `merged_overlap_candidates` counts accepted Painted Shape fins united before outlining. `merged_outline_splits`, `merged_outline_fallbacks` and `merged_outline_failures` expose multi-contour output, individual-fin recovery and unrecoverable merged outline layers. Painted Shape combines every populated rejection reason for one diagnostic stage into one `rejects=` field; each entry retains its count, first failing print Z and reason-specific value. Failed preferred lines use the `painted-shape-line-fallback` diagnostic stage and are separate from final rejection. Counts include repeated geometry attempts. `lower_contacts_accepted` excludes every root or attached point seed contact, and `suppressed_seed_projection` counts periodic bands omitted because their top reached the seed contact or its downward projection.

For an `empty-neck` rejection, value 1 means cap resolution failed, 2 means the center polygon degenerated, and 3 means model or bed clipping removed it.

The final Painted Shape line stores the first graph-attachment, component-assembly and finalization failure in `stage_failure_samples`, with the aggregate counts beside it. Detailed records for every failure remain available at debug level. `composite_intent_merge_failures` includes rejected axis connections and component assembly failures. Invalid axis connections do not join components; a resulting component without a seed needs an independently valid seed before it can proceed. Component assembly and stack-connectivity failures reject that component.

`single_projection_layers` and `composite_projection_layers` count materialized transition sections across attempts; they are not counts of unique print layers. The corresponding `*_max_layers` value is the largest count in one attempt, and `*_zero_layers` counts attempts with no materialized transition. A completed Painted Shape pass evaluates down to the bed, even if earlier sections were removed by C, so `bed` records completion of the pass rather than proof of nonempty bed geometry.

At info log level or higher verbosity, Painted Shape writes one `SVG/fin-support/painted-shape-skeleton-<run>-<chart>-r<raw>-l<logical>-k<kept>.svg` per chart. All geometry is displayed in world XY: gray is the painted boundary, orange the raw skeleton, cyan the protected global axis, red the pruned skeleton branches, blue the retained source paths, and green the final solved segments. Only original chart data is mapped to the surface for these diagnostics; green segments are drawn directly. The cyan protected-axis overlay records the earlier first-pass skeleton and remains visible even when the later blue-group filter discards every group and selects the point alternative. It does not indicate a surviving blue path or a final fin.

The companion `painted-shape-pruning-<run>-<chart>.svg` shows the graph immediately after first-pass pruning, before path joining, cap trimming or blue coarsening. Light gray means removed, cyan means protected, purple means leaf-checked and ultimately retained, and red means retained without a leaf check. Its world-XY display is enlarged fourfold for labels; this does not change geometry or pruning decisions. Labels `B0`, `B1`, etc. identify successive nonempty leaf-check events, so a branch checked again can have multiple labels. The decision uses `length + EPSILON >= max(feature_scale, mean_width)`; colors describe final first-pass edge states rather than earlier decisions. `stage=painted-shape-pruning` supplies category edge counts and the SVG path/open result. Chart numbers identify SVG charts, not island IDs.

`painted-shape-blue-groups-<run>-<chart>.svg` displays the groups after cap trimming and coarsening: blue means width-qualified, cyan marks the retained longest group, orange marks a short group retained as a connector, and pink means discarded. Its geometry is displayed in world XY, but grouping angles, lengths and widths are measured in the surface chart. `G0`, `G1`, etc. identify groups within that chart; individual groups do not emit log records. `stage=painted-shape-blue-groups` reports the longest group's identity, common threshold, 15-degree turn limit, retained short-connector count, point-fallback decision and SVG path/open result. Cyan in this group diagram means a retained longest group, unlike the earlier protected-axis overlay in the skeleton and pruning diagrams; a point-fallback group diagram has no retained cyan group. The ordinary skeleton SVG draws only the surviving blue source paths. Diagnostic collection does not affect grouping, pruning or fitting decisions.

`contact-turn` values are angles in degrees. `contact-span` values identify section 0 (middle), 1 (bottom), or 2 (top). `projection-intrusion` is the inner-edge increase in mm, compared with `w/2`; depth and contour values are mm, while `wall-facing` is a dot product. For Simple/Simulated legacy finalization, `topology` uses a bit mask: 1 means multiple or missing physical areas, 2 physical holes, 4 multiple or missing center regions, 8 center holes, and 16 an invalid center contour. `no-direction` records requested D in degrees and uses Z=-1 because no layer was selected. `contact-contour` value -1 means no outer-contour intersection; nonnegative values report displacement in mm. `top-wall` distance zero may mean no intersection rather than a valid zero-distance hit. Painted Shape reports per-region failures by stage and uses `unsupported-section` for missing immediate lower-layer material and `disconnected-stack` for multiple disconnected physical components across layers. Initial exits also log insufficient layers/raft, empty footprint, negligible base area or peak risk at most 0.8. GUI logging defaults to info; a higher severity suppresses these diagnostics.


Each directional attempt logs requested D, resolved A when available, and its own rejection summary before the object-wide aggregate. `seed-ray-miss` counts eligible layers with painted cuts but no forward intersection on the fixed A ray; its value is the cut count. `seed-low-risk` counts painted layers excluded by the 0.8 risk threshold. These layer counts explain a small number of actual seed-validation attempts despite a large painted region.

A failed direct-paint lookup receives a second diagnostic-only scan, using the same facing, parallel and endpoint tolerances. `paint-empty` means the requested section has no authorized painted cuts. `paint-facing` means none face the lookup ray. `paint-parallel` means every facing cut is parallel to that ray. `paint-distance` reports the nearest in-segment ray intersection in mm, to compare with support line width. `paint-endpoint` reports the minimum endpoint overrun in mm when intersections lie outside the painted segments. These details apply both to middle-section lookup and full-span validation; the corresponding `contact-span` section index identifies vertical sampling failures. This second scan does not run for successful lookups and does not relax any condition.
