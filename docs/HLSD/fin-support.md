# Fin support

Fin support has three styles. **Simple** preprocesses painted side cuts into intervals on the actual sliced outer contours. The paint grants permission but never supplies candidate coordinates; intervals on the same model edge are merged. Consecutive painted edges on one island form a contact line while their turn stays within 15 degrees and the straight contact remains within half a support line width of that line. Candidate centers need enough room from the ends of the joined line for the complete contact bead, including its line width. On each highest available layer it ranks sharp convex corners and long straight-edge midpoints derived from the complete sliced model contour after scale-dependent simplification. A remaining interval midpoint is the fallback. Every chosen site subtracts an unavailable range at its own contour location and along its downward wall projection. If a lower section has no new wall intersection, the last wall location snaps to a same-facing contour within one support line width; that snapped location continues to the next section. The configured placement distance limits travel along the connected island contour at each location. Walking stops at a back-facing edge, so nearby edges across a fold or on another island remain available. Candidate selection exhausts the region before constructing fins. Each selected site becomes a standard fin candidate; tracing, periodic contact validation, projection, body geometry, finalization and toolpath generation then use the same pipeline as the other styles. Risk is fixed to one and only the existing surface-tilt falloff scales body expansion. **Simulated** uses the printed-prefix planner described below and remains the default.

**Painted shape** first establishes connected painted islands and checks their area before interpreting eligible islands as scale-aware intent graphs. Island connectivity groups connected painted geometry; it does not limit the island to one fin. Within each model-part volume, painted facets connected by an edge, subdivision vertex or exactly coincident mesh vertex belong to one island. Disconnected components remain separate regardless of their distance, size or normal alignment, and islands do not join across volumes. Every nondegenerate painted facet contributes its transformed area and first moment, including horizontal and locally upward-facing facets. Each complete island must satisfy `island area >= (contact length + support line width) * contact height`. Percentage contact length and fin thickness are resolved by `fin_support_dimensions()` from `support_material_flow(&object).width()`. The island-area gate, feature scale and generated fin dimensions share this support-flow reference; no source-region lookup is needed for dimension conversion. This intent-area gate runs once after island connectivity is established and before crease extraction, geodesic normal averaging, macro-patch partitioning, skeleton extraction or ordinary/crease intent classification. Only facets of eligible islands participate in crease extraction and normal averaging; original face indices and island traversal order are retained. An island below the threshold supplies no intent or candidate. Ordinary and crease inputs use this same gate. Chart projections, represented stroke/point areas and later splits do not replace the original island area or trigger another area test. Persistent creases partition an eligible island into macro patches; each patch is projected into its own surface-aligned chart. Each patch chart is the polygon union of its projected painted triangles. `Geometry::MedialAxis::build()` extracts its unextended skeleton directly from that union, without morphological closing or boundary smoothing. Skeleton widths and contour measurements use this projected boundary and retain the existing mapping to the source surface. The source island area used by the earlier gate remains unchanged. Its minimum-width argument remains zero so a valid narrow stroke is not discarded before its topology is known. The returned `ThickPolyline` segments retain their local full painted widths and original junction endpoints.

**Painted Shape decision ownership:** Island area authorizes the painted instruction; intent resolution determines its geometry and classification; candidate generation translates the resolved intent without another eligibility decision. Physical realization alone determines whether a contact band can be constructed, using generated wall coverage, blockers and body attachment. An intent-area pass is not proof of physical contact success, and a physical failure does not revoke or reinterpret the painted instruction. Band-top failure, lower-layer continuation and taper-layer skipping retain their shared lifecycle rules.

**Separate front construction:** Ordinary and crease contacts have independent front-solving entry points. The crease entry constructs its perpendicular straight segment and finishes without ordinary span validation. The ordinary entry retains the straight tangent approximation at one physical wall hit, followed by lateral sampling with the half-sample acceptance rule below. The span samples only accept or reject that approximation and do not construct its endpoints. A shared straight-segment coordinate helper does not select either contact policy. Body attachment, contact-band lifecycle and taper clipping consume the selected front afterward. This separation applies by band contact type: a crease point's periodic bands and their tapers use the ordinary entry.

**Physical coverage criterion:** Ordinary Painted Shape contacts retain the existing sampling positions across the lateral span `c + w`, where `c` is configured contact length and `w` is support line width. There are `N = max(2, ceil((c + w) / w)) + 1` sample points, including both endpoints. A point counts as contacting only when its physical-wall query returns a hit whose displacement from the expected sample position is at most `w`. The attempt passes when at least `round(N / 2)` points contact, with .5 rounded upward: 3 points require 2 contacts, 4 require 2, and 5 require 3. Contacting samples need not be consecutive. Missing or displaced samples count as noncontacts and do not individually reject the attempt. The sample locations and straight front endpoints remain unchanged; this count criterion does not claim continuous surface coverage or an exact physical touching length. It applies to every ordinary Painted Shape band and taper, including a crease point's lower periodic bands. Crease fronts do not perform this sampling. Simple and Simulated retain their all-samples requirement. The island-area gate remains a separate intent decision.

The intent extractor rebuilds coarse connectivity from those returned polylines. Every consecutive point pair becomes an edge between exact scaled-integer vertices. Each connected graph obtains a global axis from two weighted farthest-point searches. Before local pruning, a compactness classifier measures the original axis mean full width W using chart-length weighting. It simplifies a separate copy of the axis with per-sample error tolerance equal to half the larger of local full width and physical feature scale; replacement chords must remain inside paint. Adjacent axis-edge widths are averaged at shared axis vertices for this tolerance only. The original width integral remains unchanged. With simplified axis length L, a component is compact when max(0, L - W) + EPSILON is less than max(feature_scale, W), or no axis edge exists. All edges of that component are removed together. This subtraction is a classification allowance for caps, not a geometric trim. Other components continue independently. Surviving axes remain protected during first-pass removal of side branches shorter than the larger of the physical feature scale and their length-weighted mean full painted width. Protection applies to this pruning pass only; it does not exempt a later blue group from its common-width threshold. There is no second-pass branch voting or separate isolated-short-path collapse.

Path preparation retains the existing connectivity recovery: degree-two vertices join automatically, while junction directions are measured over one painted-width/physical-feature scale. A continuation within 30 degrees is paired; one between 30 and 60 degrees is paired only when its opposing-direction score exceeds the next pair by at least 0.1. The last two unpaired incident edges are paired. Open fragments without a shared vertex may join when their endpoint tangents continue within 30 degrees, their gap is at most 1.5 times the larger of the physical feature scale and their mean endpoint width, and the complete connector remains inside the same painted area. Closed prepared paths are excluded from line construction; if no blue line survives, the chart emits a standalone point.

Paths beginning or ending at degree-one vertices have their brush caps trimmed inward by half the original path's chart-length-weighted mean full painted width. Both ends use this same pre-trim measurement, without reducing the requested distances to preserve a fraction of the path. Each end stops at the first junction and never consumes the continuation beyond it; a junction endpoint is not cap-trimmed. Only original samples inside the remaining interval survive, with the existing numerical tolerance at its boundaries. No endpoint is interpolated and no excluded sample is retained to make a line. A terminal span with fewer than two surviving points, counting its junction, is removed up to that junction while the continuation remains. A path with fewer than two surviving points is discarded; losing one path does not classify other surviving paths as points. If no blue line survives in the chart area, its point classification is explicit, including for crease diagnostics.

The remaining blue path is coarsened at one full painted width: endpoints stay fixed while sub-width edges remove the interior vertex with the smallest accumulated chord error, provided the replacement remains inside paint. Junction samples still in that interval and one direction witness about one local painted width away along each incident path cannot be removed. Source order, original widths, measurement sources and surface data survive this preparation.

The coarsened blue edges are grouped in the surface-aligned two-dimensional chart, before surface-Z orientation and green fitting. A group follows exact shared source vertices while each successive turn is at most 15 degrees. Among available continuations, the smallest turn wins; exact ties preserve source-list order. Edges belong to one group only. Unassigned edges seed groups in source-list order, extending first toward the seed's second endpoint and then outward from its first endpoint so an interior seed does not split a smooth run. The angle is local, not relative to a fixed overall direction. Group length is the sum of chart-space edge lengths, with no additional smoothing, chord-length substitution or cumulative-turn restriction. Small successive turns can therefore accumulate into a winding path; this filter does not prove that every retained group is a central axis.

For each chart area, the strictly longest blue group supplies one common threshold W; equal-length groups retain list order. W is that group's length-weighted mean full painted width, integrating the mean of each edge's two endpoint widths over its chart-space length. If the longest group is shorter than W, all blue groups are discarded and the chart emits a standalone point. Otherwise every group of length at least W is width-qualified and survives. Short groups also survive when they lie on an existing connection between distinct width-qualified groups, including chains of short groups. The qualifying set is frozen before connector recovery; rescued short groups never become new qualifying anchors. Connections use exact shared source vertices, including junctions inside groups, rather than XY proximity. A short spur touching two qualifying groups at their single shared junction is not a connector. Neither isolated short components nor dangling branches or cycles attached at only one junction qualify. Existing alternative routes between qualifying groups are retained without selecting a new shortest route. There is no per-group width threshold, feature-scale floor, extra cap subtraction or protected-axis exemption. Removing a group splits its source paths at discarded edges; remaining samples are never connected across the resulting gaps. The threshold does not alter any original width or Q measurement. A nonempty input always retains its longest group unless it takes the point alternative. At the final blue-output boundary, a chart area with no retained blue path emits one standalone blue point at its two-dimensional area centroid, with holes subtracted. This includes compactness rejection, cap exhaustion and group rejection. The point uses the same chart-to-surface mapping as line samples and retains its mapped surface position, source face, patch/chart identity, patch macro normal and represented chart area. It has no fabricated width measurement or zero-length line. Patch aggregation carries these points alongside line strokes, so a point from one patch is not lost when another patch in the island has lines. Candidate preparation consumes each explicit point through the existing point-fin pipeline; it never creates a point merely because an island has no lines, and does not substitute an island centroid or a discarded-path length moment. Invalid charts that never reach blue output do not implicitly become points. Line/point interpretation is completed within intent resolution. Candidate generation preserves that result and does not use predicted contact feasibility to select a point alternative.

Retained source paths split at source junctions, with the junction included in every adjacent span. Group filtering does not move junctions or create connections at projected intersections. At the end of blue processing, every retained edge is directed from higher original surface Z to lower Z; equal-Z endpoints follow their stable source-list order. Paths split where this direction changes. A node with outgoing edges and no incoming edge is a root, including a local maximum inside a path. Equal-Z directed edges count as incoming. Root identity and junction connectivity are independent, so a root can also be a junction. There is no component-wide highest-point fallback or later reversal from one global root.

The ordinary intent pipeline is painted area → orange medial axis → dark-blue samples and width measurements → original 3D surface → world XY → final green graph → resolved fin intent → candidate generation → physical realization. `Geometry::MedialAxis` optionally returns one measurement for every `ThickPolyline::width` entry: the actual contour segment or endpoint source, its identity and the closest point Q in scaled chart coordinates. Segment sources use the nearest point on that finite segment; vertex sources use the vertex. The existing side choice is preserved. Whenever shared samples retain the smaller full width, they retain its matching Q and source. No opposite-side averaging or side reselection is used. The chart point P, original width, source identity, mapped surface position and Z, and mapped Q all travel with retained blue samples. P and Q use the same surface mapping and world-XY projection. Fitting uses `h_i = max(norm(Q'_i - P'_i), EPSILON)`, where `EPSILON = 0.0001 mm` is only a numerical lower bound for the allowed half-width. It does not alter the original measurement. There is no perpendicular width projection or elliptical error model. Samples coincident in XY retain their independent surface heights, order and sources.

Ordinary fits, free fits and branch comparisons use the same XY arc-length weights: `w_i = (norm(P'_i - P'_(i-1)) + norm(P'_(i+1) - P'_i)) / 2`, counting only adjacent edges inside the participating range. A fixed-start orthogonal fit passes through its fixed anchor; a free fit passes through the weighted centroid. The anchor is a constraint, not an additional sample. For unit direction d and line origin O, every participating point is checked with `e_i = norm((P'_i - O) - dot(P'_i - O, d) * d)` and `E = max(e_i / h_i)`. Acceptance requires `E <= 1`, including samples of zero weight. A single sample with a fixed anchor initially uses the anchor-to-sample direction. A free first segment waits for a distinct XY position to determine its direction. If the entire group coincides in XY, it remains a degenerate planar segment with all original surface data and heights and reaches the existing downstream macro-plane handling.

Ordinary growth adds the next unaccepted blue sample, refits all participating samples and projects the newly added sample onto the line as the candidate tail. An accepted candidate saves the complete state. A failing candidate restores the previous accepted state and confirms that segment. The next segment is fixed at its predecessor's confirmed tail and retries the rejected sample. Accepted samples do not participate in the next fit, and the generated tail does not become a blue sample. The predecessor's original surface endpoint is retained separately as boundary metadata for the represented surface interval. There is no green minimum-length filter, recursive whole-range splitting, adjacent-fit merging or post-fit segment absorption. Fine-grained skeleton cleanup belongs to orange and dark blue.

Roots start green solving in source-list order. Each junction waits for all incoming blue paths before choosing continuations; arrival timing does not select the main line. Every incoming accepted state is compared with every outgoing blue path. Each pair adds up to two subsequent blue samples, excluding the starting junction. The next junction or path end stops the window and is itself included. All pairs use the smallest available count. Each pair is fitted and checked once after its entire window, using only that incoming state's samples and the selected outgoing window.

Among compliant pairs, repeatedly choose the smallest E, using each incoming and outgoing path at most once, up to two continuations per source junction. Equal scores for distinct eligible choices preserve source-list order. If multiple incoming paths score within EPSILON of the best compliant E for the same outgoing path, that outgoing path loses continuation eligibility; it remains available as a separately solved child and may reconnect geometrically. Each winner carries its full accepted state and samples into further growth. A continuation remains one straight green segment across the junction; adding an interior junction does not split or refit it. Ordinary later growth may still exceed the fit tolerance and confirm that segment under the usual rollback rule.

Unselected incoming paths confirm their accepted states and register their last actual green segments for tail attachment. Unselected outgoing paths grow freely in source order, leaving the main-facing endpoint of their first actual green segment open. Once that first segment is confirmed, subsequent segments use fixed starts. Grandchildren use the same competition and forward-growth rules. If no continuation is selected, the source-list-first incoming segment's confirmed tail supplies the common junction. Paths sharing a source root use its fixed start. Every outgoing first segment of a source root retains that root even if its tail merges elsewhere.

Each source junction records its final main segments, each outgoing child's nearest actual green segment and each unselected upstream tail. Attachment waits until these segments are solved. An internal junction is resolved after its main segments' external endpoint attachments, using committed segment identities rather than whole source paths. A confirmed stopped tail or fixed root start does not depend on the opposite endpoint. Reconnection moves only the registered head or tail, preserving its other endpoint, and may rotate, extend or truncate the whole segment. It neither refits nor checks the residual again. If actual committed segments create a cycle of endpoint dependencies, cancel the endpoint attachments at source junctions in that cycle before applying any of them, preserving the fitted segments. Junctions outside the cycle still resolve normally. A valid crossing between two continued mains may still be represented without moving either endpoint; it waits for any remaining external attachments.

When two continued finite main segments have a unique intersection, that point is their shared internal junction. Both segments remain whole, and all pending children and upstream tails at that source junction attach directly to the crossing without averaging it with their own intersections. Otherwise the main segments remain separate. Each pending segment casts a ray from its opposite endpoint toward the endpoint being attached and chooses the first finite main segment it hits; equal hit distances preserve main source-list order. Parallel, directionless, backward and out-of-range intersections are unusable. Attachments are grouped by the chosen main, and each group retains the arithmetic-mean collapse to one junction on that main. A mean outside the finite main disconnects its contributors; a ray with no usable hit disconnects individually. There is no averaging across separate mains. Connected members share an actual XY node while retaining their original blue junction and root identities. Disconnected members retain their solved geometry and independent ownership. Foldback detection is intentionally absent.

Candidate preparation, straight-line correction and fin-intent resolution share one private working graph. Each island owns a stable collection of mutable nodes and a fixed collection of line records; lines hold node references, and nodes refer to immutable source measurements. `prepare_painted_fin_sources()` creates one working entity per green node, without copying junction state into each line. `correct_painted_fin_lines()` first plans rotations and reconnections against frozen segments, then commits node positions and reference changes. A shared junction remains one entity unless its corrected attachments actually split; a broken endpoint attachment creates an independent free endpoint. Coincident hits originating at the same junction share a node. If distinct source junctions meet the same host position after correction, the existing host placement supplies the shared entity in source-list order, including its complete source identity and height bound. Incoming references reuse that entity rather than retaining its ID with a different source Z. Direction interpretation belongs to intent resolution; candidate generation does not repeat it as a contact-host or contact-top eligibility test. `make_painted_fin_candidate()` prepares physical attributes without copying the graph. The ordinary straight-line selector orders its endpoint-first node references along the accepted line before passing them to fin-intent resolution. Prepared groups carry ordered node references, allowing a nonbranching polyline with shared junctions; fin-intent resolution preserves this order without chord sorting, reversal or refitting. Only completed fin intents become value snapshots containing every path vertex and its resolved height for the layer-geometry pipeline; no working reference escapes that boundary. Ordinary line-to-point interpretation is confined to intent resolution before candidate generation. Accepted convex-crease outputs enter through a separate direct-path producer. Their source vertices bypass ordinary green fitting, straight-line normal correction and ordinary intent-direction interpretation; they still use the same shared-node fin-intent boundary and shared geometry realization with the crease contact rule.

All source charts finish blue generation before ordinary green solving begins. Selected blue chains supplied to a measured crease pair are consumed as a whole, regardless of missing/ambiguous hits, symmetry rejection or later fin failure. Point pairs consume their participating selected chains and any explicit source-chart point. Consumption uses original chart-local sample identity, so shared junction occurrences are removed consistently. Remaining ordinary blue paths split at consumed samples; there is no bridging chord across a consumed point, no restored source after rejection and no new area-center fallback. Residual spans still require at least two samples. Multiple eligible crease pairs remain separate; they are not merged or deduplicated.

Each accepted crease part has independent nodes and generation ownership, separate from ordinary fins and other crease parts. Line inputs retain all accepted original 3D crease vertices, oriented from the higher endpoint toward the lower endpoint; equal endpoint heights retain source order. A subsequent increase larger than EPSILON rejects the whole path before a root stem can be created, without splitting or repairing it. The source high end supplies the root; an isolated point supplies its own position. Crease-line XY and Z enter unchanged, without per-vertex offset. The complete-model owner samples the root 3D normal for expansion slope and computes its horizontal-section exterior bisector for root extension. Required root heights are batched through the regular mesh slicer at source Z (using slicer float precision), not the nearest print layer. The root section uses equal unit outward normals of adjacent contour edges, or the normal of a straight edge. Missing or ambiguous root direction, or an invalid root surface normal, rejects the line without restoring consumed blue points. Interior vertices no longer require normal or section-direction queries. Point inputs retain the existing direction from the normalized sum of the two separately normalized XY face normals, their source face aggregate for slope, and ordinary ray-generated geometry.

A resolved crease line retains its original 3D source geometry. Its finite body path uses the unchanged vertices with the added outer root extension; the stem and source path form one generation group. Each layer clips that body path by the common model gap and retains its A1. Independently, `PaintedCreaseIntent::position_at_z()` intersects the original 3D source with the layer's `slice_z` to obtain Q. Shared-vertex hits count once; multiple distinct intersections or no intersection supply no new source contact. The source is not extrapolated, and a missing intersection is not replaced by a nearby convex corner. The synthetic root stem is excluded from this source query. At Q, the model's horizontal section supplies its outward bisector n. A ray from Q along -n finds the printed-wall hit H; A1-H defines the contact axis before body-cap and side-chain construction. Both cap and printable-center model clearance clip the side chains, whose actual endpoints are shared by body, contact and taper. Below the source range, no new actual crease contact is created; any retained geometry follows the common continuation and taper rules.

Direct crease lines and points share contact schedules, printable-center construction and merged output. Whenever a band uses crease contact, its source query is the original-3D-source intersection at `slice_z`, followed by Q/-n wall coverage and A1-H axis resolution. The contact front is perpendicular to n, independently of A1-H, and retains the configured contact width and half-bead placement. The section-normal calculation sums adjacent unit outward edge normals equally; a source point inside a straight section edge uses that edge's normal. Missing or ambiguous source intersections or section normals are contact failures, not permission to choose a nearest or sharpest corner. Crease contacts have no turn limit. After resolving their physical wall hit, they construct the configured straight front perpendicular to the source exterior bisector, with the half-bead offset. This completes crease-front solving: no lateral surface sampling, span-coverage validation or physical touching-length test follows. Their source-normal ray must still hit printed wall coverage. Blockers and subsequent body attachment remain common contact responsibilities.

**Contact-type scope for point and line bodies:** A crease line uses crease contact for its highest band, periodic bands and each band's downward taper. A crease point uses the same crease source query for its highest band and that band's downward taper only. All lower periodic bands of a point fin, including a point selected from a crease, use ordinary contact; their tapers also use ordinary contact. The ordinary +/-30-degree turn clip and full-span coverage checks therefore apply to those periodic bands. Contact type belongs to the band and remains attached to its taper; it is not selected solely from the current layer's position inside or below the highest band's height range. A point fin's ray, expansion and body clipping remain the ordinary point-body construction throughout.

Crease point and line contacts share one source-query pipeline; point classification does not select a different source-search rule. The source geometry needed for crease contact is distinct from the body input. Classifying an input as a point fin changes its body to a ray while retaining a separate original 3D contact source. A point produced by the paired point/cap-exhaustion decision retains the common untrimmed painted crease interval; a point produced by one accepted section retains that complete source section interval. Line inputs retain their accepted source interval. Original vertices and interval endpoints supply the same unique-height-intersection query in all cases. Body classification depends on the body input, not on the number of contact-source vertices. No gap-radius corner search or conversion of a point body into a line body is needed.

Direct crease inputs do not participate in ordinary green fitting, junction attachment or normal correction. All their completed bodies participate in the common final model-gap subtraction; only actual contact and transition regions provide exemptions, never the whole crease body. Contacts use the shared side endpoints after cap and clearance clipping. Member rejection and group-based root reachability retain the existing rules. No failure restores consumed blue geometry.

Geometric attachment names describe their role rather than generation ancestry: `attachment_host_edge` identifies the line used for reconnection, shared nodes hold the attachment positions, and `source_attachment_junction` preserves the original host/node pair for sibling cavity grouping. These host references do not determine a generation root or a junction height.

The ordinary retained green graph passes through normal correction and intent-direction checks before becoming a resolved fin intent. Source surface samples, width measurements and source height bounds remain immutable. A planar junction never passes back through the chart mapping. The resulting finite path and its one-time model queries are described below.

A chart whose surface normals cancel or whose projected triangles overlap by more than 30% cannot supply ordinary blue geometry or a two-sided crease comparison. A persistent crease alone does not create fin intent: it needs usable opposite-patch blue outputs and the crease selection, cap and section rules below. Represented length, painted width, surface position and height bounds derive from source surface data, never from a generated XY segment or junction. The feature scale used by skeleton cleanup is `max(3 * support line width, contact length + support line width)`.

An ordinary chart with no retained blue path supplies its own area-center point. Every ordinary graph edge carries a fitted line description; intent resolution may classify it as a point at its highest green node when its line interpretation is unusable. Candidate generation receives the resulting type without selecting another alternative. Ordinary fitted edges from one macro patch share that patch's crease-aware, area-weighted macro normal. Direct crease inputs retain their two source face normals separately. Each edge's endpoint chord supplies its own turn within this common projection frame, and its vertical plane is anchored at the final green segment's first XY endpoint. The sheet direction therefore comes from the painted macro region rather than the normal at a later selected contact point. Direction correction is measured entirely in the XY ground-plane projection. Two equal-length alternatives follow the projected macro normal and its perpendicular tangent, each oriented to minimize endpoint displacement. If their displacements differ by at most EPSILON, the line is unusable and resolves as a point. Otherwise the smaller-displacement alternative is used when its displacement is no greater than one full local painted width plus EPSILON; if neither fits that width, the original direction is retained. This correction decision is made once per edge. Correction rotates the finite XY segment about that fixed origin while preserving its projected length; the source Z limits remain separate contact-height semantics. All corrected axes are frozen before junction updates. An affected dependent endpoint follows its fixed A axis toward the host's finite rotated segment, using its head-or-tail attachment orientation. A missed or backward hit breaks that connection without erasing a source root or another connection at the member's other end. Rotation about a shared root retains that exact pivot even for coincident corrected axes. Different hits split a source junction; coincident hits from that same source on the same host share a resolved junction. These updates do not feed back into the correction decisions. After junction resolution is complete, intent resolution first classifies ordinary sources whose macro surface is at least 80 degrees from horizontal as points, including XY-degenerate strokes. For the remaining usable lines it checks once that the normalized XY direction stays within `fin_support_painted_shape_line_angle` of its source patch macro direction. An accepted line bypasses painted-contour site selection and contact-top adjustment entirely; its source patch macro normal directly supplies its expansion slope. It interprets a usable fit and plane as a line and otherwise resolves the point description before candidate generation. This setting defaults to 40 degrees and supplies the direction-angle threshold for ordinary Painted Shape line selection. Once A is accepted, ordinary contact resolution clips its local turn to +/-30 degrees; it does not reject an intent merely because the unclipped contact direction exceeds that range. Physical contact feasibility does not participate in this line/point classification, and candidate generation does not repeat it. `fin_support_contact_projection_angle` independently controls downward projection. Accepted lines retain their shared green nodes and source height bounds; model-height resolution determines their final path and activation height. Converted green lines target the highest retained node using its corrected XY and immutable source Z, with source order breaking equal-height ties. Degenerate strokes use their retained green nodes as well. The existing painted-contour search resolves that target at or below its source height without the half-contact-height shift or a complete-model height query. Standalone blue points retain their source center and the upward adjustment by half the configured contact height. All ordinary point fins use `fin_support_expansion_factor` directly, without surface-tilt attenuation; line and direct crease expansion retain their existing rules.

Source anchoring belongs to intent resolution. An ordinary line supplies its corrected shared nodes, accepted direction and source patch macro normal directly. It does not require an intersection with a painted sliced contour, a selected-site macro-side check, or a contact-top search. Shared-node model-height resolution supplies its final finite path and activation height. An ordinary point retains the nearest source-contour selection for its ray seed, using the highest-node target for a converted green line and the contact-top adjustment only for a standalone center point. Line/point interpretation precedes that point-only search; a search failure cannot reclassify an accepted line. Candidate generation carries those resolved values into generation inputs without another area, painted-span, model-contour-span or predicted-contact-success test. In particular, it does not shift or discard a source anchor, lower its contact-top height, or replace a line with a point to satisfy a prospective physical contact. Actual H, contact direction, blocker eligibility, physical coverage and body attachment are resolved during physical realization. Failure or disappearance of a resolved line intent never creates a point replacement or an end extension.

Painted Shape detects facet adjacencies turning by at least 45 degrees. Connected sharp-edge chains shorter than the feature scale are treated as mesh-scale detail; longer chains become normal-propagation barriers and possible intent edges. A facet's macro normal is the area-weighted Gaussian average inside the same geodesic radius without crossing those barriers. A finite crease does not have to split the whole painted island: averaging may pass around its endpoint only within that radius. Adjacent candidate runs whose macro normals differ by more than 45 degrees remain separate. The macro-patch average supplies one slope and validation frame to every ordinary fitted edge from that patch; each actual contact retains its own local contour position and XY wall direction for clearance and contact geometry. Ordinary Painted Shape contacts clip the local turn to +/-30 degrees; crease contacts have no turn limit. These contact rules do not repeat intent-direction classification. Direct crease input preserves its source-derived initial body direction as described above; subsequent contact positions and fronts follow the shared crease contact rule. The island-area gate described above is the sole area-based intent authorization. Neither ordinary nor crease candidate generation rejects individual targets by represented area or tests a model-contour contact span.

Contour-based seed selection uses the layer's `slice_z`, matching the height of `layer.lslices` and final wall lookup. Simple retains the individual-facet side threshold and local non-upward contact rule. Painted Shape admits any facet with a nonzero XY cut; ordinary point selection applies its crease-aware macro-side rule. Simple and ordinary Painted Shape points clip complete painted cuts to the sliced contour before selecting a site. Accepted Painted Shape lines use their shared nodes instead of contour-site selection. Painting confined to the highest printed layer can therefore supply a candidate even when a cut at the physical layer top would collapse against the model's top edge.

**Painted Shape geometry and lifecycle contract**

The contact rule is selected for each band, separately from its point/line body input. Ordinary/crease contact crossed with point/line body input produces four geometry combinations; all four share contact geometry, side-chain construction and contact-band failure rules. Finite paths additionally retain their group-based clipping, root reachability and component lifetime.

1. **Contact rule:** Ordinary contact selects the nearest point on this model's section at the current layer, with no source-contour, surface-owner or proximity restriction. Its turn is clipped to the inclusive range of -30 to +30 degrees relative to the local body direction; an out-of-range nearest direction is clipped rather than rejected. The ordinary front is the straight tangent approximation at the configured contact length. Failure of the half-sample contact rule rejects the attempt; the front is not shortened to fit. Crease contact resolves Q from the original 3D crease source at `slice_z`, then queries physical wall coverage along the negative horizontal-section exterior bisector; it has no turn limit. Its front is perpendicular to that bisector. Crease lines use this rule for every band and taper; crease points use it only for the highest band and its taper. Lower periodic point-fin bands and their tapers use ordinary contact. Ordinary and crease retain their respective front-direction construction, and actual contacts lie on the physical outer surface of generated wall extrusions.
2. **Body input:** A point fin supplies a ray. A line fin supplies its existing finite fin-intent polyline. Prepared finite groups define start-preserving clipping and member failure scope; ordinary roots use separate stem groups, while a crease stem and its source path remain one group.
3. **Contact geometry:** One shared trapezoid construction connects the contact rule's front segment to the two actual attachment endpoints supplied by the body. A zero-length front produces a triangle. Both endpoints are shared with body assembly rather than reconstructed independently.
4. **Contact-band rules:** Every band is processed from its scheduled top downward. Failure to construct the top layer invalidates that band. Every contact failure below the top invokes continuation, including wall lookup, turn, span, blocker and geometry failures. Continuation adds the saved rear attachment to the shared side-chain cuts described below. Finite bands retain the existing path/source-presence and downstream-member rules: a missing retained path clips the band; a missing source contact clips it only when no downstream line is available, otherwise continuation can retain its scheduled bottom. Taper begins below the retained band bottom. A taper-layer failure emits no taper geometry on that layer and continues below. Failure of a highest band rejects its entire generating member; surviving members are checked again for root reachability.
5. **Body geometry:** Both inputs produce two continuous side chains. Inner bends use line intersections and outer bends use circular arcs. Root outer ends are flat. Cross-member junctions receive only the missing circular sectors when at least two incident contributions meet; a lone surviving endpoint remains flat. An internal bend already owned by a member receives no duplicate junction sector. Contact ends are clipped by both model gap and the contact's rear cap before the body hands over its actual attachment endpoints.
6. **Body rules:** Each layer clips the complete centerline input afresh. A retained finite fragment must contain its entire generation group's original start and connect to a valid root. An interior junction is not a replacement start. Contact search cannot extend beyond a finite endpoint. Geometry failure cannot convert a line to a point or create a root.
7. **Lifecycle:** A band progresses from contact or continuation to taper, then finishes. A later successful query cannot reopen that band after taper begins. A failed taper layer is skipped without ending the taper. Finite-member rejection and group-start retention follow the prepared grouping. Root reachability uses the existing component-wide termination state: once no root-connected body survives after root activation, that component does not reappear below. There is no additional per-junction segment withdrawal or per-segment permanent-stop policy.

**Printed-wall contact boundary:** All styles, point/line inputs and ordinary/crease contacts resolve their actual contact with the same wall-coverage query. Ordinary contacts first determine their source axis, then query from A1 towards the source. Bands using crease contact, on either point or line bodies, instead query from their source intersection Q along its inward horizontal normal -n; after finding H, they resolve the contact axis from H toward A1 before building the body and its shared attachment. Both queries intersect the physical coverage of the current layer's generated perimeter extrusions. Each segment is a round-ended capsule with radius half its actual `ExtrusionPath::width`, including widths produced by Arachne. Nested perimeter collections, loops and multipaths are traversed, including thin-wall/gap-fill paths held in those collections; force-no-extrusion paths are excluded. Infill is not a contact target. A per-run, lazy per-layer AABB index limits capsule tests to the query axis. For an origin outside wall coverage, the smallest forward capsule entry is the first boundary of the coverage union, without constructing or Boolean-unioning full-layer coverage polygons. The capsule intersection primitive is shared with infill through `Geometry`.

Painted Shape uses one bidirectional wall-boundary query for ordinary and crease contacts, independently of point/line body input. The contact rule supplies the origin and inward direction; intersections along that axis retain signed distances. An origin outside coverage extends inward to the first covered interval. An origin inside coverage clips back in the outward direction to the outer boundary of the covered interval containing it. Overlapping capsule intervals are treated as their coverage union, so an individual capsule boundary hidden inside another bead is not a contact surface. A zero-distance hit is valid only when the origin is already on the applicable surface. Both cases resolve the same outward-facing boundary using extension or clipping; they do not select whichever of the two directions gives the nearest arbitrary wall. This query changes the physical contact location, not source eligibility, finite-path endpoint limits or the body's prescribed centerline.

The resolved hit H is on the physical outer surface of the generated wall coverage. An origin inside the coverage is not itself an outer-surface hit merely because it is covered. If a valid surface hit H coincides with A1, the existing axis direction is retained rather than normalizing a zero vector. No hit means no actual contact, with no nominal-contour fallback. Source-model anchors remain separate for paint eligibility, surface queries and axis provenance; physical hits do not rewrite source heights, normals or paint measurements. Contact fronts retain the half-support-bead placement along the contact rule's front normal. Ordinary fronts use the straight local tangent approximation; crease fronts are perpendicular to the source intersection's horizontal-section exterior bisector. The front normal is separate from the angle-clipped query axis and the body cap normal. Actual contact bridges are not recut against the nominal model outline, which may extend beyond the printed corner. Body gap clipping, lower-transition clearance and contact continuation retain their separate rules. Coverage is the planar swept-width model of the generated wall paths, not a prediction of molten extrusion deformation.

Fin support generation requires completed perimeters, including support preview. Perimeter invalidation also invalidates Fin support so changed wall placement or widths cannot leave stale contacts. Other support types do not gain this dependency. Debug-level `fin-wall-contact` records identify the source anchor, A1, actual query origin and inward direction, resolved contact axis, layer, hit status, distance from the query origin and signed distance along the inward query direction. Failure snapshots distinguish the source-model anchor from the actual printed-wall point.

Source-model or crease anchors remain separate from printed-wall hits. A missing physical hit is a contact-construction failure, not a path-end event. At a band top it invalidates the band; below the top it invokes the same saved-front continuation as every other contact failure. Source presence does not restrict eligibility for continuation.

**Required contact-authorization invariant:** Simple and Simulated require direct paint at the seed and every lower periodic contact. Painted Shape applies the common island-area gate and resolves its intent graph before generating candidates without further intent-eligibility checks. It then constructs actual contacts against printed wall coverage, the blocker mask and the selected contact rule without requiring the brush mask to cover the bead. Failed attempts below a band top use the common continuation rule, including attempts rejected by blockers. Paint therefore cannot create a Painted Shape seed on unrelated geometry, but it does not clip the contacts after the intent has been accepted. The seed's mathematical height is its highest relevant section, so geometry above that section is ignored. Periodic-band suppression belongs to the same contact-eligibility stage as support-blocker checks, before complete band geometry is constructed. A band suppressed by the seed contact or its taper is ignored and cannot reject its generating member through later contact construction. Suppression omits the band; blocker rejection remains a failed contact attempt governed by the band-top and lower-layer continuation rules.

All styles share candidate realization, contact authorization and final toolpath storage, but Painted Shape constructs its geometry in printable-center space. `select_simple_fin_candidates()` returns a complete candidate batch, while the feedback-driven Simulated planner calls `select_simulated_fin_candidate()` for one candidate at a time. Ordinary Painted Shape fixes its line/point classification during intent resolution before candidate generation; direct crease inputs use the classification from their two-sided comparison. A point outcome creates only point-fin intent and retains its source island and edge for diagnostics; discarded line endpoints, attachment links and junctions do not participate in subsequent line assembly. A point neither owns downstream lines nor attaches back to a surviving line. Model clearance and contact failures after conversion do not change an intent's type.

Each accepted ordered, nonbranching path remains one finite generation group, retaining all vertices and shared interior junction references. Group activation uses the resolved head height. An interior junction does not split the group into separate clipping or failure units. A bend alone does not create a root, junction, activation height or contact schedule. Roots are determined in the line-only graph before fin-intent conversion: a node with outgoing spans and no incoming span is a generation root. Removing a point-classified edge can expose a root here; layer clipping or geometry rejection cannot create one later. Ordinary roots receive separate finite stem groups, each owning its highest contact band, while the original outgoing groups use periodic contacts. A direct crease path and its prefixed stem remain one group owning its highest and periodic bands. Highest-band failure rejects that complete member. Surviving members must retain a connection to an available root. Simple and Simulated use `validate_fin_contact()` on source-model anchors to require a complete painted bead. Actual contact attempts use the applicable printed-wall, span, blocker and contact-geometry checks; Painted Shape lower-layer failures invoke continuation.

Ordinary green roots resolve first, retaining the highest model-surface position at their XY not above their source Z. Every other ordinary node resolves once after all incoming starts have finished: at its fixed corrected XY, it selects the model-surface intersection closest in Z to its immutable source Z, bounded above by the lowest resolved height among its available incoming starts. Thus a node may rise above its source Z after fitting or reconnection, but cannot rise above an available upstream segment start. Equal-distance intersections select the lower Z. A bounded reference already on the surface is retained, including a vertical face with a continuous interval of heights. Shared junctions have one result used by all incident groups. Failed starts do not supply a height bound; another resolved incoming start may still supply one. Without an available incoming height or an admissible surface intersection, the node stays unresolved and groups requiring it are omitted. No node copies an upstream height as a substitute for a surface hit, moves along its adjacency span to a model projection, or retries from the complete model top. Cyclic height dependencies remain unresolved without affecting unrelated resolved groups. Direct crease nodes retain their exact source-surface Z and bypass this ordinary height selection. Source measurements remain unchanged; resolved Z belongs to the shared working node. Root stems retain their existing shared root height and are not queried again. Directions and activation layers are computed after all shared node updates. Input paths must be nonascending in resolved Z from head to tail; equal heights are allowed. Before layer tracing, an increase larger than EPSILON rejects the whole group and emits an info-level ascending-path diagnostic with its island, source edge, vertex indices and heights. This check does not reorder, reverse, split or repair the path. A valid complete group activates at its head height. Interior vertices and junctions retain their own source and resolved heights, but do not delay any part of the group until a separate activation layer.

At a root's activation layer, the model contour at `slice_z` is offset by `gap + 3 * extrusion line width`. For ordinary input, a new outer root is placed on that boundary along the bisector of the largest angular gap between the original outgoing directions, with source-list order breaking equal gaps. One ordinary outgoing line uses the same formula, giving its opposite direction. Direct crease-line input uses the horizontal-section exterior bisector at its source root instead, retaining the unchanged original root as an interior junction. For ordinary input, the original root stays at its original XY as a junction, and a new finite stem runs from the outer root to that junction. Original lines and interior junctions do not move or rotate. Both stem endpoints share the original resolved root Z and source identity; the outside-model point is not sampled for a new height or normal. The stem length does not count toward top-layer depth. Its contact width and thickness use the shared support-line-width conversion; outgoing contact widths are not added together. While gap covers the original junction, only the stem can survive there. Original outgoing groups appear only on layers where their start survives gap clipping and connects back to a surviving root.

Each stem owns one outer extension from its new root. Its initial length is top-layer depth; expansion continues with descending `slice_z` until the intent terminates or reaches the bed. It does not freeze at a group endpoint's height or at a ray-tracing stop. The stem retains the greatest existing expansion coefficient among the original outgoing lines, preserving the common-extension maximum, and uses the same outward direction. Finite groups form a composite through their actual shared endpoint or interior junctions; several generation roots can belong to one component.

A prescribed line supplies its finite centerline directly. Every layer starts from its full activated finite polyline and subtracts that layer's model offset by gap; it does not progressively expose segments according to their vertex heights. A retained fragment must contain the whole generation group's original start and connect to a surviving root. An interior junction is not a replacement start; another valid root cannot rescue a fragment cut away from its own group start. Connections are usable only where the actual retained polylines reach their shared junction, checked against constituent segments rather than the head-to-tail chord. Root extensions receive the same start-preserving clip. Reachability is recomputed after member failures using surviving members and roots. Within a connected finite component, once no root-connected body survives after root activation, the shared termination state suppresses all lower layers of that component. Before that termination, layers are independently clipped from the untrimmed group input. Highest-band failure withdraws the whole generating member, not only its prefix up to an interior junction.

Ordinary Painted Shape contacts use the same nearest-point and angle-clipping rule for point and line bodies. The search covers this model's entire current-layer section rather than an originally selected contour. After clipping the query direction to plus or minus 30 degrees around the local body direction, the ordinary resolver constructs its straight tangent front at the physical wall hit. It retains the configured contact length and requires at least half the sample points to contact, rounding .5 upward, rather than shortening the front. At a band top this invalidates the band; below the top it invokes the confirmed continuation rule. The resolved front and the body/contact axis remain separate values.

Crease contact uses the same source-intersection and wall-query method for a line body and a point body's highest band. The original crease geometry, rather than an XY search radius, determines Q at each queried `slice_z`. The highest point band's taper retains this method below the band; periodic point bands and their tapers use ordinary contact. A source query without a unique result supplies no new actual contact and follows the applicable band or taper failure rule. `support_object_xy_distance` controls body clearance, not a nearest/sharpest-corner search radius for crease contact.

For prescribed paths, tracing retains the terminal segment direction and supplies A1 to the ordinary or crease contact resolver before analytical cap construction. The gap boundary locates A1 and determines whether a natural endpoint can contact; its normal does not orient the cap. Each layer stores its complete retained polyline. Body strips use their own segment directions and join at bends with the existing rounded-junction geometry; a bend does not receive a model-facing cap. Member-local contact construction and compound body assembly both retain the bends. Natural endpoints and junctions do not acquire contacts by tracing past their finite limits. A finite endpoint on the clearance contour can supply an actual contact, including at zero gap; an endpoint away from that contour does not authorize a new contact. Band clipping follows retained-path and source-contact presence, with the downstream-member exception described below, rather than an unconditional transition at every finite endpoint. When A1 coincides with the model contour, that actual model contour's outward normal supplies the zero-length contact-axis direction, with hole normals oriented out of the solid. A band whose top has no body is invalid. A missing retained path clips the band. Source-contact loss clips it when there is no downstream line; otherwise continuation may retain the scheduled bottom. Fixed-plane transitions stop when their member has no retained path. Analytical cap construction is shared by ordinary and crease fins. It accepts both signs of the longitudinal-direction/cap-normal dot product; only an absolute denominator at most EPSILON rejects the parallel line intersection. No cap-facing angle rejection is applied. The physical envelope is expanded from the common clipped center geometry only at toolpath generation; it does not impose another per-intent acceptance check. The completed center geometry receives the existing contact-and-transition-exempted model-gap mask afterward; the first path clip does not replace this physical-clearance step.

A component with multiple generation roots may have separate cross-sections above their physical junction. Physical layer regions remain an `ExPolygons` collection through bounds, overlap checks and outlining; no secondary region is dropped to fit a single-polygon container.

Each member keeps a component-center record until layer finalization. It retains its source edge, uncropped body-center layers for continuation and exposure queries, completed member contours, contact/transition regions, and the owned attachment edge for each materialized layer. Each resolved layer/contact-type pair owns one printable-center cap, quantized to integer XY and shared by its body and contact producers. Contact lookup and body attachment are cached by layer and contact type. A highest crease-point taper and an ordinary periodic band can therefore resolve different contacts on the same layer without overwriting each other. Each section closes its own body/contact boundary before publication; member assembly unions those completed local contours while retaining contact-only regions for clearance exemptions. Every taper layer forms its local trapezoid, from a new contact or the saved successful front, and clips it against the fixed transition plane. Contact regions remain separate semantic data for contact flags, clearance exemptions and diagnostics; they are not independent polygons left for the compound pass to attach. A compound is assembled only after all members have completed their local boundary joins.

For a composite contact, the member-local body supplies the owned attachment used by contact construction and supplies continuation geometry and the projection-exposure reference. A sibling branch cannot terminate or shorten that member's transition. Completed member contours already include their root extensions and internal bends; compound assembly unites these completed contributions with the required shared-junction sectors, without adding another root extension. The component's greatest member thickness is resolved before member geometry, so body and contact attachments agree without a later widened-body copy; each member retains its own contact width and attachment. After line components and independent point intents are prepared, every prepared support center on the same print layer is united. Actual contact triangles/trapezoids and transition regions supply exemptions in the cached round `support_object_xy_distance + extrusion width / 2` model-clearance mask. When subtracting the contact/transition exemptions from that mask, `ApplySafetyOffset::Yes` slightly enlarges the exempted region so clearance does not cut contact geometry exactly at its shared boundary. That mask clips the layer union once. The resulting layer centers go directly to toolpath generation, where they are expanded once by half an extrusion width to record the common physical envelope used for output-region grouping, inner walls and bed fill. No per-contributor geometry is reconstructed after clearance. Region count, holes, thickness and cross-layer connectivity do not trigger a second whole-fin rejection pass. Source contours remain available only for diagnostic attribution and seam-direction selection; transition-retention statistics compare against the common clipped layer. Ordinary and crease point/line bodies all participate in this common clearance pass; only their actual contact and transition sections supply clearance exemptions.

Fin support is an FFF support type for resisting side-to-side motion of a tall, narrow object. It requires support painting to create every seed. As printed height rises, each active Simulated plan reduces remaining directional demand and may extend its seed along a painted wall. Simple and Simulated also require paint at every model contact. Painted Shape treats its painted region as a point, line or branch instruction; accepted point intents may follow the actual model wall to the bed, while line intents use their finite paths and do not trace beyond their endpoints. Actual contacts use the printed-wall physical boundary and the style's authorization policy. Simple and Simulated require their complete bead span; Painted Shape ordinary contacts use the half-sample physical-wall coverage criterion, with lower failures invoking continuation. Each layer retains all generated polygon regions: body thickness is controlled by `fin_support_thickness`, and the neck width along the model surface by `fin_support_contact_width`. Simple and Simulated retain their body and lower-taper clearance; their actual contact bridges are bed-clipped without a second nominal-model cut. Painted Shape first completes the body, first-layer foot, downward transitions and actual contacts, then clips the complete support with the contact-and-transition-exempted model-clearance mask. Its first-layer foot surrounds the expanded bottom fin path, including its line width and end caps, remains inside the bed, and is filled by the ordinary support infill generator.

Body direction A, finite contact axis N and the actual surface normal remain separate quantities. Simple, Simulated and Painted Shape point intents use a fixed sheet plane through the seed and A. Painted Shape finite paths supply the retained local segment and its A1 directly. Ordinary and crease point/line fins use their resolved contact axis N as the cap normal. Their physical cap passes through A1 perpendicular to N and intersects the two symmetric body side lines; no preliminary flat end truncates those line extensions. In printable-center space, the cap line is shifted from A1 by half an extrusion width along its cap normal. The two continuous symmetric side chains, separated locally by `fin thickness - extrusion width`, are clipped by both that cap line and model clearance. The actual surviving endpoints are stored once as shared integer XY coordinates; a clearance-limited endpoint need not lie on the cap line. The open body perimeter and contact outer arc use those endpoints directly; no closed body or half-plane mask is used to locate the attachment. Both point and line inputs obtain their contact axes from the selected ordinary or crease contact rule at the current layer. Their actual contact points come from printed-wall intersection. For each newly resolved contact, body and contact construction consume that layer's stored hit and axis without repeating the same query. Taper resolves its own lower-layer contacts under the contact rule and retains the band's fixed clipping plane. For ordinary contacts, N is the outward, angle-clipped query axis; for crease contacts, N points from H toward A1. A valid coincident hit retains its resolved direction. Zero-gap and ray-generated point handling retain their existing rules. Highest-band failure rejects the complete generating member; remaining members are checked again for an available root under group-start retention. A periodic band whose top fails is omitted. Neither event changes point/line classification. Member-local construction and contact-band validation precede assembly; completed Painted Shape layers do not undergo a separate whole-fin topology or stack-connectivity rejection.

Painted facets are sampled at `slice_z` and just inside the physical top and bottom of each layer's vertical extent. The physical limits come from `print_z - object_print_z_min` and layer height; they are independent of `slice_z`, which Z anti-aliasing can move away from the layer center. Every style places seed XY on the `slice_z` section. A Simple or Simulated seed is the terminal painted contact at that mathematical height, so its highest layer checks the middle and bottom sections and ignores the physical top section above the seed. Their periodic bands remain painted across middle, bottom and top. Simple and Simulated sum only sampled normals with `z <= 0`; patches containing only upward-facing samples are rejected. Painted Shape points use the painted samples to select their seeds; ordinary lines retain their corrected nodes and source patch macro normals without a painted-contour seed search. Actual contacts are resolved against printed walls during geometry construction. Each new contact resolves its outward XY direction independently of macro smoothing. Painted Shape lower-layer failures, including blocker rejection, use saved-front continuation.

For the Simulated planner, the first object layer supplies base area `A0`, centroid `C0`, and an XY covariance, subtracting holes. Its outer contours form a convex-hull footprint; each hull edge supplies an outward direction and the original centroid margin to that tipping boundary. For every subsequent layer, slice area and first moments update the centroid `C(h)` of only the material printed so far. The geometric risk is `h / sqrt(A0) * (1 + norm(C(h) - C0) / sqrt(A0))`. Risk at or below 0.8 does not start a fin. Above this threshold, each footprint direction receives demand proportional to `(1 - 0.8 / risk) * original_margin / current_margin`; current margin is clamped at half a support line width to keep the heuristic finite when a centroid crosses the footprint. The current section-centroid offset and covariance growth add signed directional demands. Covariance is measured about the section centroid relative to the first-layer covariance, so translating an unchanged section does not masquerade as expansion. Its directional variance uses a support-bead-sized floor, and positive spread growth is compressed logarithmically so a nearly linear first-layer footprint cannot create unbounded demand. Nearby directions merge within 15 degrees to avoid repeated equivalent requests. The footprint, moments, and directions are model geometry; painting does not move the centroid or change these raw demands. The method is a geometric stabilizing heuristic, not a force or adhesion calculation.

Planning sweeps upward through these printed-prefix states. An active plan has a fixed sheet direction A and fixed sheet plane. Its seed follows the intersection of that plane with the next layer's painted wall; a missing or invalid intersection, an excessive between-layer jump, or an inward retreat that would erase the lower sheet projection closes the plan at its previous valid height. Candidate plans whose tops converge in the same direction stop duplicating future demand, while retaining their separate lower histories. A stopped plan remains in every later layer's support calculation. Planning capacity is proportional to `(seed_top_height / current_height)^2 * thickness / 1.2`, reduced by a nominal contact-neck-distance factor. For every layer and direction u, remaining demand is recomputed from that layer's raw demand minus `capacity * max(0, A dot u)^2` for each existing plan. Support therefore reduces risk without moving the printed material's center of mass. The opposing direction receives no capacity from that plan.

Whenever a layer has remaining demand above 15% of its raw demand, the planner may add another seed on that same layer and immediately recomputes the residual before considering a further seed. It chooses the greatest unmet direction D. For each painted run, A is the direction closest to D that stays within 30 degrees of the run's XY normal; runs more than 60 degrees from D are unavailable. The run supplies a direct A-ray intersection and central/quarter alternatives, all sharing that A. These are geometric sites, not angular bins. Sites are ranked first by alignment with D, then by position: a short curved run favors the D-ray from the printed centroid, while a broad flat face favors the center of its whole sliced contour rather than the center of its painted subset. The selected point must still lie on the painted run. Existing fins facing the same run impose a preferred lateral separation limited by half that run's painted span. The complete printed-layer patch must be painted, unblocked, and have a local averaged normal within 30 degrees of A. The seed's source XY is evaluated at this layer's `slice_z`; `print_z` identifies its printable output height. The final averaged contact normal validates A but does not rotate it. At most four plans begin on one layer, eight plans may remain active, and 32 may be retained in total; a blocked direction does not prevent another direction from being considered.

The upward sweep stores seed and contact feasibility, without rebuilding polygons or toolpaths on every layer. Once all plans close, the existing downward tracing, band validation, contact projection, body, foot, and overlap checks generate final geometry. When a plan's final top fails geometry or collides with another sheet, earlier valid tops from its upward history are checked. A printable earlier top found by an exponentially bracketed geometry check limits that plan on the next upward sweep, so lower contact history can remain while upper residual demand is solved again. If no earlier top is printable, the plan's original seed is excluded. All failed plans in a pass are collected before another upward sweep; one failed fin no longer restarts the entire sweep immediately. A taller printable fin can supersede a nearby lower fin of the same direction when it has a separate accepted contact band no later than each lower band and within one band period, and its bed reach is no shorter. An outline failure excludes its original seed before replanning. The emitted set contains only plans with validated printable geometry. Contact bands still use their own local averaged normals and remain all-or-none. Their height is not searched or adjusted in this version. All selected geometry is completed before any extrusion paths are emitted.

`fin_support_max_spacing` is displayed as Fin placement distance. Simple uses it as the maximum contour travel distance when subtracting an unavailable range at the selected seed contour location or a lower wall projection location. Each subtraction stays on one island contour at its own layer. Line width and fin thickness do not determine candidate spacing. Simulated uses it as preferred same-facing spacing and to scale the capacity penalty for long contact necks. The calculation uses the whole PrintObject; Simulated does not track connected-component ownership.

`fin_support_expansion_factor` is the dimensionless multiplier `k`, defaulting to 0.05. The seed normal selected by the candidate's surface-normal policy supplies an outward tilt along A: `beta = atan(-normal.z / norm(normal.xy) * max(0, B dot A))`. Lean perpendicular to the sheet does not attenuate its extra reach. Its expansion weight is `w = (1 - clamp(beta / 30 degrees, 0, 1))^2`: vertical surfaces have full weight, 15 degrees outward along A has 25% weight, and outward tilt of 30 degrees or more along A gives a vertical fin outer edge. This 30-degree threshold is a fixed initial heuristic. Each selected seed retains `tan(theta) = k * R * w` independently of remaining directional demand, with the selected risk factor R for Simulated and R = 1 for Simple and Painted Shape. Ordinary Painted Shape point fins instead use R = 1 and w = 1 regardless of surface tilt, including points converted from green lines. Other fins calculate the weight once around the seed; it does not change between layers. At height `z`, its outer endpoint is `seed + (D_top + (H - z) * k * R * w) * A`. For ray-generated fins, top depth is measured along A from the seed and wall clearance changes only the inner edge. Painted Shape root stems receive the outer extension described above. The first-layer endpoint uses the actual first-layer height. Zero expansion factor gives a vertical outer edge. Model and bed collision checks still determine whether the sheet can be generated. Contact projection angle controls only contact necks, independently of this body expansion.

Contour lookup along body direction A locates each layer's source-model anchor for Simple and Simulated; actual contact is subsequently resolved on printed wall coverage. Painted Shape point and line inputs use the shared ordinary or crease contact rule. Simple and Simulated retain their painted-contact rule. Painted Shape points resolve A1, N and the actual local wall normal without requiring further paint; they have no finite line endpoint inherited from a discarded green line. Their lower inner edges follow the round model-clearance boundary while outer endpoints expand with their retained slope. Each layer resolves the point body's ray against that layer's clearance independently; a missing clearance intersection supplies no finite ray-body fragment on that layer and does not substitute a historical minimum inner edge. Contact availability and failure use the shared contact rules rather than this body-history fallback. Painted Shape line intents instead use the prescribed finite groups and two clearance operations described above. `fin_support_depth` controls the initial outward depth. Periodic contact-band top heights are anchored to the build plate and shared by every fin: `bed_z + fin_support_contact_start_height + n * fin_support_contact_spacing`. `fin_support_contact_height` extends each band downward from that top. The default start height is zero, so the first nominal band is below the printable volume and is naturally omitted. Simple and Simulated require a real painted wall across every layer of a band. A new Painted Shape contact is checked against printed-wall coverage, blockers and its contact rule; any failed attempt below the band top invokes common continuation. Ordinary contacts retain their clipped query axis; crease contacts resolve A1-H. The contact-front direction is supplied separately by the contact rule. The seed has a separate highest contact band ending at its own top layer. Painted Shape rejects the generating member on failure and rechecks the remaining finite graph for root reachability; Simple and Simulated reject the candidate. A periodic band whose top section reaches the seed contact or its downward projection is omitted. The completed body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before the plan is emitted. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

Before a Simple or Simulated fin is emitted, its completed printable polygons are checked for positive-area overlap against existing fins on every shared layer above the bed, regardless of seed height or direction. Simple excludes an overlapping candidate; Simulated repeats its upward planning with that original seed unavailable. Painted Shape instead permits different intent edges and brush islands to meet or cross: their completed center areas are united and jointly gap-clipped before the common physical envelope and closed outlines are generated, removing duplicate internal paths. A merged area may contain several disconnected center contours, and each is emitted instead of invalidating the complete layer. The required first loop uses only those completed center contours. Missing contours or first-loop failure are recorded without reconstructing contours by eroding the physical area or retrying individual fins. Optional inner loops use physical-area erosion; exhaustion stops further inner loops and retains the preceding loops without recording a first-loop failure. Merged first-loop failure stops outlining only that layer region and records the failure count, layer heights, region index, center-region count and line width. A geometry-failure SVG shows the merged physical envelope, saved first-loop centers and thickness probe; its source identity is one representative contributing fin, not the ownership of the whole merged region. This failure does not withdraw the contributing fins or previously emitted layers. Every style unites bed feet before printing. Nearby plan checks remain a cheap preliminary filter, not the final overlap test.

The generated paths and their occupied areas are stored in ordinary `SupportLayer` records, so preview and G-code export use the existing support path pipeline. The type does not generate supports under overhangs and does not use the normal support interface or infill patterns.

Geometry uses completion barriers rather than incremental compound merging. Connected finite members resolve their common maximum thickness before constructing boundaries or contacts. Each member constructs two continuous, open symmetric side chains in source-path order. Adjacent inner side lines meet at their intersection; an outer bend uses one circular arc with the shared arc-discretization utility and a 0.005 mm chord tolerance. Segments do not produce overlapping closed strips. The root outer end connects the two sides with a flat edge. Cross-member junctions add only missing circular sectors where at least two incident contributions meet. A lone surviving endpoint stays flat, and an internal bend already owned by a member is not supplemented with a duplicate sector.

The contact cap is perpendicular to the resolved contact axis for ordinary and crease intents alike. A contact cap retains the half-bead inset. A reached tail junction omits that inset; junction assembly supplies the applicable shared sectors rather than an unconditional round end cap. Each complete open side is traversed from the outer end toward the model, including every sampled circular join. The cap-line cut and the model-clearance cut retain their common outer-connected prefix: the first encountered limit owns that side endpoint, and all subsequent segments and arcs are discarded even if they later re-enter the allowed area. Side clearance uses the same model offset by gap plus half the support extrusion width as final layer clipping, rather than the gap-only offset used to locate A1. Only when the finite side has no cap-line intersection may its terminal line extend forward to the cap before clearance clipping; it cannot extend backward across a prior bend. Both clipped side endpoints are stored once and shared by the body, contact and taper. No gap-contour arc is inserted between them; the contact front connects directly to these endpoints, and the temporary shared bottom edge disappears during boundary splicing. The open perimeter runs from one attachment through the root end to the other. It is never reconstructed by locating guessed endpoints in an already normalized offset polygon. Exact path reversal has no finite inner-side intersection; no fold-repair policy is provided.

The generating member prepends its own gap-clipped root depth before side-chain construction and contact attachment. This extension retains its flat outer end and half-bead inset. Where the root extension and intent continue through an internal vertex, their side chains form one continuous boundary without an internal transverse cap or a duplicate root strip. Junction contributions follow the incident-member sector rule; a single incident endpoint is not given a standalone round cap. Source intent heights and graph connectivity remain unchanged.

Seed and periodic contacts and their transitions are completed against the member's own body before the compound barrier. Body regions closed across the attachment are normalized, bed-clipped query snapshots for exposure and diagnostics. Final member construction starts from the owned perimeter instead of those snapshots. Boundary splicing closes it with the accepted contact/transition outer arcs using the shared attachment endpoints. Region normalization and bed clipping follow this assembly; the separately constructed bed foot retains its existing expansion geometry. Each completed member contour enters assembly exactly once. Compound assembly adds only missing cross-member junction sectors not already owned by an internal bend; it does not give each junction endpoint a separate round cap. Root depth is already part of its generating member. It does not regenerate a second whole body network, widen completed members, or unite their pre-contact body copies with their completed contours. Shared junction contributions are collected at exact printed-coordinate endpoints without creating new intent-graph connections or duplicating already owned boundary geometry. The union resolves physical intersections between these distinct completed contributions. Shared node identities retain connectivity between finite groups, including different roots and interior junctions. Clipping and member rejection follow prepared generation groups; interior junction identities provide connectivity without splitting those failure units. Member body snapshots remain available for contact solving and diagnostics; they are never added alongside the completed member contour. Only that completed contour supplies member geometry to output. Transition and actual-contact regions separately provide the model-clearance exemptions. The layer-wide clearance pass follows assembly, and its result directly enters merged outlining. Half-bead expansion records the physical envelope at that output boundary; there is no intervening ownership remapping or per-fin topology/stack rejection. No toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

Every actual Painted Shape contact layer uses its own resolved contact axis and its member-owned attachment. Point and line bodies supply A1 and the local body direction to the resolver selected for that band; a taper keeps its parent band's contact type. Before constructing the body, each contact layer resolves the printed-wall hit using its contact-type query. Crease contacts update their contact axis to A1-H; ordinary contacts retain their source axis. Body caps use the resolved contact axis, and each band and its taper consume the cached hit and shared attachment for their contact type. The physical hit anchors the contact front. Ordinary fronts use the straight tangent approximation at the configured length and the half-sample acceptance rule; they are not defined by taking a perpendicular to the clipped query axis. Crease fronts use the source intersection's horizontal exterior bisector as their normal. Its outer arc replaces the body's shared cap edge. Each contact validates its applicable span, blocker and geometry rules. No contact midpoint is constrained by the highest contact layer's vertical plane. Simple and Simulated retain their existing per-layer painted contacts.

Painted Shape source-height resolution compares source model heights with `slice_z`; candidate generation carries the resolved height without a contact-span eligibility test. `start_z` remains the maximum carried source-sample height. Finite lines derive activation from their resolved head node; center-anchor selection and upward contact-layer adjustment apply only to ordinary point targets. Source minimum Z describes the sampled range only and does not set a stopping layer. Neither height is reconstructed by projecting solved green geometry back onto the model. Seed and periodic contact bands also select surface samples by `slice_z`; a physical layer-bottom height is converted to the model frame before defining the periodic start height. Fin expansion and fixed-plane clipping use these model sampling heights. Printable support layers retain `print_z`; diagnostic rejection heights identify the corresponding print layer, while X, A1 and geometric drop diagnostics use model sampling heights.

Painted Shape seed and periodic bands are processed from their scheduled top downward. The top must construct an actual contact; it cannot borrow geometry from an earlier band. Every failed contact attempt below the top invokes continuation, including wall, turn, span, blocker and geometry failures. This failure-routing rule does not settle the continuation attachment method described below. Actual and carried sections retain distinct contact flags. Neither continuation nor taper creates roots or restores rejected members.

Finite band clipping retains the existing code's rules. Scanning down from the scheduled top, a missing retained path ends the band above that layer. A missing source contact does the same when the member has no downstream line. With a downstream line, source-contact loss can continue using retained geometry through the scheduled bottom. Downstream membership comes from resolved directed node order, including an interior junction of another continued line; a junction shared only by incoming lines is insufficient. The test uses source-contact presence, not printed-wall-hit presence. A wall-query miss with a source still present is a contact failure and follows the lower-layer continuation rule. Taper starts below the resulting retained band bottom and stops if the member's retained path disappears.

**Continuation attachment:** A continued layer retains the last successful actual contact front and adds the preceding successful rear attachment line to the current layer's shared side-chain cuts. The complete current centerline produces both sides; the current cap, saved rear line and printable-center model clearance retain their common outer-to-inner prefix. The saved rear line only clips a side; it never extends it. The resulting two endpoints belong to both the rebuilt body boundary and the contact lower base. The saved two front points connect directly to those endpoints through the common trapezoid/triangle construction. No backward corner rays or auxiliary rear bridge are constructed, and no separate connected/hole-free query-body test decides continuation eligibility. A successful continued layer carries its resulting rear attachment to the next layer while retaining the same front. A failed continuation leaves the saved state intact and follows the existing lower-band omission or taper-layer skip rule. The same construction serves ordinary and crease contacts, point and finite-path bodies, contact-band continuation and downward taper.

Sibling cavity filling groups attached lines by their immutable source island, attachment-host edge and original junction node. Correcting, splitting or detaching a junction does not erase this provenance. Once all members are assembled, each layer unions only the group's completed member contours, which already include their contacts and transitions, excluding the attachment host and other groups. With at least two contributing source children (finite splits of one child count once), bounded holes of that union are filled once; open notches are unchanged. Member sections remain identifiable after compound assembly so the attachment host cannot accidentally close a hole for the attached lines. Fills retain a touching component as their diagnostic source, enter the common layer union, and receive ordinary model-clearance clipping without introducing a contact exemption or restoring graph connections.

**Required model-surface query contract:** The model surface is the actual boundary of the whole object's Boolean result: the union of its positive model parts with negative volumes subtracted. Queries exclude internal faces hidden by overlapping parts and include surfaces created by subtraction, with normals pointing out of the remaining solid. Surface ownership and adjacency refer to this resulting boundary, not to an individual source part or the painted subset. Layer-dependent model-surface and normal queries use the layer's `slice_z`, in a consistent model coordinate frame. Crease-line root preparation samples the unchanged root XYZ; its stored Z is not replaced by a layer height. `print_z` belongs to printable output placement and must not substitute for a model-surface sampling height. Macro-normal estimation and other explicitly defined aggregate calculations retain their own methods. A point strictly inside a face uses that face's outward unit normal. A point on an edge uses the normalized sum of the incident faces' unit normals. A point at a vertex uses the normalized sum of incident unit face normals weighted by each face's corner angle at that vertex. Surface ownership retains all incident faces, including unpainted faces; it does not select one arbitrary face at an edge or vertex. Surface membership uses Orca's existing `EPSILON` tolerance without snapping the query point to a nearby surface; no matching surface invalidates the contact. A non-finite or near-zero sum, or failed normalization, invalidates the contact. When Boolean construction fails, the query uses the un-Booleaned positive model-part meshes: negative volumes are not subtracted and overlap interiors remain present. A successfully computed empty result stays empty. This contract governs operations that require 3D surface queries; source-axis preparation uses sliced contours, while actual contact and transition fronts use printed-wall hits. Crease-line preparation queries the root normal and horizontal section through the same complete-model owner already used for ordinary node-height resolution; it does not construct another per-contact Boolean mesh.

Painted Shape downward support clips independent per-layer contact trapezoids against one fixed inclined plane C. X is the mathematical midpoint of the lowest contact segment, before the half-bead offset. Let n be the bottom section's resolved unit XY contact-to-body axis, and t = (-n.y, n.x, 0). This is the ordinary clipped axis or the crease A1-H axis, retained with the saved front when continuing a contact. Starting with the horizontal plane through X, rotate it downward toward A1 by `90 degrees - fin_support_contact_projection_angle` about the horizontal hinge through X parallel to t. For setting x, the retained half-space is `cos(x) * dot(n, P.xy - X.xy) + sin(x) * (P.z - X.z) >= 0`. The hinge and C stay fixed throughout this band. Tapering starts below the retained band bottom. The retained bottom follows the finite-band path/source-presence and downstream-member rules. A downstream line can preserve the scheduled bottom through continuation; without one, the first missing source contact clips the band. A missing retained path clips it in either case. The bottom section's saved front midpoint and resolved contact-to-body axis define C at that bottom layer's `slice_z`; the separate crease-front normal does not rotate C. Carried sections preserve both directions from their last actual contact. Each lower layer with an actual contact forms its local trapezoid from its own A1 and Ncap. Each newly resolved taper contact uses that lower layer's stored printed-wall hit and resolved axis; it does not reuse a higher layer's hit unless it is continuing the saved front after a failed attempt. A failed local contact query invokes the shared continuation: the saved rear edge joins the current side cuts, and the saved front connects directly to their resulting endpoints. Taper retains its parent band's contact rule. A crease line's tapers and a crease point's highest-band taper use original-source intersections at each `slice_z`; lower periodic point-band tapers use ordinary contact. No taper selects a nearby or sharper corner to replace a missing source intersection. Neither query searches beyond a finite endpoint. Once taper begins, a successful lower-layer query supplies taper geometry only and cannot reopen the contact band. The resulting geometry is clipped to C at `slice_z`. A layer whose contact, cap or carried trapezoid cannot be constructed contributes no taper geometry; solving continues on the next lower layer without discarding the other layers or the fin. No convergence layer is selected and no endpoint is interpolated between layers. Abrupt changes of the local contact axis receive no special smoothing or cross-layer distortion checks.

The lower layers are evaluated down to the bed. Entirely cut-away sections emit nothing. Every nonempty retained section proceeds through the common body-attachment step; successful sections publish both their completed body/contact contour and their separate transition region. A section is not omitted merely because it lies inside a query body. These sections remain identifiable until compound completion and participate in the existing contact-and-transition model-clearance exemption and final layer assembly. A vertical C (setting 0 degrees) retains its fixed XY half-plane at every height; a horizontal C (setting 90 degrees) removes the sections below X. Projection clipping does not reposition the seed, A, Ncap or the fin's outer endpoints. The seed projection remains a temporary mask for suppression of redundant periodic contacts and does not create another contact band.
For Painted Shape, each contact is created directly in printable-center space. Its front edge comes from the contact rule, with a half-support-bead offset along that rule's front normal outside the resolved printed-wall surface. Ordinary contact uses the full configured contact length and rejects insufficient coverage. Only an exactly zero-length front produces one nose vertex; no EPSILON threshold collapses a positive-length front into a triangle. Front endpoints connect to the stored integer side endpoints after both cap-line and model-clearance clipping. The shared cap chain is removed from the body and contact contours, and their remaining arcs form one closed polygon before any compound union. There is no guessed rear overlap and no edge-only Boolean union as an attachment method. Taper or bed clipping may shorten the shared chain: the retained endpoints are inserted into that same owned body edge. Membership on the recorded attachment allows only one scaled integer unit for clipping-intersection quantization, not an EPSILON search or snapping to unrelated nearby edges. Carried contacts record their actual rear attachment after adding the saved rear edge to the current body-side cuts. Where a carried section or another transition already has positive-area overlap, region union resolves that real overlap. An unattached edge-only section fails construction and follows the common band-top, lower-layer continuation or taper-skip rule. A failed highest band produces a `top-band` snapshot; failed lower-band continuation produces a `contact-continuation` snapshot. A failed taper attachment logs `contact-boundary` and skips that layer without an automatic failure snapshot. The diagnostic does not introduce a separate member-wide rejection. Semantic contact regions remain separately available for masks and diagnostics. Simple and Simulated retain their existing convex center bridge and short internal overlap. `fin_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. For all fin styles, contact length and `fin_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the object's resolved support line width at its configured layer height. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. Simple and Simulated sample contact length plus one support extrusion width and require direct paint across their source-model samples. Their physical-span samples project from laterally shifted A1 positions and reject missing coverage or displacement greater than one support line width. Painted Shape ordinary contacts retain the configured contact length; failure of the half-sample coverage rule rejects the attempt rather than shortening its front. The island-area formula does not establish an additional physical-contact requirement over `c + w`. Crease contacts retain their own front-direction construction. Any failed Painted Shape attempt below the band top follows the common continuation rule. The rounded physical envelope can be wider than its contact span.

The first outer toolpath is the contour of the complete printable-center region. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its body-facing edge uses the shared side endpoints after contact-axis and printable-center gap clipping, prepared before the layer-wide model-gap pass. Its front segment has the configured `contact_width`, including positive lengths below one extrusion width; lack of coverage is not resolved by reducing that length. On a flat wall, expanding this center region by half a bead width makes the model-facing physical boundary tangent along the same span. Ordinary polygon erosion is never used to recover Painted Shape's first center contour because it would change a short contact span or erase a zero-length nose. Additional loops offset inward from the completed physical area by the configured spacing. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance converted to scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, Simple and Simulated finalize each fin's polygons and center areas and check topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry supplies no directional capacity to the Simulated planner. Once geometry for all selected fins is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. For Simulated, an outline failure discards the batch's temporary paths and restarts upward planning with that plan's original seed excluded; its capacity is therefore never counted without a printable fin. Only a successful Simulated batch is stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and used to generate bed infill. Simple excludes the failing candidate before emission. Painted Shape uses the merged outlining behavior described above. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Above the bed, generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit or its center contour is absent or invalid; already generated loops are retained. Painted Shape emits all valid contours of a merged region, including split inner contours; Simple and Simulated require their single-contour loop. Thicker fins can therefore have hollow interiors. Simple and Simulated reject a fin if its first contour cannot be generated; Painted Shape records merged first-loop failure as described above. Each fin contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.

The bed layer unites all accepted fin footprints before generating any bed contours or infill. Its outer center area combines the merged footprint's half-width inset with the saved contact centers, preserving narrow fronts and point contacts. Overlapping feet therefore share a perimeter instead of retaining intersecting internal walls. Disconnected regions and holes retain separate closed contours; further walls use the configured loop count and support flow spacing. Bed contours start at their vertex with the greatest clearance from the first model layer. Infill is generated once over the merged interior after subtracting space for the emitted walls, using the ordinary support infill generator.

## Contact eligibility and diagnostics

Let `w` be support line width, `t` fin thickness, `c` contact length and `g` model XY clearance. Simple and Simulated require their complete painted contact span. Painted Shape ordinary contacts use the half-sample physical-wall coverage criterion and use the common top-failure/lower-continuation rules; table entries below state style-specific exceptions explicitly. Painted Shape point bodies, like line bodies, rely on shared side-chain geometry and model/bed clipping. They do not use the legacy minimum ray-depth checks, highest-wall distance veto, historical projection-intrusion veto or sampled center/side collision veto.

Skipped layers stay inactive in the component record; neither a member rejection nor a point replacement is triggered by a taper-layer failure. Actual contact-band validation and final body checks remain separate.

The final Painted Shape line stores the first graph-attachment and component-assembly failure in `stage_failure_samples`, with the aggregate counts beside it. Detailed records for every failure remain available at debug level. `composite_intent_merge_failures` includes rejected axis connections and component assembly failures. Invalid axis connections do not join components; remaining paths must reach an already valid root before they can proceed; failure does not create a replacement seed or root. Component assembly failures reject that component before it enters the common clearance pass. `painted-shape-layer-clearance` summarizes that pass. A previously nonempty common layer that is completely removed records `empty-center-after-gap`, its slice/print heights and a geometry snapshot; only that layer is skipped. No post-clearance whole-fin rejection or per-fin overlap statistic is computed.

`single_projection_layers` and `composite_projection_layers` count materialized transition sections across attempts; they are not counts of unique print layers. The corresponding `*_max_layers` value is the largest count in one attempt, and `*_zero_layers` counts attempts with no materialized transition. A completed Painted Shape pass evaluates down to the bed, even if earlier sections were removed by C, so `bed` records completion of the pass rather than proof of nonempty bed geometry.

Each blue point is checked separately against its existing previous and next segments. Among incident-to-patch persistent convex crease segments, the probe selects the nearest perpendicular foot inside a finite projected segment; exterior feet are not clamped, exact ties retain source order, and lateral distance is recorded without rejection. The crease chain retains the existing greater-than-45-degree adjacency and component feature-scale filter. Probing and direct output have no additional crease-intent length/area thresholds. From the foot, the blue segment selects the traversal direction along the crease. The probe walks half that point's measured full painted width in chart-space arc length and normalizes the sum of segment directions weighted by traversed length, including a partial last segment. A crease end truncates the window; non-convex and patch boundaries also stop it. Zero-width, empty/cancelling windows and unavailable traversal directions are reported rather than assigned a direction. Only the angle between the blue segment and this average is compared with 15 degrees; consecutive blue segments are not compared with one another. Each measurement is written to SVG comments and debug-level `painted-crease-probe` records. Info-level `painted-crease-probes` records contain counts, projection degeneracies and the SVG path/open result. `both_sides_in_patch` counts probes on creases whose adjacent faces still share one patch; this is not a successful two-sided match. This single-chart selection does not extend crease cuts or decide two-sided symmetry. Its SVG is a source snapshot before consumption and remains available independently of downstream green or fin success.

Single-patch crease selection uses original source vertices to collect every incident blue segment at a junction, including branches stored in separate blue paths. Incident directions are ranked by their angle to the sampled crease direction, not by lateral distance or the angle between two blue segments. The smallest and second-smallest angles are the only eligible junction branches; equal angles retain source-list order and unavailable angles sort last. Both must pass the existing inclusive 15-degree check for the junction point to survive. Other incident branches do not contribute to that junction's selection or replace a failed member of the pair. Ordinary points initially survive when at least one incident direction passes at that point. Rejecting a junction does not recursively remove other samples. Original branch connections are not carried into the selected-point chain. These decisions select auxiliary source points for the two-sided comparison. Ordinary blue paths remain unchanged until the comparison consumes participating samples.

A rejected junction receives one local recheck using the same two originally ranked branches. If their distinct outer endpoints are both ordinary points, a temporary chord bypasses the junction. At each endpoint, the existing probe uses that endpoint's original half full width to recompute the crease's length-weighted average direction toward the other endpoint. Both chord angles must independently pass the inclusive 15-degree check and refer to the junction's same source crease; angles are not averaged. Success retains both outer points, even if their original junction-facing directions failed, while the junction remains excluded. Failure leaves the original selection unchanged and does not try a third branch. Originally accepted junctions remain unchanged. Adjacent junctions are not recursively bypassed. Rechecks use frozen source geometry and ranking, without changing widths, surface positions, or the ordinary blue graph.

The fourth probe SVG panel shows accepted recheck chords in magenta and rejected chords in red, with `RECHECK` identifiers, both endpoint angles, cyan sampling windows and orange average directions. Selected-point records distinguish `junction-bypassed` and `junction-recheck-selected`.

Selected points retain their original surface coordinates, Z, full-width measurement and source identity. The chart-space perpendicular foot's segment fraction locates the corresponding point on the original 3D crease; cumulative 3D crease arc length orders the selected points separately for each crease and source chart. Equal positions retain source-list order. Adjacent selected points are connected directly, including across discarded junctions, missing samples and original blue-path breaks; these chords are not fitted or checked again against 15 degrees. A single surviving point is reported without inventing a segment. Source surface positions are not snapped to the crease or recovered from the new chords. These points are auxiliary data for local width lookup and section comparison; their first and last positions do not define painted crease coverage. `painted-crease-probe` records include `junction_rank`, `branch_selected`, `point_selected` and `crease_position_mm`; `painted-crease-selection` records preserve the selected/discarded reason and source 3D coordinates, and `painted-crease-chain` records identify the ordered point sequence. These details appear both in SVG comments and debug logs. The info summary includes selected point/chain counts and selected/rejected junction counts.

Each source chart obtains contiguous painted crease spans from its projected painted area and incident convex source-crease edges, independently of blue-point selection. Boundary crossings use the existing polygon clipper, and interval containment includes the painted boundary with the existing numerical tolerance, so a crease coincident with the polygon boundary is retained regardless of open-path clipping orientation. Disconnected painted spans remain separate. For each span endpoint, the nearest selected blue point assigned to that crease is chosen by 2D Euclidean distance; exact ties retain original list order, not reordered crease order. Its original local full painted width is the inward trim length along the 2D crease path. The two ends have independent widths, without path-wide averaging or a half-width factor. The resulting segment fractions map each trimmed endpoint back onto the original 3D crease. This crease trimming is independent of the ordinary blue skeleton's own cap trimming and does not shorten the auxiliary blue chain.

Source snapshots are collected across patches by persistent crease ID. A pair requires overlapping untrimmed painted crease spans from two distinct patches whose adjacent source faces lie on opposite sides of that crease. Face-slot ordering may change along the crease; chart identity and color remain stable. A patch containing both adjacent faces is marked `SIDES NOT SEPARATED`, and that segment supplies no two-sided pairing. Multiple eligible chart/span pairs are reported separately without selecting a semantic winner. For a line pair, the two trimmed starts are averaged in source 3D crease arc length, as are the two trimmed ends. This is neither a coordinate midpoint off the crease nor an intersection of the trimmed spans. The fused range retains every original crease bend.

The blue-stage `blue_point_fallback` decision is carried explicitly with each chart. If either paired side has this point classification, the result is a point candidate at the path-length midpoint of their common untrimmed painted crease range; selected blue points, cap widths and section comparisons are not required. If either side's two full-width trims meet or cross, the same midpoint point candidate is used, without scaling down the trim distances or restoring the untrimmed line. An empty selected-blue result alone is not a point classification: without an explicit point decision or known trim exhaustion, missing width samples are reported as `no-selected-point`. Crease point interpretations become independent direct point inputs; crease line interpretations must pass the section symmetry checks within intent resolution before their accepted parts become direct inputs. Candidate generation does not repeat these interpretation checks.

Each fused line range is split at original crease vertices, with one midpoint section per nondegenerate interval perpendicular to that source segment's 3D direction. Sampling follows source mesh tessellation, not print layers or a new fixed spacing. Each section intersects both finite selected-point chains in 3D. Distinct intersections are retained; a shared-vertex hit within `EPSILON` is counted once. A segment lying within `EPSILON` of the section plane is reported as coplanar, rather than choosing a point along it. A unique intersection supplies its Euclidean distance from the crease section origin. Only two unique intersections supply the absolute difference between side distances. Missing, multiple and coplanar cases are marked explicitly, without nearest-hit selection, interpolation or fallback; distance curves break at unavailable measurements and uncovered intervals. All discrete hits and coplanar segments are recorded in SVG comments and debug logs, and ambiguous geometry is shown in red. A section is accepted only when both sides have unique intersections and `abs(d0 - d1) <= (d0 + d1) / 2`. The comparison has no absolute-distance floor and does not divide by the mean; two zero distances satisfy the inequality. Missing, multiple or coplanar measurements fail the section. Consecutive accepted sections retain their complete original crease intervals and intervening source bends as one independent line intent. Failed sections and uncovered intervals split intents, without reconnecting across them. A single isolated accepted section supplies a point body input at its midpoint, while retaining that complete original 3D section interval as its separate contact source. The body is a ray; contact lookup still intersects the retained source interval at each queried slice height. Two-sided comparison and intent construction are separate from SVG rendering.

Geometry failures use the common `export_fin_geometry_failure` snapshot writer. It reuses the SVG renderer and receives actual intermediate polygons, paths and points; it does not rerun solving, repair geometry or change rejection decisions. At the failure entry points described below, snapshots are written independently of the normal diagnostic verbosity to `SVG/fin-support/geometry-failure-<timestamp>-<sequence>.svg`; `geometry-failure-dump` records the stage, reason, object, island/edge, crease identity when present, layer, slice Z, print Z, file path and open result. Member snapshots include completed contours and the recorded attachment edges so an unclosed contact can be traced before compound assembly.

Automatic Painted Shape failure entry points cover empty member bodies (once per source), failed lower-band continuation (`contact-continuation`), failed or missing highest contact (`top-band`), incomplete geometry extensions, component merge/assembly failures, empty common clearance output, and failed merged or standalone outline generation. A failed contact attempt below the band top first invokes continuation; it does not independently produce a failure snapshot when continuation succeeds. Failed periodic-band tops are omitted without an automatic failure snapshot. Failed taper attachment logs `contact-boundary` without an automatic failure snapshot; optional crease taper probes remain controlled by diagnostic verbosity. Simple/Simulated retain snapshots for failed contact-geometry construction and their per-fin topology and thickness rejection. Partial snapshots reflect only data available at the failing stage. Contact-construction diagnostics do not enlarge the failure scope defined by the shared lifecycle. The final clearance pass also records `contact-rear-gap-cut` once per source when it removes body material within half a support bead of an actual contact's owned rear edge. This snapshot compares member body/contact geometry, the nearby removed body and the final mask/output; it is diagnostic evidence, not a rejection or an automatic clearance exemption. Expected candidate filtering, skipped periodic contact bands and skipped taper layers do not each produce a file. Legacy finalization snapshots precede whole-fin rejection. Painted Shape output snapshots describe the failing merged layer region and do not imply whole-fin rejection.

Crease contact diagnostics distinguish accepted candidates from emitted toolpaths: `emitted_top_z` is the highest print Z with actual support extrusion entities. `crease-root-band` reports source top Z, candidate slice/print heights, configured contact height, and planned/retained half-open layer ranges. `crease-root-layer` records each planned root layer's path availability, wall hit, accepted contact, source point, physical hit, resolved contact axis, front normal and sideways displacement relative to the source bisector. At the existing SVG diagnostic level, `crease-root-probe` snapshots show each planned root layer after member assembly, and `crease-taper-probe` snapshots show nonempty taper sections before/after plane and bed clipping, their attachment and current query body. Taper probes identify current-layer versus carried trapezoids and record the fixed plane's origin, axis and band-bottom height. They reuse the geometry snapshot writer and its filenames even for valid geometry; these probes do not reject or repair sections.

`contact-turn` values are angles in degrees. `contact-span` values identify section 0 (middle), 1 (bottom), or 2 (top). `projection-intrusion` is the inner-edge increase in mm, compared with `w/2`; depth and contour values are mm, while `wall-facing` is a dot product. For Simple/Simulated legacy finalization, `topology` uses a bit mask: 1 means multiple or missing physical areas, 2 physical holes, 4 multiple or missing center regions, 8 center holes, and 16 an invalid center contour. `no-direction` records requested D in degrees and uses Z=-1 because no layer was selected. `contact-contour` value -1 means no intersection in the sampled span; source-paint checks use the model contour and actual contact checks use printed-wall coverage. Nonnegative values report displacement in mm. `top-wall` distance zero may mean no intersection rather than a valid zero-distance hit. Painted Shape reports assembly failures before common clearance and output failures per merged layer region; it does not perform a separate post-clearance stack-connectivity rejection. Initial exits also log insufficient layers/raft or an empty footprint; the Simulated planner additionally reports negligible base area or peak risk at most 0.8. GUI logging defaults to info; a higher severity suppresses these diagnostics.

Each Simulated directional attempt logs requested D, resolved A when available, and its own rejection summary before the object-wide aggregate. `seed-ray-miss` counts eligible layers with painted cuts but no forward intersection on the fixed A ray; its value is the cut count. `seed-low-risk` counts painted layers excluded by the 0.8 risk threshold. These layer counts explain a small number of actual seed-validation attempts despite a large painted region.

A failed direct-paint lookup receives a second diagnostic-only scan, using the same facing, parallel and endpoint tolerances. `paint-empty` means the requested section has no authorized painted cuts. `paint-facing` means none face the lookup ray. `paint-parallel` means every facing cut is parallel to that ray. `paint-distance` reports the nearest in-segment ray intersection in mm, to compare with support line width. `paint-endpoint` reports the minimum endpoint overrun in mm when intersections lie outside the painted segments. These details apply both to middle-section lookup and full-span validation; the corresponding `contact-span` section index identifies vertical sampling failures. This second scan does not run for successful lookups and does not relax any condition.
