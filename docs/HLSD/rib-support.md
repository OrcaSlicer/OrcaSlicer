# Rib support

Rib support is an FFF support type for resisting side-to-side motion of a tall, narrow object. It requires support painting on side faces. As printed height rises, each active rib plan reduces remaining directional demand and may extend its seed along a painted wall. A new plan begins at the painted site on the layer where it is first needed. Coplanar connected facet cuts are joined before this scan. Unpainted faces do not create contacts. A candidate is rejected if its contact bead extends beyond the allowed wall. Every contact layer checks samples spanning the configured contact width against the actual outer contour and allowed painted surface. Each layer is a polygon: body thickness is controlled by `rib_support_thickness`, and the neck width along the model surface by `rib_support_contact_width`. Model-clearance offsets trim the body; contact polygons are united with it after subtracting the model interior. Lower model contours adjust only the inner edge; they do not translate the sheet. Its first-layer foot surrounds the expanded bottom rib path, including its line width and end caps. The foot is clipped to bed limits and model clearance, united with the rib polygon, and its interior is filled by the ordinary support infill generator. Support islands are derived from the generated paths and their actual extrusion widths.

Body direction A and contact direction B are independent within a 30-degree limit between A and the normalized XY projection B of the contact surface normal. The highest contact and every subsequent contact layer must satisfy `A dot B >= cos(30 degrees)`. A neck may accommodate an oblique attachment, but a sheet may not run nearly tangent to its allowed face. The sheet direction is fixed before local contact normals are evaluated. A contact can turn toward its own surface normal without changing the sheet direction. With no eligible surface, the direction remains unmet rather than generating a tangential fallback. The fixed sheet plane passes through the seed and A; the seed is its pivot, not the outer tip. At each height the A-ray intersects the actual sliced wall. With local wall normal B and sheet transverse direction T, the inner edge retreats along A by `(gap + thickness / 2 * abs(T dot B)) / (A dot B)`. This keeps the entire sheet outside model clearance without losing the transverse component of a wall displacement. The neck connects a front segment oriented along the surface to the body's differently oriented inner corners. A convex bridge prevents crossed edges when these orientations differ. Body collision, band validity and connected polygon topology determine geometric feasibility.

Painted facets are sampled at the actual model slicing height and just inside the physical top and bottom of each layer's vertical extent. The physical limits come from `print_z - object_print_z_min` and layer height; they are independent of `slice_z`, which Z anti-aliasing can move away from the layer center. Seed positions come from the top section: the plan begins at the top of its first contact layer, follows consecutive valid painted layers, and geometry extends downward from its last valid top. Its complete contact span must remain painted at all three heights. Normals may differ across this patch; equally spaced width samples at each height contribute to a normalized average 3D normal. Horizontal normals (`normal.z() == 0`) are the fixed boundary: upward-facing normals (`normal.z() > 0`) never contribute to the average, while horizontal and downward-facing normals do. A neighboring upward-facing surface therefore cannot alter a downward-facing contact at a crease. Patches with only upward-facing samples are rejected instead of falling back to an upward-facing reference. A top-section point on an upward-facing facet remains eligible when its complete patch supplies a non-upward average at other heights. Painting is still checked across the complete patch. This rule applies both to seed expansion and independently to each contact band's projection. Paint lookup intersects along the contact plane, rather than using a facet's perpendicular nearest point. This permits curved patches whose surface normals vary across contact width or layer height. A facet intersecting only the slicing midpoint cannot create a seed whose printed layer extends above that face. Each section resolves its outward normal independently, so a flip at one height cannot propagate to another. Invalid painting boundaries are skipped without requiring identical face normals or lowering every seed by one layer.

The first object layer supplies base area `A0`, centroid `C0`, and an XY covariance, subtracting holes. Its outer contours form a convex-hull footprint; each hull edge supplies an outward direction and the original centroid margin to that tipping boundary. For every subsequent layer, slice area and first moments update the centroid `C(h)` of only the material printed so far. The geometric risk is `h / sqrt(A0) * (1 + norm(C(h) - C0) / sqrt(A0))`. Risk at or below 0.8 does not start a rib. Above this threshold, each footprint direction receives demand proportional to `(1 - 0.8 / risk) * original_margin / current_margin`; current margin is clamped at half a support line width to keep the heuristic finite when a centroid crosses the footprint. The current section-centroid offset and covariance growth add signed directional demands. Covariance is measured about the section centroid relative to the first-layer covariance, so translating an unchanged section does not masquerade as expansion. Its directional variance uses a support-bead-sized floor, and positive spread growth is compressed logarithmically so a nearly linear first-layer footprint cannot create unbounded demand. Nearby directions merge within 15 degrees to avoid repeated equivalent requests. The footprint, moments, and directions are model geometry; painting does not move the centroid or change these raw demands. The method is a geometric stabilizing heuristic, not a force or adhesion calculation.

Planning sweeps upward through these printed-prefix states. An active plan has a fixed sheet direction A and fixed sheet plane. Its seed follows the intersection of that plane with the next layer's painted wall; a missing or invalid intersection, an excessive between-layer jump, or an inward retreat that would erase the lower sheet projection closes the plan at its previous valid height. Candidate plans whose tops converge in the same direction stop duplicating future demand, while retaining their separate lower histories. A stopped plan remains in every later layer's support calculation. Planning capacity is proportional to `(seed_top_height / current_height)^2 * thickness / 1.2`, reduced by a nominal contact-neck-distance factor. For every layer and direction u, remaining demand is recomputed from that layer's raw demand minus `capacity * max(0, A dot u)^2` for each existing plan. Support therefore reduces risk without moving the printed material's center of mass. The opposing direction receives no capacity from that plan.

Whenever a layer has remaining demand above 15% of its raw demand, the planner may add another seed on that same layer and immediately recomputes the residual before considering a further seed. It chooses the greatest unmet direction D. For each painted run, A is the direction closest to D that stays within 30 degrees of the run's XY normal; runs more than 60 degrees from D are unavailable. The run supplies a direct A-ray intersection and central/quarter alternatives, all sharing that A. These are geometric sites, not angular bins. Sites are ranked first by alignment with D, then by position: a short curved run favors the D-ray from the printed centroid, while a broad flat face favors the center of its whole sliced contour rather than the center of its painted subset. The selected point must still lie on the painted run. Existing ribs facing the same run impose a preferred lateral separation limited by half that run's painted span. The complete printed-layer patch must be painted, unblocked, and have a local averaged normal within 30 degrees of A. The seed's XYZ comes from that site's XY and this layer's physical top Z. The final averaged contact normal validates A but does not rotate it. At most four plans begin on one layer, eight plans may remain active, and 32 may be retained in total; a blocked direction does not prevent another direction from being considered.

The upward sweep stores seed and contact feasibility, without rebuilding polygons or toolpaths on every layer. Once all plans close, the existing downward tracing, band validation, contact projection, body, foot, and overlap checks generate final geometry. When a plan's final top fails geometry or collides with another sheet, earlier valid tops from its upward history are checked. A printable earlier top found by an exponentially bracketed geometry check limits that plan on the next upward sweep, so lower contact history can remain while upper residual demand is solved again. If no earlier top is printable, the plan's original seed is excluded. All failed plans in a pass are collected before another upward sweep; one failed rib no longer restarts the entire sweep immediately. A taller printable rib can supersede a nearby lower rib of the same direction when it has a separate accepted contact band no later than each lower band and within one band period, and its bed reach is no shorter. An outline failure excludes its original seed before replanning. The emitted set contains only plans with validated printable geometry. Contact bands still use their own local averaged normals and remain all-or-none. Their height is not searched or adjusted in this version. All selected geometry is completed before any extrusion paths are emitted.

The saved `rib_support_max_spacing` key and its numeric values remain compatible. Its displayed meaning is Rib placement distance scale: it sets the preferred spacing of same-facing ribs up to half the available run length, and scales the capacity penalty for long contact necks. It does not require uniform spacing. These thresholds are initial geometric heuristics, not predictions of bed adhesion or contact strength. The calculation uses the whole PrintObject; connected-component ownership is not tracked.

`rib_support_expansion_factor` is the dimensionless multiplier `k`, defaulting to 0.05. The averaged non-upward normal around the seed supplies an outward tilt along A: `beta = atan(-normal.z / norm(normal.xy) * max(0, B dot A))`. Lean perpendicular to the sheet does not attenuate its extra reach. Its expansion weight is `w = (1 - clamp(beta / 30 degrees, 0, 1))^2`: vertical surfaces have full weight, 15 degrees outward along A has 25% weight, and outward tilt of 30 degrees or more along A gives a vertical rib outer edge. This 30-degree threshold is a fixed initial heuristic. Each selected seed retains `tan(theta) = k * R * w` independently of remaining directional demand. The weight is calculated once around the seed and does not change between layers. At height `z`, its outer endpoint is `seed + (D_top + (H - z) * k * R * w) * A`. Top depth is measured along A from the seed; wall clearance changes only the inner edge. The first-layer endpoint uses the actual first-layer height. Zero expansion factor gives a vertical outer edge. Model and bed collision checks still determine whether the sheet can be generated. Contact projection angle controls only contact necks, independently of this body expansion.

Contour lookup along body direction A locates each layer's wall in the fixed sheet plane and supplies its local XY normal for clearance. Lower inner edges follow these actual intersections while retaining the upper sheet's vertical projection. Lower endpoints extend outward by the expansion slope. A candidate is rejected when the lower model blocks that load-bearing projection. A missing wall intersection is recorded explicitly: the sheet may continue vertically toward the bed, but a previous layer's wall position cannot create contacts there. The saved key `rib_support_depth` is retained for compatibility, but its displayed name and meaning are Top layer depth. Foot padding is independent of this value, while its length follows the generated bottom sheet. `rib_support_contact_height` and `rib_support_contact_spacing` define vertical contact bands. A band touches the actual contour on every layer only when all of its layers have a real wall intersection, painted and unblocked side faces, and locally averaged normals within 30 degrees of A; otherwise the entire band remains separated. Each contact layer looks up its actual local painted surface at the wall intersection, then independently averages its non-upward normals to orient its front segment. It does not reuse seed B to locate every lower contact. The seed's highest contact band must remain valid, or the seed is rejected. Skipped bands reduce the measured contact fraction; when this is below the planning estimate, planning repeats with a lower capacity for that original seed. The completed body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before the plan is emitted; the foot is clipped against the same limits. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

Before a planned rib is emitted, its completed printable polygons are checked for positive-area overlap against existing ribs on every shared layer above the bed, regardless of seed height or direction. Overlapping candidates are excluded and the upward planner repeats with that original seed unavailable. The bed is excluded because its feet are deliberately united before printing. Nearby plan checks remain a cheap preliminary filter, not the final overlap test.

The generated paths and their occupied areas are stored in ordinary `SupportLayer` records, so preview and G-code export use the existing support path pipeline. The type does not generate supports under overhangs and does not use the normal support interface or infill patterns.

Geometry is built from the bed upward. When the traversal reaches the lowest layer of a contact band, a short look-ahead verifies that every layer in that band is painted and unblocked. An accepted band's lowest contact section immediately starts a nested traversal at the preceding layer. This traversal backfills the already stored lower geometry and returns to the main traversal when the projection is absorbed or reaches the bed. There is no separate full-layer contact-planning or projection pass, and no toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

Each contact band projects only its lowest contact layer's front segment downward in that band's B/-Z plane; zero length projects a point. Its lowest layer independently averages the painted normals over its own width and vertical extent during band validation; it does not reuse the seed's normal or another band's normal. `rib_support_contact_projection_angle`, from 0 to 90 degrees, rotates this local average toward negative Z in the contact plane. The signed outward component divided by the downward component gives the segment's horizontal advance per unit descent, without an extrusion-overlap cap or a clamp to vertical. Its endpoints connect directly to the current lower layer's inner body corners along A, with a short overlap into the body. A front wider than the transverse projection of the body center contour narrows toward that span as it approaches the body. Narrower fronts retain their contact length throughout projection. Each lower layer's model and bed clipping boundaries are cached across bands. The projection stops when its segment or point is contained in the body center area, passes the body's extent along B, disappears after clipping, or reaches the bed.

Oblique B motion can leave the sheet's lateral edges even before reaching its farthest B coordinate. Each lower projection is therefore also clipped to the union of the actual body center area and its original contact neck, with that neck restricted to the inner-body join plus one line width of overlap along A. Beyond this short join only the body's center area is allowed; expanding it by half a bead width therefore cannot create a side spur or overrun the sheet tip. The original neck uses the band's unadvanced front, so projection advance cannot enlarge its lateral allowance. A clipped projection wholly contained in the body center area is absorbed and ends backfilling. This geometry clipping applies only to lower projection layers, preserving actual contact fronts and the requested projection ray angle.

On a vertical wall, zero is horizontal and produces no lower-layer projection; the contact geometry remains on its own layer. At 90 degrees the projection is vertical. Intermediate angles require a downward height approximately equal to the side gap multiplied by the angle's tangent. Inclined walls use their actual normal rather than the vertical-wall formula. Horizontal or upward rays do not intersect lower layers. Directions rotated past vertical retain their inward component and are clipped against the model instead of being changed to vertical. Projection length does not reposition the seed, the rib's top layer, or its expanded outer endpoints. Noncontact projection layers subtract the model with a positive clearance of the smaller of the configured XY gap and one quarter of the support line width. Once the projection is absorbed by the body, the ordinary XY gap applies. Contact-front overlap with the layer below does not modify the specified angle or reject the rib.

The contact neck joins the rib body through a convex center bridge in each XY slice, reducing to a trapezoidal neck when A and B agree. The saved key `rib_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. Contact length and `rib_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the resolved outer-wall line width in the seed's print region. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Existing numeric values remain absolute millimeters. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. The painting check spans contact length plus one extrusion width to include both bead ends, so the bead does not escape a wall edge. The rounded neck envelope can be wider than its contact span.

The outer toolpath is the contour of the body polygon offset inward by half the extrusion width and united with the contact and projection center areas. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its base joins the body contour with width `thickness - line_width`. The front center segment has length exactly `contact_width`, including positive values below one extrusion width. On a flat wall, the model-facing boundary of this segment's bead is tangent along the same span. Only zero contact width resolves to a single nose vertex. Ordinary polygon erosion alone would change the contact span or retract this vertex and prevent contact. Center contours are clipped against the model expanded by half the line width plus clearance and the bed contracted by half the line width. Geometry for inner loops and bed infill includes the center areas expanded outward by half the extrusion width with round joins. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance in scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, each rib's polygons and center areas are finalized and checked for topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry supplies no directional capacity. Once geometry for all selected ribs is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. An outline failure discards the batch's temporary paths and restarts upward planning with that plan's original seed excluded; its capacity is therefore never counted without a printable rib. Only a successful batch is stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and used to generate bed infill. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Above the bed, generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit, or its center contour splits or collapses; already generated loops are retained. Thicker ribs can therefore have hollow interiors. A rib is rejected if its first contour cannot be generated. Each rib contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.

The bed layer unites all accepted rib footprints before generating any bed contours or infill. Its outer center area combines the merged footprint's half-width inset with the saved contact centers, preserving narrow fronts and point contacts. Overlapping feet therefore share a perimeter instead of retaining intersecting internal walls. Disconnected regions and holes retain separate closed contours; further walls use the configured loop count and support flow spacing. Bed contours start at their vertex with the greatest clearance from the first model layer. Infill is generated once over the merged interior after subtracting space for the emitted walls, using the ordinary support infill generator.


## Contact eligibility and diagnostics

Let `w` be support line width, `t` rib thickness, `c` contact length and `g` model XY clearance. A large painted plane is not sufficient by itself: the fixed sheet plane must intersect the actual wall at each contact height, and the complete bead span must pass painting validation.

| Stage | Condition | Consequence on failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Painted mesh | Only enforced facets of model-part volumes participate; the 3D normal must have XY magnitude at least 0.5 | Facet supplies no painted cut |
| Painted cut | Facet must intersect the section height in two distinct points; its outward XY normal must leave the model at a 0.6 mm probe | Cut discarded |
| Direction | Forward intersection with painted XY geometry; A within 30 degrees of D | Demand blocked, other demands continue |
| Seed | Layer risk above 0.8; actual outer wall within `w`; unblocked point; an owning print region at `wall - 0.25*w*normal` | Candidate skipped |
| Seed reuse | Nearby aligned accepted rib or an exact cached failed seed | Candidate skipped |
| Contact wall | Actual outer-wall intersection must exist in the fixed sheet plane | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact paint | Middle section must contain a forward-facing painted segment within `w` along A | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact blocker | Contact point must not lie in a support-blocker slice | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact span | Sample the complete `c + w` bead span; each sample must project to an outer contour within `w` | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact vertical extent | Every span sample must find a painted segment at layer middle, bottom and top; segment intersection fraction tolerance is `1e-7`, ray distance at most `w` | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact average | Only normals with `z <= 0` contribute; their summed normal must be nonzero | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact turn | A and the normalized XY component of the local average must differ by at most 30 degrees (`cos` tolerance `1e-9`) | Entire contact band skipped |
| Contact neck | Subtract model interior expanded by `w/2`, clip to bed contracted by `w/2`; neck must remain nonempty | Whole candidate rejected |
| Highest band | Highest contact layer must belong to an accepted band | Whole candidate rejected |
| Body top | Top depth minus gap at least `2*w`; highest wall within `w` of seed | Whole candidate rejected |
| Body projection | Local wall must face A; lower inner edge may not intrude into prior vertical projection by more than `w/2` | Whole candidate rejected |
| Body clearance | Remaining body depth at least `2*w`; sampled center and both thickness edges outside model and inside bed | Whole candidate rejected |
| Completed geometry | One connected polygon and one valid center contour per layer, without holes, with room for the first loop | Whole candidate rejected |
| Rib overlap | No positive-area overlap above the bed with accepted ribs | Candidate rejected; next painted intersection tried |
| Outline | Every non-bed layer must produce its first closed outline | Exact seed excluded and batch selection retried |

The all-or-none contact-band rule stops on its first failed layer. It does not mean all other layers failed. Lower contact projection absorption, lateral clipping and bed termination affect only the lower neck geometry, not whether the original band was accepted. Contact-height adjustment is not implemented.

Info-level logs use the `Rib support` prefix. Object-wide `all-attempts` summaries report rejection counts, the first failing print Z and one reason-specific value. Counts include repeated geometry attempts; `seed-cached` means a previously failed seed was skipped rather than regenerated. Each final selected rib separately logs its seed XYZ, A in degrees, accepted/planned band count and its own band failures. This distinguishes why a rib never appeared from why an accepted rib has sparse contacts. Logs are emitted as summaries rather than per-layer messages; logging does not bypass any eligibility checks.

`contact-turn` values are angles in degrees. `contact-span` values identify section 0 (middle), 1 (bottom), or 2 (top). `projection-intrusion` is the inner-edge increase in mm, compared with `w/2`; depth and contour values are mm, while `wall-facing` is a dot product. `no-direction` records requested D in degrees and uses Z=-1 because no layer was selected. `contact-contour` value -1 means no outer-contour intersection; nonnegative values report displacement in mm. `top-wall` distance zero may mean no intersection rather than a valid zero-distance hit. Initial exits also log insufficient layers/raft, empty footprint, negligible base area or peak risk at most 0.8. GUI logging defaults to info; a higher severity suppresses these diagnostics.


Each directional attempt logs requested D, resolved A when available, and its own rejection summary before the object-wide aggregate. `seed-ray-miss` counts eligible layers with painted cuts but no forward intersection on the fixed A ray; its value is the cut count. `seed-low-risk` counts painted layers excluded by the 0.8 risk threshold. These layer counts explain a small number of actual seed-validation attempts despite a large painted region.

A failed paint lookup receives a second diagnostic-only scan, using the same facing, parallel and endpoint tolerances. `paint-empty` means the requested section has no painted cuts. `paint-facing` means none face the lookup ray. `paint-parallel` means every facing cut is parallel to that ray. `paint-distance` reports the nearest in-segment ray intersection in mm, to compare with support line width. `paint-endpoint` reports the minimum endpoint overrun in mm when intersections lie outside the painted segments. These details apply both to middle-section lookup and full-span validation; the corresponding `contact-span` section index identifies vertical sampling failures. This second scan does not run for successful lookups and does not relax any condition.
