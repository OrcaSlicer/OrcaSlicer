# Rib support

Rib support is an FFF support type for resisting side-to-side motion of a tall, narrow object. It requires support painting on side faces. The painted mesh facets supply candidate seed positions and outward normals; unpainted faces do not create ribs. Candidate positions remain fixed; a candidate is rejected if its contact bead extends beyond the allowed wall. Every contact layer checks samples spanning the configured contact width against the actual outer contour and allowed painted surface. The top-layer depth places the top outer endpoint along the seed normal. Lower endpoints expand outward in the same fixed rib plane according to the geometric risk and expansion factor. Each layer is a polygon: body thickness is controlled by `rib_support_thickness`, and the neck width along the model surface by `rib_support_contact_width`. Model-clearance offsets trim the body; contact polygons are united with it after subtracting the model interior. Lower model contours adjust only the inner edge; they do not translate the sheet. Its first-layer foot surrounds the expanded bottom rib path, including its line width and end caps. The foot is clipped to bed limits and model clearance, united with the rib polygon, and its interior is filled by the ordinary support infill generator. Support islands are derived from the generated paths and their actual extrusion widths.

Painted facets are sampled at the middle and just inside the top and bottom of each layer's vertical extent. Seed positions come from the top section: the seed is the top of its highest contact layer, and geometry extends downward from there. Its complete contact span must remain painted at all three heights. Normals may differ across this patch; equally spaced width samples at each height contribute to a normalized average 3D normal. Horizontal normals (`normal.z() == 0`) are the fixed boundary: upward-facing normals (`normal.z() > 0`) never contribute to the average, while horizontal and downward-facing normals do. A neighboring upward-facing surface therefore cannot alter a downward-facing contact at a crease. Patches with only upward-facing samples are rejected instead of falling back to an upward-facing reference. Painting is still checked across the complete patch. This rule applies both to seed expansion and independently to each contact band's projection. Paint lookup intersects along the rib plane, rather than using a facet's perpendicular nearest point. This permits curved patches whose surface normals vary across contact width or layer height. A facet intersecting only the slicing midpoint cannot create a seed whose printed layer extends above that face. Each section resolves its outward normal independently, so a flip at one height cannot propagate to another. Invalid painting boundaries are skipped without requiring identical face normals or lowering every seed by one layer.

The first object layer supplies base area `A0` and its area centroid `C0`, subtracting holes. At each height `H`, slice first moments and areas are accumulated with layer-thickness weights to obtain the printed volume's XY centroid `C`. Candidate risk is `R = H / sqrt(A0) * (1 + norm(C - C0) / sqrt(A0))`. It uses the magnitude of centroid offset, independent of nozzle direction and seed normal. Candidates are visited in descending residual risk. A printable rib covers nearby candidates with similar normals, and selection continues until no uncovered candidate exceeds the geometric risk threshold. `rib_support_max_spacing` controls this local coverage. This score is a geometric estimate, not a prediction of bed adhesion or contact strength. The calculation uses the whole PrintObject; connected-component ownership is not tracked.

`rib_support_expansion_factor` is the dimensionless multiplier `k`, defaulting to 0.05. The averaged non-upward normal around the seed supplies an outward tilt `beta` from vertical and the rib's fixed XY direction. Its expansion weight is `w = (1 - clamp(beta / 30 degrees, 0, 1))^2`: vertical surfaces have full weight, 15 degrees outward has 25% weight, and outward tilt of 30 degrees or more gives a vertical rib outer edge. This 30-degree threshold is a fixed initial heuristic. Each selected seed retains `tan(theta) = k * R * w` independently of its residual coverage score. The weight is calculated once around the seed and does not change between layers. At height `z`, its outer endpoint is at depth `D(z) = D_top + (H - z) * k * R * w` from the seed along its outward XY normal. The top depth stays unchanged, and the first-layer endpoint uses the actual first-layer height. Zero expansion factor gives a vertical outer edge. Model and bed collision checks still determine whether the sheet can be generated. Contact projection angle controls only contact necks, independently of this body expansion.

The fixed rib plane intersects each sliced outer contour to locate its inner edge, with the configured support/object XY clearance. Lower layers extend inward toward the model and outward by the expansion slope while retaining the upper sheet's vertical projection. A candidate is rejected when the lower model blocks that load-bearing projection. The saved key `rib_support_depth` is retained for compatibility, but its displayed name and meaning are Top layer depth. Foot padding is independent of this value, while its length follows the generated bottom sheet. `rib_support_contact_height` and `rib_support_contact_spacing` define vertical contact bands. A band touches the actual contour on every layer only when all of its layers have painted, unblocked side faces; otherwise that band remains separated. The body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before a seed is accepted; the foot is clipped against the same limits. Ribs with colliding body paths are discarded. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

The generated paths and their occupied areas are stored in ordinary `SupportLayer` records, so preview and G-code export use the existing support path pipeline. The type does not generate supports under overhangs and does not use the normal support interface or infill patterns.

Geometry is built from the bed upward. When the traversal reaches the lowest layer of a contact band, a short look-ahead verifies that every layer in that band is painted and unblocked. An accepted band's lowest contact section immediately starts a nested traversal at the preceding layer. This traversal backfills the already stored lower geometry and returns to the main traversal when the projection is absorbed or reaches the bed. There is no separate full-layer contact-planning or projection pass, and no toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

Each contact band projects only its lowest contact layer's front segment downward in the fixed rib plane; zero length projects a point. Its lowest layer independently averages the painted normals over its own width and vertical extent during band validation; it does not reuse the seed's normal or another band's normal. `rib_support_contact_projection_angle`, from 0 to 90 degrees, rotates this local average toward negative Z in the rib plane. The signed outward component divided by the downward component gives the segment's horizontal advance per unit descent, without an extrusion-overlap cap or a clamp to vertical. Its endpoints connect directly to the current lower layer's inner body corners, with a short overlap into the body. A front wider than the body center contour narrows toward that contour as it approaches the body. Narrower fronts retain their contact length throughout projection. Each lower layer's model and bed clipping boundaries are cached across bands. The projection stops when its segment or point is contained in the body center area, reaches the inner body edge, disappears after clipping, or reaches the bed.

On a vertical wall, zero is horizontal and produces no lower-layer projection; the contact geometry remains on its own layer. At 90 degrees the projection is vertical. Intermediate angles require a downward height approximately equal to the side gap multiplied by the angle's tangent. Inclined walls use their actual normal rather than the vertical-wall formula. Horizontal or upward rays do not intersect lower layers. Directions rotated past vertical retain their inward component and are clipped against the model instead of being changed to vertical. Projection length does not reposition the seed, the rib's top layer, or its expanded outer endpoints. Noncontact projection layers subtract the model with a positive clearance of the smaller of the configured XY gap and one quarter of the support line width. Once the projection is absorbed by the body, the ordinary XY gap applies. Contact-front overlap with the layer below does not modify the specified angle or reject the rib.

The contact neck joins the rib body through a trapezoidal center contour in each XY slice. The saved key `rib_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. Contact length and `rib_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the resolved outer-wall line width in the seed's print region. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Existing numeric values remain absolute millimeters. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. The painting check spans contact length plus one extrusion width to include both bead ends, so the bead does not escape a wall edge. The rounded neck envelope can be wider than its contact span.

The outer toolpath is the contour of the body polygon offset inward by half the extrusion width and united with the contact and projection center areas. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its base joins the body contour with width `thickness - line_width`. The front center segment has length exactly `contact_width`, including positive values below one extrusion width. On a flat wall, the model-facing boundary of this segment's bead is tangent along the same span. Only zero contact width resolves to a single nose vertex. Ordinary polygon erosion alone would change the contact span or retract this vertex and prevent contact. Center contours are clipped against the model expanded by half the line width plus clearance and the bed contracted by half the line width. Geometry for inner loops and bed infill includes the center areas expanded outward by half the extrusion width with round joins. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance in scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, each rib's polygons and center areas are finalized and checked for topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry rejects the candidate before it suppresses neighboring seeds. Once geometry for all selected ribs is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. Only then are the paths stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and the bed infill generated. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Above the bed, generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit, or its center contour splits or collapses; already generated loops are retained. Thicker ribs can therefore have hollow interiors. A rib is rejected if its first contour cannot be generated. Each rib contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.

The bed layer unites all accepted rib footprints before generating any bed contours or infill. Its outer center area combines the merged footprint's half-width inset with the saved contact centers, preserving narrow fronts and point contacts. Overlapping feet therefore share a perimeter instead of retaining intersecting internal walls. Disconnected regions and holes retain separate closed contours; further walls use the configured loop count and support flow spacing. Bed contours start at their vertex with the greatest clearance from the first model layer. Infill is generated once over the merged interior after subtracting space for the emitted walls, using the ordinary support infill generator.
