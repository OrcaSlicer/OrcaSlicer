# Rib support

Rib support is an FFF support type for resisting side-to-side motion of a tall, narrow object. It requires support painting on side faces. The painted mesh facets supply candidate seed positions and outward normals; unpainted faces do not create ribs. Candidate positions remain fixed; a candidate is rejected if its contact bead extends beyond the allowed wall. Every contact layer checks samples spanning the configured contact width against the actual outer contour and allowed painted surface. The top-layer depth places the outer endpoint once, along the seed normal. The resulting rib is projected vertically to the bed in a fixed plane. Each layer is a polygon: body thickness is controlled by `rib_support_thickness`, and the neck width along the model surface by `rib_support_contact_width`. Model-clearance offsets trim the body; contact polygons are united with it after subtracting the model interior. Lower model contours adjust only its inner edge; they do not translate the sheet or its outer endpoint. Its first-layer foot surrounds the full bed-projected rib path, including its line width and end caps. The foot is clipped to bed limits and model clearance, united with the rib polygon, and its interior is filled by the ordinary support infill generator. Support islands are derived from the generated paths and their actual extrusion widths.

The first object layer defines the footprint proxy. An area-weighted average of sliced layers estimates the model's volume center. Candidate risk increases with contact height, outward distance from the footprint center, and volume-center displacement toward the facet normal; it decreases with the footprint's narrow dimension. Candidates are visited in descending residual risk. A printable rib covers nearby candidates with similar normals, and selection continues until no uncovered candidate exceeds the geometric risk threshold. `rib_support_max_spacing` controls this local coverage. This score is a geometric estimate, not a prediction of bed adhesion or contact strength.

The fixed rib plane intersects each sliced outer contour to locate its inner edge, with the configured support/object XY clearance. Lower layers extend inward toward the model while retaining the upper sheet's vertical projection. A candidate is rejected when the lower model blocks that load-bearing projection. The saved key `rib_support_depth` is retained for compatibility, but its displayed name and meaning are Top layer depth; foot dimensions are independent of this value. `rib_support_contact_height` and `rib_support_contact_spacing` define vertical contact bands. A band touches the actual contour on every layer only when all of its layers have painted, unblocked side faces; otherwise that band remains separated. The body is checked against the sliced model and build plate before a seed is accepted; the foot is clipped against the same limits. Ribs with colliding body paths are discarded. Raft layers are incompatible with this type.

The generated paths and their occupied areas are stored in ordinary `SupportLayer` records, so preview and G-code export use the existing support path pipeline. The type does not generate supports under overhangs and does not use the normal support interface or infill patterns.

Geometry is built from the bed upward. When the traversal reaches the lowest layer of a contact band, a short look-ahead verifies that every layer in that band is painted and unblocked. An accepted band's lowest contact section immediately starts a nested traversal at the preceding layer. This traversal backfills the already stored lower geometry and returns to the main traversal when the projection is absorbed or reaches the bed. There is no separate full-layer contact-planning or projection pass, and no toolpath is generated while geometry can still be backfilled.

Each contact band projects only its lowest contact layer's front segment downward in the fixed rib plane; zero length projects a point. Painted segments retain their full transformed 3D outward normals. `rib_support_contact_projection_angle`, from 0 to 90 degrees, rotates the normal at the band's lowest contact toward negative Z in the rib plane. The signed outward component divided by the downward component gives the segment's horizontal advance per unit descent, without an extrusion-overlap cap or a clamp to vertical. Its endpoints connect directly to the current lower layer's inner body corners, with a short overlap into the body. A front wider than the body center contour narrows toward that contour as it approaches the body. Narrower fronts retain their contact length throughout projection. Each lower layer's model and bed clipping boundaries are cached across bands. The projection stops when its segment or point is contained in the body center area, reaches the inner body edge, disappears after clipping, or reaches the bed.

On a vertical wall, zero is horizontal and produces no lower-layer projection; the contact geometry remains on its own layer. At 90 degrees the projection is vertical. Intermediate angles require a downward height approximately equal to the side gap multiplied by the angle's tangent. Inclined walls use their actual normal rather than the vertical-wall formula. Horizontal or upward rays do not intersect lower layers. Directions rotated past vertical retain their inward component and are clipped against the model instead of being changed to vertical. Projection length does not reposition the seed or the rib's top layer. The rib's outer endpoint stays fixed. Noncontact projection layers subtract the model with a positive clearance of the smaller of the configured XY gap and one quarter of the support line width. Once the projection is absorbed by the body, the ordinary XY gap applies. Contact-front overlap with the layer below does not modify the specified angle or reject the rib.

The contact neck joins the rib body through a trapezoidal center contour in each XY slice. The saved key `rib_support_contact_width` is displayed as contact length and specifies the span contacting the model, not the full extrusion envelope. Contact length and `rib_support_thickness` accept millimeters or a percentage of the resolved outer-wall line width in the seed's print region. Contact length defaults to 100%; thickness defaults to an absolute 1.2 mm. Existing numeric values remain absolute millimeters. Body thickness must fit two extrusion lines; contact length may range from zero to body thickness. The painting check spans contact length plus one extrusion width to include both bead ends, so the bead does not escape a wall edge. The rounded neck envelope can be wider than its contact span.

The outer toolpath is the contour of the body polygon offset inward by half the extrusion width and united with the contact and projection center areas. The contact front is half a bead width from the wall; its base joins the body contour with width `thickness - line_width`. The front center segment has length exactly `contact_width`, including positive values below one extrusion width. On a flat wall, the model-facing boundary of this segment's bead is tangent along the same span. Only zero contact width resolves to a single nose vertex. Ordinary polygon erosion alone would change the contact span or retract this vertex and prevent contact. Center contours are clipped against the model expanded by half the line width plus clearance and the bed contracted by half the line width. Geometry for inner loops and bed infill includes the center areas expanded outward by half the extrusion width with round joins. Actual molten contact area depends on extrusion and cannot be guaranteed to be zero.

Round expansion of contact envelopes for inner loops and bed infill uses a 0.005 mm arc tolerance in scaled coordinates. This explicit tolerance avoids excessive vertices from the round-offset API's default scaled-unit tolerance.

After backfilling, each rib's polygons and center areas are finalized and checked for topology and room for the first loop. Invalid geometry rejects the candidate before it suppresses neighboring seeds. Once geometry for all selected ribs is complete, a separate toolpath stage generates all outlines. Only then are the paths stored in SupportLayer extrusion entities and the bed infill generated. This ordering keeps lower paths consistent with every later contact backfill.

The requested loop count is `max(1, tree_support_wall_count)`: zero and one both produce one loop, and two or three request that many loops. Additional contours are inset by the support flow spacing and do not extend to the single outer contact tip. Generation stops when another complete bead cannot fit, or its center contour splits or collapses; already generated loops are retained. Only the bed layer adds infill, inset beyond the loops actually generated. Thicker ribs can therefore have hollow interiors. A rib is rejected if its first contour cannot be generated. Each contour starts at its farthest outer vertex along the seed normal. It is emitted as an oriented closed path, rather than an ExtrusionLoop, because generic loop emission moves the seam toward the previous nozzle position. This preserves the outer start and keeps it away from the contact face.
